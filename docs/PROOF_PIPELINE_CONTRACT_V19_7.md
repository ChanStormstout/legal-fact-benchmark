# V19.7: common checker and rule contracts

`scripts/proof_pipeline_contract_v19_7.py` connects proposals, deterministic conversion, merging, use predictions, conditional derivation, learning inputs, final-answer assembly, and saving. Historical entries/freezes are unchanged. Two fixed development partitions are generated by `scripts/proof_together_contract_v19_7.py` through the same acceptance entry. Old batches, training, and additional final-answer calls are not resumed.

The independent checker reconstructs complete conversion results and merge offsets, comparing every field and count, including limitation categories, reasons, locations, restored witnesses, and source metadata. It independently enumerates every route within fixed limits and compares trace multisets and coverage records, rejecting omissions, duplicates, or changes. It does not invent absent traces. Routes excluded for incompatible objects have separate coverage records. The checker calls none of the production conversion, merge, premise-decision, or rule-combination core functions. Both sides share format/type vocabulary and source location, which remains a common dependency.

Rule input contracts are versioned data without premise answers, correct objects, or reference labels. They check original rule hashes, preserving premise text, operations, exceptions, and counterarguments, while declaring role types, shared variables, required/optional objects, and fixed proposition components. Type compatibility excludes mechanical conflict but does not confirm identity. Cross-premise shared variables still require explicit sourced object links; unknown objects are not wildcards. These contracts are model-assisted research configuration without formal legal approval.

New proposals select proposition content with fixed `component_id` and use `basis_mode` to distinguish judgment of the requested proposition, attributed opinion, partial fact, and unmapped content. These prevent explicit scope substitution but cannot prove correct semantic use. Statuses admissible for a complete legal proposition differ from retained party assertions; valid counterarguments remain usable. Background, use eligibility, and premise truth remain separate. OPEN_TEXT is not executed.

Old V19.6 P is read under its original contract without guessed component addresses, modes, or links. UNKNOWN during new-version replay can reflect missing new fields, not worsened old-model semantics. New generation and old cache replay are registered separately.

`final_task`/`final_run` and the separately saved full-presentation entry `final_delivery_v19_7.task`/`run` use V19.7 computation, independent checks, and prediction contracts. A–G retain their original roles. E/F/G require matching versions/input hashes and complete use predictions, with no fallback to old weights. C reads original P only; D–G share one check. Full sources and complete original legal packages can be supplied separately; a P partition is not a complete legal package. Format/citation checks do not establish legal correctness, which still requires source review. This round used an explicit mock for final-runner entry acceptance; it did not generate real final answers or measure complete-answer benefit.
