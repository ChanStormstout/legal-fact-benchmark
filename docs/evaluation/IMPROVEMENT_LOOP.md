# Using the measurements to improve RAPIDO

The desired behavior is a useful supported report, not a high checker acceptance
rate. For every new frozen method, retain the old output and compare delivered texts
under the same evaluation version. Keep A0 to its one ordinary call. Do not add
baseline mechanisms or spend experiment budget through an evaluation script.

## Diagnose from final output, then trace the relevant stage

| Observed pattern | Plausible problem | Evidence needed before changing the system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High R, low P or serious reversals | Broad answer with unsupported binding/law/outcome | Wrong assertion, exact sources, and where it first appeared |
| Low R, high conditional P | Safe-looking incompleteness or selective deletion | Missing reference items; candidate-to-delivery transition and reason |
| Candidate R above delivered R | Checking/processing loses supported content | Lost correct IDs, rejected mixed paragraphs, actual repair diff |
| R/P both poor before checking | Source reading, attribution or rule application error | Actual request, original span and proposed relation; not ACCEPT count |
| Correct prior fact becomes UNKNOWN | Description treated as a new legal proof | Trace attribution vs legal derivation, and the actual unknown reason |
| Available property/stage relation never reaches output | Interface/representation or coverage gap | Confirm source was permitted/read and track its consumption |
| Relevant history read but no useful final effect | Analogy qualification or report integration gap | Raw historical sources, actual assessment and final supported claim |
| Correct text but low score | Possible reference/grading defect | Source-based review of the disputed criterion, without method identity |

These patterns identify hypotheses, not automatic root causes. A source error and a
report-processing error can coexist. Inspect existing execution evidence first;
do not replace an intermediate answer with a correct one and call that system success.

## One iteration

1. Register a concrete hypothesis about an observed final failure, the affected
   metric/item, and the expected risk (including correct-content loss).
2. Make the smallest relevant method change in a new version. Preserve sources,
   failed generations, model settings and accounting. Do not add another reviewing
   role merely because the previous reviewer missed the error.
3. Perform necessary offline contract/arithmetic acceptance, separate from semantic
   performance. It proves interfaces/calculations, not a better answer.
4. Freeze the method and the unchanged evaluation/reference. Run only the authorized
   cohort/budget. Include all failures and scope limits. No case-specific retries
   or selective restarts to manufacture a pass.
5. Obtain blind final-report annotations and explicit source adjudication. Display
   per-case R/P/H, serious errors, answer status, delivery, objection coverage and cost.
   Inspect candidate-to-delivery retention and actual corrections as diagnostics.
6. Compare the registered endpoints. Higher R with unchanged/worse P or serious
   errors is a content gain with a quality tradeoff, not a broad reliability gain.
   Lower error counts caused by deleting correct arguments are not a repair success.
   Keep negative results and decide whether to revise, simplify or stop.

## Promotion criteria

On one or two exposed cases, a report can be described as better on specified
dimensions, with item-level evidence. It cannot establish a general advantage. The
Pareto observed-improvement rule requires no lower macro R, no lower all-case effective
support, no higher serious-error case rate, no lower SUPPORTED_ANSWER fraction and
no lower delivery fraction, with at least one dimension strictly better. Equal
coverage with fewer errors is an improvement; it simply does not establish the
primary R-increase research target. Conditional precision and its denominator remain visible.
This is an **observed development improvement**, not legal validation. Unresolved
labels that could reverse that statement block the statement.

For a future confirmatory cohort, use the pre-frozen paired inference and serious
error non-inferiority design in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md). Development's 5-point R
target and the earlier 2-point serious-error planning margin are not already proven
effects or sufficient sample-size calculations. No product acceptance, lawyer
approval, novel contribution, or same-cost superiority follows from a development
score increase.

The report-repair candidate remains a research proposal until actually implemented
and evaluated: retain a complete source-grounded draft, locate the wrong relation or
qualification, repair that content once with original evidence, and check both the
correction and preservation of supported neighboring content. Mechanical checks can
still protect hashes, locators and contracts. Whether this approach is better than
the existing paragraph rejection is an end-to-end empirical question, not a fact
established by this evaluation document.
