# Proof-carrying graph integration V8

This study ranks rule-application candidates in eight exposed judgments. It is not pre-decision prediction or training a judicial decision model. Graph scores are neither approval nor legal truth. Earlier three-relation tasks, fixed-9B experiments, A/P/B, and source S/B/C experiments are historical context; they were not resumed.

## Data and computational boundaries

New model fact proposals are not manually corrected. Candidate graphs are saved before labels. References do not see scores, ranks, or checker results. Supervision labels candidate uses as usable or explicitly unusable; unresolved/unlabeled items are masked. Independent-reference premise acceptance and target-court assessments are shared external research policy, not ranking-model contributions. There is no formal legal approval.

Statement status, object binding, time, source role, rule scope, conflict, and missingness enter both JSON and model input. Discrete attributes use fixed numeric fields; binding values, stages, and full limitation text enter fixed E5 representations. Text is not truncated; long text uses the existing chunked encoder. Flat and R-GCN receive the same request/rule/fact/object/source/candidate triples.

An unapproved predicate mapping cannot authorize derivation solely through similarity; the checker still requires compatible types. Textual burden policies and open judgments enter the model without becoming unimplemented legal operations. OPEN_TEXT supports conditional reconstruction only through external court-assessment policy with matching source, binding, and version. Reports identify this external contribution.

Entry: `scripts/proof_graph_v8.py prepare|ingest|encode|build|freeze|gate|train|deliver`. Encoding uses the existing E5 environment; fitting/checking use the project's existing MLX environment. No new dependencies, SEALED access, or push.

## Delivered results

Eight cases, 16 ordinary High tasks, 12 actual fits, and 64 downstream deliveries were completed. R-GCN actually participates in candidate selection, derivation, and independent checking, but did not show a stable complete-analysis benefit over simple ranking. Shared bottlenecks include overly strict role binding, citation/acceptance policy, and budget-related missing dependencies. Formal legal approval remains pending.

After the initial freeze, missing slot-correspondence encoding was discovered. A separate `freeze/model-input-v2` revision was saved before training; original graphs/freezes remain. Simple uses compatibility flags as a soft penalty, not an upfront hard filter. Shared downstream processing may over-unify roles or reject existing support because of citation presentation. Full semantic-interface correctness is not established.
