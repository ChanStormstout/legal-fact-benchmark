# Legal AI: Core Algorithm Design

2026-09-29 · Research and implementation proposal · No end-to-end experimental results

## Recommended choices

Keep the A/B/C division, with separate responsibilities. A creates reusable fact representations; B discovers recurring relational combinations across cases; C connects sourced facts to reviewed legal conditions and arguments. C can use A directly without waiting for statistical patterns from B. Patterns do not automatically become legal rules.

Use explicit combinations of existing methods, without inventing a name that implies originality.

| Module | Preferred method | Output | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Source-constrained extraction, positive-evidence-constrained local correlation clustering, versioned repair | Instance-preserving assertions, stable categories, merge evidence | Stricter admission reduces false merges at the cost of unresolved items and review |
| B | Bounded WARMR-style query enumeration and relational dictionary set-cover optimization | Compact patterns, exact witnesses, support over independent units | Bound the language before compressing redundancy; no promise to discover every legal regularity |
| C | Reviewed rule compilation, finite ASPIC+ arguments, grounded semantics | Support, opposition, unresolved states, authority-to-source traces | Checkable inference under current evidence and rules; no automatic adjudication |

A retains complete-link admission: every pair of distinct semantic signatures in a cluster needs an equivalence decision. A binary integer program chooses among competing admissible merges instead of relying on arrival order. B reuses variables within a query, so payment and nondelivery must concern the same contract. It charges for definitions, each use and its bindings, and uncovered facts. C begins with unattacked arguments and iteratively accepts arguments whose attackers are defeated by accepted arguments; unresolved cycles remain undecided.

These are implementable starting choices, with alternatives and rejection criteria below. All examples and rules are constructed illustrations, not statements of any jurisdiction's law. Comparator proposals in this dated design are historical proposals, not authorization to add mechanisms to the current plain-LLM baseline.

## 2. Shared representation and running example

### 2.1 Stored data and computational use

Each source span stores `doc_id, field, start, end, text_hash, quote`. Add original webpage paragraph locations only when verified. Local character offsets identify the snapshot, not original judgment paragraph numbers. Every downstream node records input IDs and versions.

```text
Assertion {
  assertion_id, document_id, litigation_family_id,
  claim_id?, stage?, jurisdiction?, legal_context?,
  predicate_id?, args:[(slot, entity_or_event_id)],
  polarity: POS|NEG, modality, time_interval?,
  statement_status: ALLEGED|DENIED|FOUND|ADMITTED|UNRESOLVED,
  speaker, source_span_ids,
  interpretation: ACCEPTED|AMBIGUOUS|INVALID,
  candidate_interpretations[], type_version, extraction_version
}
```

`statement_status` records how the document presents a proposition, not real-world truth. `interpretation` records whether the system understood and bound that assertion. Do not combine them into one confidence score. Negation must be explicit; missing assertions do not generate NEG. Ambiguous dates/entities retain candidates and sources rather than forced nearest-mention links.

Entity identity is case-local. Cases share role types such as buyer, not buyer instances. Roles are contract/event scoped: a buyer in K1 need not be the buyer in K2. Contract and event IDs differ. Content predicates may be shared, but canonical assertion classes retain polarity, modality and status partitions: `FULL_PAYMENT × ALLEGED` differs from `FULL_PAYMENT × FOUND`. A broader content family does not imply equivalence, evidential strength or substitutability.

Accepted A records enter the default query view. Ambiguous interpretations remain stored and reviewable, without voting as accepted facts. B's main view includes explicit court findings and undisputed facts, with a separate allegation view; mixed views require explicit status conditions. Coexisting p and ¬p for the same arguments/time retain both assertions and a CONFLICT link. B excludes them from its default conflict-free view and preserves a conflict view. C can still read both sources and construct opposing arguments. Its factual view is configured separately; allegations are not established premises.

## 3. A: Positive-evidence-constrained local correlation clustering

### 3.1 Task and rationale

Inputs are re-segmented documents, reviewed role/predicate constraints and the published type dictionary. Outputs are assertions, definitions, memberships, cached pair decisions and a review queue. First constrain legally material false merges, then improve reuse within coverage and call budgets. Do not assume a known category count or spherical clusters.

Use EDC's extract–define–retrieve–canonicalize structure and the PDF's conservative clustering. Every cluster requires complete positive evidence. A constrained correlation-clustering integer program retains as many verified equivalence pairs as possible among competing merges. Initially all accepted edges have weight 1; uncalibrated model scores are not probabilities. The optimizer neither creates EQ evidence nor guarantees that the judge is correct.

### 3.2 Extraction and semantic-judgment contract

1. Extract over source-aligned sliding windows: source spans, predicate, argument slots, referents, polarity, modality, time, speaker and status. Overlap supplies context. Split conjunctions into atomic relations while preserving negation, conditions and nested-claim scope.
2. Check quotes, offsets, hashes, arity, role types, local references and time formats mechanically. Do not infer controller from company or refusal from nonprovision. Unsupported slots remain unknown.
3. A second model call returns `SUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED / NOT_STATED` per slot, citing the original span. Any required unsupported slot permits one correction; persistent failures become ambiguous/invalid. The two calls are not independent certification.
4. Deduplicate identical proposition/instance/status/source records across overlapping windows by unioning sources. Different speakers, times or statuses remain separate. Resolve identity only from explicit evidence; retain alternatives across ambiguous contracts.

Compare semantic signatures that remove case names while retaining roles, slots and legally material qualifiers. Instance IDs remain in mappings. TYPE_EQ compares variable-abstracted content and defining qualifiers; assertion classes additionally partition status/polarity/modality. Instance deduplication separately requires coreference and compatible time. Definition checks use the same abstraction granularity, not concrete facts against a general definition. Check arity, role direction, status, polarity, modality and known temporal relations first: incompatibility yields NON_EQ; missing critical information yields UNKNOWN.

The remaining pairs receive bidirectional entailment judgments over original sentences, signatures and a frozen type scope:

```text
Does x imply every required slot and qualifier of y?
Does y imply every required slot and qualifier of x?
For each direction: ENTAILS / CONTRADICTS / NOT_ESTABLISHED,
with source spans and the exact unmatched qualifier.
```

Repeat with reversed order. Record EQ only if both calls entail both ways, slots align and no qualifier is added. One-way entailment is SUBTYPE; opposite polarity with matching object/time is CONTRADICTORY; different conditions on the same topic are SIBLING; missing support/disagreement is UNKNOWN. Reviewed synonym templates may produce EQ with a template version. This is a complete program contract, not a guarantee of semantic correctness; errors need expert-sample measurement. Multiple model agreements are neither calibrated probabilities nor proof of legal truth.

### 3.3 Retrieval, complete admission and competing merges

Block by legal context, with status partitions at the assertion-class level. Hard-exclude only known incompatibility. Unknown context remains marked and eligible for compatible fallback retrieval. Within blocks retrieve top k from BM25 and embeddings, fuse using `sum 1/(60+rank)`, and prioritize exact signatures.

A candidate type has a frozen definition, required qualifiers, exclusions and distinct signatures. Check known conflicts, distant/boundary members, then stable IDs. A failed check can reject admission while preserving earlier positive decisions. Every signature pair needs valid EQ. Cache keys include context, signatures, original-text differences and judge version: identical extracted ASTs do not prove that distinct source passages can skip review.

Verified EQ decisions form E+. Let z[i,j]=1 mean same cluster. Do not fill unchecked edges through transitive closure. Unknown, uncertain, non-equivalent and revoked edges all forbid z=1 while preserving their distinct reasons.

```text
maximize  sum_(i,j in E+) z[i,j]
subject to
  z[i,j] = 0                         if (i,j) not in E+
  z[i,j] + z[j,k] - 1 <= z[i,k]       for every triple and permutation
  z[i,j] in {0,1}
```

Triangle constraints define a partition; positive-edge constraints make each cluster a verified clique. The objective counts reusable verified relations, not expected legal harm. Among primary optima, minimize membership changes relative to the previous version, then break ties by stable IDs. Do not mix objectives with epsilon weights. Without a proven primary optimum, report the incumbent and gap rather than claiming lexicographic optimality.

For edges a–b, a–c and b–d, merging a–b greedily leaves two singletons. The integer program can choose {a,c} and {b,d}, retaining two pairs without inventing edges. This illustrates optimization, not language-understanding correctness.

```python
canonicalize_batch(assertions, previous_version, budget):
    V = extract_validate_and_sign(assertions)
    pairs = retrieve_candidate_pairs(V, previous_version, k)
    for x,y in priority_order(pairs):
        if budget.exhausted: break
        relation[x,y] = cache.get_or_judge(x,y,profile_version)
    Eplus = current_valid_EQ_pairs(relation)
    for component in connected_components(Eplus):
        if size(component) <= 80:
            partition = solve_clique_partition_MILP(component, time_limit=60)
        else:
            partition = complete_link_greedy_then_local_improve(component)
        if no_feasible_solver_result: partition = verified_greedy_fallback(component)
        assert every_internal_pair_in_Eplus(partition)
    publish_only_definitions_equivalent_to_every_member()
    return partition, pending_pairs, decisions, objective_and_solver_status
```

For large components, use deterministic complete-cross-admission greedy clustering, followed by at most five rounds of single-node moves, split/reinsert moves and whole-cluster merges. Accept only complete cross-EQ and strict objective improvement in fixed traversal order. No optimality guarantee applies. Retrieval misses limit the graph; optimization cannot recover missing evidence.

### 3.4 Updates, splits and recovery

Review new candidates and re-solve the entire positive component containing competing clusters, rather than inserting into the first admissible cluster. The secondary objective minimizes old membership changes. If two mutually incompatible clusters each admit a new member, the unused EQ remains verified-but-unused, not NON_EQ.

Separate extraction, identity, pair-judgment and definition/context errors. Correct assertions or revoke decisions before recomputing. Try the old definition first, otherwise the shortest signature's canonical wording with ID tie-breaking. Require bidirectional equivalence with every member. Allow one rewrite based on unmatched qualifiers, then recheck all members. Persistent failure leaves an unpublished grouping for review. A broader/narrower definition creates parent/child relations or delays publication; it cannot silently expand to absorb errors. Preserve versions and split/merge lineage.

A graph change may split or connect components. Recompute the union of affected pre/post components. After one extraction correction and one component solve, persistent semantic conflicts go to review; do not call until EQ appears. Retain singletons and report reuse, unresolved and singleton rates.

### 3.5 Budget, conditional properties and decision criteria

Prototype settings: k=20, at most 100 pair judgments per assertion, component size ≤80 with a 60-second MILP limit, otherwise bounded fallback. Development grids: k∈{10,20,40}, judgments∈{50,100,200}. Choose greatest coverage subject to a prespecified false-merge ceiling and minimum reuse, with cost as tie-breaker. Unit edge weights avoid unsupported calibration; weighted variants require separate calibration and cost accounting.

Worst-case pairs are O(m²), triangle constraints O(m³), and MILP complexity exponential. Report model cost, solver time and human effort. Correct EQ judgments plus complete admission exclude internal non-EQ pairs. A proven optimum on a fixed graph retains at least as many verified pairs as a feasible greedy partition under identical constraints. Neither property implies higher legal accuracy; false EQ edges remain exploitable.

The rationale is to keep complete-link safeguards while resolving competing admissible merges jointly. If graph-objective gains do not improve correct reuse or downstream outcomes at equal budget, revert to lazy complete-link.

### 3.6 Alternatives and decisive comparisons

| Alternative | Existing mechanism and limitation | Proposed difference and possible benefit | Cost or failure criterion | Decisive comparison |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lazy complete-link on identical E+ | Complete evidence, but early local choices can block better partitions | Joint component optimization under the same hard constraints | Solver overhead; edge count may misalign with legal utility | Fixed EQ judgments/budget: correct reuse, singletons, objective gap and time; revert without practical gain |
| Full PDF implementation | Definition, medoid, boundary and multi-member checks already address chaining/drift; sampled representatives can miss internal counterexamples | Lazy checks of every distinct signature without widening definitions | Extra calls; no benefit if representatives suffice | Same retrieval/judge/budget: internal false pairs and coverage |
| EDC with identical status/identity schema | Extraction, definitions, retrieval and model canonicalization already exist | Complete admission and versioned splitting | Schema, rather than search, may explain gains | Give EDC the same schema, add admission/repair separately at fixed cost |
| Cequel / LSCK-HC with identical sources/schema/constraints | Sparse model constraints and global distance optimization; soft constraints tolerate noise but do not require complete signature equivalence | Open category count and hard admission; unknown is not must-link | Noisy judgments overfragment; soft clustering may scale better | Equal-budget B-cubed, false merges, singletons and downstream queries |
| ClassMATe-style definition optimization | Expert feedback already revises fixed-label definitions | Versioned classes, explicit splits and dependency recomputation | Human/version-management cost | Equal expert minutes: incremental errors, changes to old queries and repair cost |

The integer program only resolves competition among legal merges. Source review and admission constrain false merges. Better optimization is not better legal performance without the corresponding experiment.

## 4. B: Bounded relational queries and a decodable pattern dictionary

### 4.1 Task and rationale

Inputs are frozen A, fixed case/claim/stage scope, allowed predicates and joins. Outputs are executable queries, supporting units, exact fact witnesses and a compact dictionary.

WARMR-style levelwise search expresses finite-arity relations, shared contract variables, time comparisons and explicit status. Select patterns by total description length: pay for definitions in return for repeated-use savings. Following KG-MDL's preservation of bindings, specify a decodable encoding over fixed candidates/witnesses and optimize dictionary/coverage jointly. SLIM-style greedy improvement is a same-encoding comparator and timeout fallback. This adaptation is not the original implementation or its experimental result.

Without reliable outcomes, the target is recurring recorded structure. Compression and legal importance differ. Preserve all candidates and expert-marked rare patterns outside the selected dictionary; rare-pattern flags do not secretly change the reported objective.

### 4.2 Finite language

```text
P(U) := exists b,s,K,t ... :
        paid_full(U,b,s,K,FOUND)
    AND not_delivered(U,s,b,K,FOUND)
    AND due(U,K,t,FOUND)
```

U is a claim/stage unit; b/s/K/t denote buyer, seller, contract and time. Repeated variables must bind to the same ID. Default matching is homomorphic: distinct variables may corefer unless explicit `neq(x,y)` forbids it. Use neq when two logically distinct events must differ. Adapt gSpan to the same identity/matching semantics.

Prototype bounds are 2–4 content atoms, at most six non-U variables, no functions, explicit negation and a finite role/time dictionary. Status is a predicate argument or frozen view condition. After removing U, entity/contract/event variables must connect the query; being in one case alone is insufficient. Every variable appears in a content atom; time/inequality filters only use bound variables. Parent families cannot silently replace predicates.

Constants are limited to declared statuses, legal categories and coarse temporal relations, not discovered names, contract IDs or arbitrary date thresholds. Learn any thresholds only on a prespecified development grid and freeze before discovery.

### 4.3 Generation, counting and pruning

Start with allowed single atoms. Add one content atom sharing at least one non-U variable and satisfying slot types, then allowed time/neq filters in fixed order. This version only adds conjunctions; replacing an atom with a parent family is a distinct generalization operation.

Canonicalize by the lexicographically smallest sequence over type-compatible variable renamings and permutations of at most four atoms, preserving slot order. This removes alpha/order duplicates, not all equivalence under background axioms. Remove exact duplicate atoms; reviewed implication redundancy may be removed with its axiom version. Equal discovery support alone does not justify removing semantically different patterns.

Index relation tables by `(predicate, status, arg slots)`. Extend parent bindings through hash joins, filtering types, identity, polarity and time. Store witnesses as `unit_id, variable_binding, sorted assertion_ids`. Duplicate sentences or synonymous sources do not inflate facts/support.

The unit is `case_id × claim_id × stage`, retaining judgment identity and a separate litigation family. Preselect one designated judgment's complete source snapshot per family/claim/stage; add other sources only in explicitly retrospective views, never by unrestricted cross-stage pooling. Count a pattern once per unit regardless of witnesses. Also count distinct families for minimum independent recurrence. Multiple claims can increase unit_support but not family_support; splits and uncertainty intervals account for family dependence.

Fix the eligible unit denominator N and family denominator for the task. Main support is units with a valid witness divided by N. Record `NO_RECORDED_WITNESS`, not real-world falsity. Do not redefine an observable denominator separately for each pattern.

```python
F[1] = permitted_single_atoms()
for size in range(1, max_atoms + 1):
    for q in canonical_order(F[size]):
        W[q] = exact_join(q, frozen_assertion_view)
        sup[q] = count_distinct_unit(W[q])
        fsup[q] = count_distinct_family(W[q])
        if sup[q] < min_support or fsup[q] < min_families: continue
        save_frequent(q, W[q])
        if size < max_atoms:
            for atom in legal_one_atom_extensions(q):
                q2 = canonical_code(q & atom)
                if within_limits(q2): enqueue_if_unseen(F[size+1], q2)
# Stop at fixed bounds, exhausted frontier, or resource cap.
# Timed-out queries are UNKNOWN_COUNT, never support=0.
```

Prune only from established parent support: conjunctive extension cannot increase matches. Do not apply an arbitrary delete-one-atom Apriori test; deletion may break typing/connectivity and leave the language. Enumeration covers only queries constructible from valid prefixes, not all logic queries. Time/candidate caps further remove completeness; report completed levels and pending queues.

### 4.4 Dictionary and set-cover selection

Deduplicate ground atoms per unit. Preserve sources, text, entity-type tables, predicate dictionary and versions losslessly as fixed metadata. The objective compresses fact relations, not judgment prose. Freeze candidates and all witnesses; incomplete candidates are excluded from this selection round.

Use order-independent encoding costs:

- Two-bit token tags distinguish literal, macro and unit-end. A literal encodes its predicate and all typed entity IDs; a macro encodes pattern ID and every variable binding, expanding to atoms.
- Sort n entities per type by stable ID and use `ceil(log2(max(1,n)))` bits for indexes. Predicate width comes from the frozen dictionary. Relation symbols or fixed-width fields retain polarity, status and required modality. Encode each variable even if variables corefer.
- Pattern IDs are integers in canonical candidate order with fixed width `ceil(log2(max(1,M)))`, where M is the completed candidate count. Store that width in the header; unselected patterns need not be transmitted. Each selected entry stores ID, atom/variable counts, variable types, predicate arguments and time/neq constraints.
- Dictionary entries start with a bit: 0 ends the dictionary, 1 begins an entry. Encode variable-length integers as Elias-gamma(n+1). Known arity determines argument count; references distinguish variables/constants. Dictionary/unit terminator costs are fixed.

Tokens decode unambiguously. Only verified witnesses may instantiate macros, so expansion creates no new facts. Decoding unions literals and expansions. Overlap needs covering once, but every macro use pays its full cost. Unit/atom IDs retain provenance.

Let xp select pattern p, zo use witness o and rf keep fact f as a literal. Lp is complete definition cost, co macro-with-bindings cost and cf literal cost. The binary MILP is:

```text
min  Σp Lp·xp + Σo co·zo + Σf cf·rf + fixed header/metadata cost
s.t. rf + Σ{o: f∈o} zo ≥ 1                     every fact must be reconstructible
     zo ≤ xpattern(o)                          an unselected pattern cannot be used
     Σ{o in (p,u)} zo ≤ xp                     at most one use per pattern per unit
     hpg ≥ zo  for each witness in (p,g)
     hpg ≤ Σ{o in (p,g)} zo                    hpg indicates use of p in family g
     Σg hpg ≥ 3·xp                            use in at least 3 independent families
     xp,zo,rf,hpg ∈ {0,1}
```

Discovery support and selected usage differ. Require discovery in five families, then actual compression use in at least three families, preventing one long case from paying for a dictionary entry. One use per pattern/unit deliberately limits compression, not support semantics; it sacrifices within-case repetitions. Retain all bindings and compare an unrestricted multiple-macro variant. Overlapping patterns are allowed; definitions and coverage jointly determine value.

```python
candidates, witnesses = freeze_completed_search()
initial = all_literals()                       # always feasible
initial = better(initial, feasible_greedy_cover(candidates))
solution = solve_binary_program(time_limit=300, incumbent=initial)
if no_feasible_solution: solution = all_literals()
assert all_constraints_hold(solution)
assert decode(encode(solution)) == original_atom_sets
return dictionary, bindings, residuals, objective, bound, solver_status
```

The greedy fallback must obey identical constraints. Start with literals; for each unused pattern select the best-saving witness per unit, enumerate family prefixes ranked by net saving with at least three families, subtract full definition cost, and take the greatest positive gain. Recompute against currently uncovered facts after every choice. A cheaper solution violating independent-use constraints is not a valid comparator.

Run at most 300 seconds with fixed inputs/versions/seed. Save incumbent, lower bound, relative gap and status. A timeout incumbent is `FEASIBLE_NOT_OPTIMAL`; no useful candidate yields an empty dictionary plus literals. Never accept a result worse than the initial feasible solution under the same objective.

Conditional properties: passing constraints and decode checks reconstructs the frozen fact set; a proven optimum is no longer than any feasible greedy solution under identical candidates/encoding/constraints. This says nothing about legal importance or unseen-case compression. Incomplete solving/search cannot justify global optimality claims. Joint optimization handles redundant patterns competing for facts; revert to greedy if tiny bit savings cost too much. Different KG-MDL encodings require a common evaluation encoding, expert review and independent replication, not raw comparison of incompatible bit counts.

### 4.5 Example

Both s1 and s2 are stored, but the default FOUND view uses s2. Joining s2+s3 for payment and nondelivery fails because K1≠K2. Adding s5 permits a K1 witness. s4 is ALLEGED refusal, so neither its predicate nor status can replace s5. Adding due to the query leaves no complete witness because s7 is missing; due is not thereby false.

Count recurrence by units and separately by families, then account for definition cost. Illustrative costs: three literal atoms cost 33 bits, a macro costs 7, saving 26 per use; a 70-bit definition is not worthwhile once but may save 8 bits after three uses. These hypothetical numbers explain the calculation; actual lengths must come from the encoder.

### 4.6 Parameters and resources

Defaults: four content atoms, six variables, `min_support=max(5,ceil(0.02*N))`, `min_families=5`, 50,000 candidates or two CPU hours per batch. Query timeouts retain cursors and do not imply infrequency. Store witnesses in SQLite/relational tables; use EXISTS for support before enumerating coverage witnesses, and report cover timeouts. Resource exhaustion returns the best feasible dictionary over completed candidates with `INCOMPLETE_SEARCH`.

Development grids: atom limits {3,4,5}, support thresholds {1%,2%,5% with ≥5 families}. Select valid nonredundant yield under fixed expert minutes and minimum case coverage, then freeze. Search is worst-case exponential in predicates/variables; witness enumeration can explode. Language limits are a resource tradeoff, not a free advantage.

### 4.7 Alternatives

| Alternative | Mechanism and limitation | Proposed difference | Failure criterion / decisive comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| gSpan with identical typed incidence graph/status/identity/support | DFS canonical codes and frequent connected subgraphs; n-ary predicates need relation nodes | Slot/shared-variable query generation directly restricts object/time grammar | Same expressible patterns, inputs and budget: candidate recall/time; use gSpan if equally effective |
| Same-candidate/encoding/constraint greedy selection | Cheap and interpretable; local coverage can block a better set | Joint pattern/witness optimization | Compare gap, time and useful patterns; bit-only gains are insufficient |
| WARMR plus top-k/deduplication | Already handles relational binding/support | Binding-aware dictionary and coverage selection | Same review/output budget: new useful information, replication and rare-pattern loss |
| KG-MDL / GraphMDL+ | Relational ports and compact dictionaries; cover timeouts matter | Fixed case boundaries, status views, language, unit/family support and explicit bindings | Give identical constraints; compare common-code length, useful patterns, coverage and CPU/expert cost |
| Relational query features plus FP-Growth/closed itemsets/SLIM/Snor | Strong if supplied full relational information; compression/overlap already exist | Shared variables and witnesses across combinations | With complete features and binding rechecks, correctness advantage may vanish; compare incremental search/selection cost |
| LLM language bias plus ILP, e.g. MAXSYNTH/ADVENT | Predicate invention and executable rules usually require targets/labels | Fixed interpretable, unlabeled descriptive language | May miss new concepts/long patterns; later compare independent confirmation with equal labels/background/budget |

FP-Growth can efficiently process relational features; gSpan is a serious relational comparator. The inspected ANCO-HITS application co-clusters/ranks a signed bipartite graph, not itemsets or queries. Use it only after defining meaningful nodes, positive/negative edges and an appropriate task.

The preferred route fits small status-aware claim-bound queries, not a claim that it understands relations better than gSpan. Adopt general graph search or KG-MDL if the data favor them. Combining WARMR and compression names is not novelty.

## 5. C: Reviewed rules and a finite argument graph

### 5.1 Task and rationale

Inputs are A's snapshot, a reviewed issue-specific legal rule package, explicit source views/priorities and optional B witnesses. Outputs are supporting/opposing arguments, acceptance states and missing premises per element/claim, not a single WIN/LOSE.

ASPIC+ distinguishes attacks on facts, conclusions and applicability; grounded semantics yields one cautious accepted set. A conjunctive checklist cannot express all three conflicts. Reuse a mature compatible engine rather than claiming a custom solver is more accurate.

### 5.2 Authority acquisition and rule compilation

1. Retrieve authority candidates from the issue and case citations, recording exact text, paragraph, date, jurisdiction, authority and scope. Start with actually cited sources, then same-scope statutes and exceptions. Actual citations are retrospective inputs and must be labeled accordingly.
2. Models draft rules with condition-level sources using a shared predicate table: body/head, exceptions, strict/defeasible strength, time/jurisdiction, objects, AND/OR and priority support. Split OR into rules and express layered conditions with intermediate predicates.
3. Check spans, typing, variable safety, undefined predicates, explicit negation, priority/dependency cycles and conflicting definitions. Permit one syntax correction; syntax success is not legal review.
4. Experts review scope, missing conditions, exceptions and normative strength. Unreviewed rules remain DRAFT outside automatic inference. Fact-to-element bridge rules also need review; `paid_full` cannot simply be renamed breach. Record human time.
5. Construct rule checks with all premises, each premise removed, exceptions added, contract/role changed and time changed. These test compilation fidelity, not legal correctness.

```text
Rule {
  rule_id, version, body:[typed_signed_literal], head,
  kind: STRICT|DEFEASIBLE,
  scope:{jurisdiction, issue, effective_interval, stage},
  material_exceptions:[literal], exception_mode:ATTACK_ONLY|REQUIRES_NEG|UNSPECIFIED,
  exception_policy_sources[],
  priority_edges:[(stronger_rule,weaker_rule,source)],
  authority_spans[], review_status, reviewer_decisions[]
}
```

Limit the first version to finite, function-free, range-restricted Horn rules; head variables must occur in the body. Positive dependencies form a DAG; cyclic packages require expert revision or a future compatible engine. Argument attacks may cycle. Finite construction sacrifices complex legal logic, quantitative burdens, dynamic duties and value balancing.

### 5.3 Source assertions, object facts and exceptions

Keep `reported_support(p)=(qualified source for p, qualified source for ¬p)` separate from IN/OUT/UNDEC. Values 10/01/11/00 represent support/opposition/conflict/absence, not probabilities. Conflict and absence are not one unknown state.

In this restricted ASPIC+ instance, Kn contains reviewed source records such as “s2 records a court finding of paid_full” and the formal dictionary. It certifies the snapshot record, not its worldly truth. Kp is empty. Defeasible projection `source_record(s2,FOUND,p) => p` introduces object facts, so p/¬p can coexist as sources and conflict through attackable projections. FOUND is not eternally true.

ALLEGED normally derives only `says(party,p)`. A separately labeled party view may project p defeasibly. Run/report court-record and party views separately. Fact bridges, legal and exception rules are defeasible. Strict rules handle technical relations, not substantive conclusions directly from unattackable axioms.

| Reviewed exception policy | Compilation | No E or ¬E recorded |
|---|---|---|
| ATTACK_ONLY | conditions ⇒ q; E ⇒ ¬applicable(r,binding) | Build a defeasible argument if conditions hold, without inventing ¬E; requires sourced default/defense policy |
| REQUIRES_NEG | conditions ∧ ¬E ⇒ q; E may also attack applicability | Missing ¬E blocks construction and records a gap |
| UNSPECIFIED | Exception policy unreviewed | Rule remains DRAFT/BLOCKED_POLICY; independent rules may run |

Not retrieving E is not evidence for ¬E. Conversely a sourced default must not acquire an invented obligation to disprove every exception. Conflicting E sources are handled by attacks. Preserve policy authorities in `exception_policy_sources` and application traces. Initially avoid a separate positive applicable predicate that would confuse named-rule undercut interfaces. Every output identifies package, view and policy.

### 5.4 Construction, priorities and acceptance

Filter by time/jurisdiction/issue, join bodies in dependency topological order, and instantiate all legal bindings. Each argument stores `head, top_rule, source_ids, children, rule_version`; deduplicate identical structure/source sets. Positive dependencies are acyclic; attacks may cycle. Final acceptance is program-computed, not model-decided.

- Rebut attacks a defeasible subargument's conclusion.
- Undercut concludes `¬applicable(rule_id,binding)` and attacks that defeasible application.
- Undermine attacks an ordinary premise; with empty Kp there are no instances here. Conflicting facts rebut projection arguments instead of deleting source records.

Rebut cannot directly attack a strict-top technical subargument, but can attack vulnerable descendants and thus the containing argument. Undercut ignores rule preferences. Rebut becomes defeat only if the attacker is not strictly weaker than the attacked subargument.

Expert-registered, authority-backed `>R` is an acyclic strict partial order; compute its transitive closure and reject cycles. Confidence, outcomes and retrieval rank cannot supply priorities. Fix last-link and strict elitist set comparison: a defeasible top rule gives `Last(A)={top_rule}`; otherwise union the descendants' last defeasible rules.

```text
weaker(A,B) := Last(A) and Last(B) are nonempty, and
              some a in Last(A) satisfies b >R a for every b in Last(B)
```

Empty Last sets are not arbitrarily weaker. No priority typically means neither side is weaker, not random choice. Separate technical strict conclusions from substantive predicates to prevent bypassing source projections.

```python
Args = construct_all_arguments(source_records, reviewed_rule_package)
Defeats = set()
for A in Args:
    for B in Args:
        for sub in B.subarguments:
            if undercuts(A, sub): Defeats.add((A,B))
            elif rebuts(A, sub) and not weaker(A, sub):
                Defeats.add((A,B))
IN = set()
while True:
    defended = {a for a in Args
                if all(any((c,b) in Defeats for c in IN)
                       for b in attackers(a, Defeats))}
    if defended == IN: break
    IN = defended
OUT = {a for a in Args if any((b,a) in Defeats for b in IN)}
UNDEC = Args - IN - OUT
```

Grounded iteration terminates on a finite graph. OUT means defeated by an accepted argument, not that its conclusion is false. For p, separately report IN support for p/¬p, undecided arguments and blockers. If both sides have IN, report INCONSISTENT and inspect theory conditions rather than asserting impossibility without verification.

Connect facts to legal premises through exact shared predicates or reviewed bridges. Retrieve draft bridges from authority conditions/A definitions; check slots, status, jurisdiction, time and sources, then review/version them. Similarity proposes mappings, never makes payment equal breach. B can retrieve a group of facts, but expansion uses original assertion IDs and adds no independent evidence or vote.

### 5.5 Running example

Illustrative rules only:

```text
r1: agreed_delivery(K) AND due(K) AND not_delivered(K)
    AND not_extended(K) => breach_element(K)
r2: extended(K) => not_applicable(r1,K)
```

r1 is defeasible and r2 an application-specific exception. Payment alone establishes no delivery obligation. Original J1 lacks due(K1), blocking r1; the extension dispute is another gap. A payment+nondelivery pattern cannot bypass due or agreed_delivery.

V1 adds explicit sources for agreed_delivery(K1), due(K1) and ¬extended(K1), without opposition. The unattacked r1 argument is IN, supporting breach_element under this package, not the whole claim/case outcome.

V2 has agreement/due but unresolved extension conflict. In the party view, E/¬E projections rebut each other and r2 undercuts r1; relevant disputes remain UNDEC. In a court view accepting neither party, E is unknown and r1 is blocked: similarly unresolved, for a different reason.

V3 has qualified support only for extended: r2 supplies the exception and r1 cannot be accepted. V4 requires a separately reviewed ATTACK_ONLY version that removes the ¬extended premise but retains r2. Missing extension evidence then allows a defeasible r1 argument without creating a nonextension fact; new extension evidence triggers recomputation. Do not change policy after seeing a case's missing information to obtain a desired result.

### 5.6 Cost, limits and alternatives

Prototype caps: 30 rules per issue, five variables per rule and 10,000 arguments per case. Exceeding a cap returns `INCOMPLETE_ARGUMENT_GRAPH`; never certify IN on a truncated attack graph. Existing traces may be displayed as incomplete. DAG rules and finite instances guarantee finiteness, but argument combinations can grow exponentially. Grounded labeling is polynomial in the already-built graph, not necessarily in the original input.

| Alternative | Existing capability | Adaptation / possible value | Decisive comparison or limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct LLM or structured checklist with identical facts/authorities | Open-text analysis or premise checking, usually without fixed recursive defeat | Compiled rules, subargument attacks, fixed-point acceptance may help layered exceptions/equal conflicts | Same models/material/rules/expert time and matched coverage: erroneous assertions and correct resolutions; compilation may cost too much |
| SPINdle/defeasible logic | Mature rules, conflict and priority handling; ambiguity semantics depend on configuration | Source/subargument traces with grounded semantics | Compare compatible semantic subset; do not rank different semantics as accuracy; reuse if traces suffice |
| Mature ASPIC+ | Same formal foundation | Source bridges, exception policies and version interfaces | Same theory should produce identical states; differences are implementation/semantic issues, not algorithmic superiority |
| ANGRY plus ASPforASPIC, KR2025 | Datalog reduces grounding; reported experiments concern preference-free frameworks, with preferences future work | Complete finite construction retains reviewed priorities | Verify result/trace equivalence before speed/memory comparisons; do not transfer unproven preference guarantees |
| LRI/SILVER-style induction or legal-formalization LLMs | Rule generation can reduce drafting effort, but errors precede symbolic inference | Review drafts before frozen-package inference | Same review budget: condition/exception recall, compilation fidelity and coverage; review cost may erase gains |

C computes grounded acceptance for the declared finite graph/defeat/package. It does not guarantee factual truth or legal correctness. Reuse a compatible mature engine; prefer a cheaper checklist if it is equally effective for the target issue.

A stores source/extraction/identity/type versions; B adds view/grammar/search/encoding versions; C adds rule-package/preference/view versions. Publish complete snapshots, never new categories with old support counts.

After A changes, old mappings, witnesses and arguments are stale. Tracking existing positive dependencies alone is insufficient: new facts may make pruned queries frequent or create new attackers. Initially repair A locally, rerun B's search/selection for affected domains, and fully rebuild C for affected cases.

Union sources for repeated ground atoms while retaining different assertion statuses. Pattern macros add no evidential weight beyond their atoms. C must still access atoms outside the dictionary, especially rare defenses. Correct A from sources, never because a pattern looks attractive or correlates with outcomes. Freeze A before B discovery, B before independent confirmation; revisions require new versions and confirmation material.

EDC alone does not validate cross-contract joins; WARMR cannot repair merged contract IDs; ASPIC+ cannot repair false premises. Measure each contribution separately. Benefits need not add: strict clustering can lower support, MDL can omit rare defenses, and incorrect exception/burden compilation can reduce correct resolutions. Direct A→C access, retained rare candidates and separate coverage/gap reporting expose these tradeoffs.
