### 1. Board Status
The petitioner, J.B. Dadachanji, challenged the legal status of the Bombay Provincial Housing Board (the Board) to determine if it qualified for statutory exemptions.
*   **Original Status:** The Board was constituted by a Government Resolution in 1948 and established as a body corporate by the *Bombay Housing Board Act, 1948* [Sff6d93cc06d0 | IKWEB-152211:L96].
*   **Deemed Status:** Under Section 3(3) of the 1948 Act, the Board was deemed to be a "local authority" for the purposes of the *Bombay Rents, Hotel and Lodging House Rates Control Act, 1947* (Rent Act) and the *Land Acquisition (Bombay Amendment) Act, 1948* [S5f96a5787509 | IKWEB-152211:L102].
*   **Petitioner's Challenge:** The petitioner argued that the Board was **not** a local authority and therefore could not claim the benefit of Section 4 of the Rent Act, which exempts premises belonging to the Government or a local authority [S5c5f95d73288 | IKWEB-152211:L113].
*   **Legislative Clarification:** To resolve this ambiguity, the *Bombay Housing Board (Amendment) Act, 1951* was passed, adding Section 3-A. This section explicitly declared that the Rent Act shall not apply to land or buildings belonging to or vesting in the Board, nor to tenancies created by the Board [Sd74eb1d0ec62 | IKWEB-152211:L113].

### 2. Statutory Scope
The challenge centers on the scope of the *Bombay Rents, Hotel and Lodging House Rates Control Act, 1947* and the specific exemptions granted to the Board versus other housing entities.
*   **Original Exemption (Section 4):** The Rent Act did not apply to premises belonging to the Government or a local authority [Se44560fe91e7 | IKWEB-152211:L109].
*   **Amended Exemption (Section 3-A):** The 1951 Amendment extended this exemption retrospectively to the Board, stating the Act "shall not apply nor shall be deemed to have ever applied" to the Board's premises or tenancies [S928d0b11125e | IKWEB-152211:L115].
*   **The Comparator Evidence:** The petitioner argued that this exemption created unequal treatment compared to **Co-operative Housing Societies**. These societies are incorporated under the *Co-operative Societies Act*, have similar objectives (solving the housing problem), and manage housing lands, yet their lands and buildings are **not exempted** from the operation of the Bombay Rent Act [S3a4cb5d57469 | IKWEB-152211:L124].
*   **Constitutional Claim:** The petitioner contended that Section 3-A offended Article 14 of the Constitution (Equal Protection Clause) because it granted a retrospective exemption to the Board while denying the same to similarly situated Co-operative Societies [S5c5f95d73288 | IKWEB-152211:L113; S3a4cb5d57469 | IKWEB-152211:L124].

### 3. Proceedings
The legal history involves a progression from a small claims court to the High Court and finally to the Supreme Court via special leave.
*   **Initial Dispute:** In February 1950, the Board served a notice to quit, offering to waive it if the petitioner paid the revised rent of Rs. 56-8 per month. The petitioner refused, leading the Board to file suit in the **Court of Small Causes** for possession [S425c649bee00 | IKWEB-152211:L106].
*   **Lower Court Ruling:** The trial court held the Board was a local authority under Section 4 of the Rent Act and that Section 4 did not violate Article 14. It ordered delivery of possession [S808d8993dc70 | IKWEB-152211:L119].
*   **High Court Revision:** The petitioner moved the High Court in Civil Revision Application No. 567 of 1952. The High Court found it difficult to hold the Board was a local authority but ruled that **Section 3-A** of the 1951 Amendment retrospectively extended the exemption to the Board. It held there was no infringement of fundamental rights under Article 14 and dismissed the revision [S69838ebf5310 | IKWEB-152211:L120; S44341cce9445 | IKWEB-152211:L121].
*   **Current Appeal:** The petitioner applied for special leave to appeal to the Supreme Court and filed a substantive application under **Article 32** of the Constitution for enforcement of fundamental rights. Both were heard together [Sf0ec8991e3f5 | IKWEB-152211:L100; S149698b5d452 | IKWEB-152211:L122].

### 4. Comparator Evidence
The core of the petitioner's argument for unequal treatment relies on the existence of Co-operative Housing Societies.
*   **Similarity in Object:** The petitioner pointed out that Co-operative Housing Societies, incorporated under the *Co-operative Societies Act*, share the same objective as the Board: solving the housing problem [S3a4cb5d57469 | IKWEB-152211:L124].
*   **Difference in Treatment:** Despite this similarity in purpose and function, the lands and buildings of these Co-operative Societies are **not exempted** from the Bombay Rent Act, whereas the Board's lands are exempted by Section 3-A [S3a4cb5d57469 | IKWEB-152211:L124].
*   **Legal Basis for Challenge:** This disparity was used to argue that Section 3-A violates the "equal protection clause" of the Constitution (Article 14) [S3a4cb5d57469 | IKWEB-152211:L124].
