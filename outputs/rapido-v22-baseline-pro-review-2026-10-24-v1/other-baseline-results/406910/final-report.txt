Based on the supplied materials, here is an assessment of the competing positions regarding debt relief for the 1931 mortgage and the objection to reopening accounts from 1922, along with what the record establishes and what remains unresolved.

### 1. Competing Positions on Debt Relief (The 1931 Mortgage)

**The Judgment-Debtors' Position:**
The five judgment-debtors applied under **Section 8 of the U.P. Debt Redemption Act, 1940** to amend the decree passed in Suit No. 33 of 1938. Their primary contention was that they were "agriculturists" entitled to the benefits of the Act, which would allow for a reduction of interest and a recalculation of the principal.
*   **Status of Agriculturist:** They argued that despite Sita Ram having paid income-tax in 1932, he was not assessed to tax at the time of the 1931 mortgage or the 1940 application. They relied on witness testimony (Sita Ram and Suraj Mani Tripathi) stating that no income-tax was deducted in 1930 or 1931, and the first deduction occurred in February 1932 [S130, S132].
*   **Nature of Transaction:** They asserted the debt was a "loan" as defined by the Act, advanced in 1922, and recoverable from agriculturists [S125].

**The Decree-Holders' Position:**
The decree-holders objected to the application on two main grounds:
*   **Status of Agriculturist:** They contended that Sita Ram was assessed to income-tax at the date of the mortgage (1931) and continued to pay it, thereby disqualifying him (and by extension the joint family) from being classified as an "agriculturist" under the Act [S127]. They argued that because Sita Ram was a party to the 1931 mortgage, the entire family was ineligible for relief.
*   **Nature of Transaction:** They argued the debt advanced under the 1931 deed was not a "loan" as defined in the Act, implying the Act did not apply [S127].

### 2. Competing Positions on Reopening Accounts (1922 vs. 1931)

**The Judgment-Debtors' Position:**
The petitioners explicitly prayed that an account of the money-lending business be made from the beginning of **June 2, 1922**, the date of the earlier mortgage deed, rather than July 28, 1931 [S125]. They argued the debt was actually advanced in 1922 and that the 1931 deed merely recited the payment of the 1922 debt.

**The Decree-Holders' Position:**
The decree-holders did not contest the petitioners' claim that the debt originated in 1922. However, they did not actively argue against reopening the accounts from that earlier date; rather, they relied on the evidence adduced in the original 1938 suit, which likely focused on the 1931 transaction [S128, S135].

### 3. What the Available Record Establishes

The lower court (Additional Civil Judge) and the High Court have established several key findings based on the evidence and statutory interpretation:

*   **Establishment of Agriculturist Status:** The Additional Civil Judge found that Sita Ram was **not** assessed to income-tax at the date of the 1931 mortgage or the 1940 application. Consequently, the Judge held that the applicants were agriculturists and the case related to a "loan" as defined in the Act [S134].
*   **High Court's Ruling on Agriculturist Status:** The High Court dismissed the decree-holders' appeal on this point. The Division Bench held that the question of whether Sita Ram was an agriculturist on July 28, 1931, was **not material**. Their reasoning was that all judgment-debtors were admittedly agriculturists on the date of the suit (1938). Under **Section 21 of the U.P. Debt Redemption Act**, the mortgage amount could be recovered only from the mortgaged property, not personally. Therefore, the proviso requiring the advance to be made to an agriculturist at the time of the advance did not apply, provided they were agriculturists at the time of the suit [S138, S139].
*   **Establishment of Discharge:** The Additional Civil Judge, after concluding the applicants were agriculturists and the transaction was a loan, reopened the accounts from **June 2, 1922**. He found that the principal and interest payable according to the Act had been fully discharged. He declared the decree in Suit No. 33 of 1938 **discharged in full** and directed a note to that effect in the Register of Suits [S137].
*   **Evidence Deficiencies:** The record notes that the decree-holders failed to adduce any rebutting evidence regarding the income-tax assessments or the nature of the loan during the hearing of the Section 8 application [S133]. The Judge also adversely commented on the non-production of books of account by the decree-holders [S136].

### 4. What Remains Legally or Procedurally Unresolved

Despite the lower court's declaration of discharge, the matter is currently in an appellate phase before the Supreme Court (implied by the certificate under Section 110 of the CPC), leaving the following issues unresolved or subject to final determination:

*   **Finality of the Discharge:** While the Additional Civil Judge declared the decree discharged in full based on a recalculation from 1922, the decree-holders have appealed to the High Court, and subsequently sought a certificate to appeal to the Supreme Court. The final legal validity of the "full discharge" order is pending the Supreme Court's decision on the High Court's appeal [S140].
*   **The "Loan" Definition vs. Joint Family Liability:** Although the High Court ruled that the specific timing of the "agriculturist" status (1931 vs. 1938) was immaterial due to Section 21, the broader question of whether the transaction fits the strict definition of a "loan" under the Act when involving joint family members and recitals of prior debts remains a point of contention that the Supreme Court may review, particularly regarding the interaction between the Act and the Transfer of Property Act [S139, S151].
*   **Procedural Scope of Reopening:** The High Court noted that the question of whether accounts should be reopened from 1922 or 1931 was **not raised by the decree-holders** in their objection petition [S139]. Therefore, the Supreme Court may need to determine if the lower court had the jurisdiction to unilaterally reopen accounts from 1922 when the decree-holders did not contest the starting date, or if the relief should have been limited to the 1931 transaction.
*   **Impact of Income Tax Evidence:** The discrepancy between the income-tax assessment form dated February 9, 1933 (showing tax for 1931-32) and the witness testimony that no tax was deducted in 1931 remains a factual ambiguity. While the lower court accepted the witness testimony, the Supreme Court may scrutinize whether the existence of the assessment form invalidates the claim that no tax was levied in 1931, potentially affecting the "agriculturist" classification if the High Court's "materiality" argument is overturned [S133].

In summary, the record establishes that the lower courts favored the debtors, finding them eligible for relief and discharging the debt entirely based on a 1922 calculation. However, the legal resolution of the debtors' liability is currently suspended pending the Supreme Court's review of the decree-holders' appeal, which challenges the statutory interpretation regarding the timing of the "agriculturist" status and the propriety of reopening accounts from 1922.
