Based on the supplied materials, here is an assessment of the competing paths to specific performance and compensation, distinguishing the relevant legal issues regarding contract formation, prior leases, term, waiver, and procedural objections.

### 1. Contract Formation and Validity
The core dispute centers on whether a valid, binding contract existed between the Lime Co. (Plaintiff) and the State of Bihar (Defendant No. 1).

*   **Existence of Agreement:** The trial court found that the correspondence between the Lime Co. (Exhibit A, dated Jan 25, 1934) and the Board of Revenue (Exhibit 3z, dated March 14, 1934) constituted a valid agreement for a 20-year lease commencing April 1, 1934, with specific royalty terms ([S06cd204b2cf0](S06cd204b2cf0), [Sa9049bcb69cf](Sa9049bcb69cf)).
*   **Defendants' Objection (Mistake):** Defendant No. 1 argued that the contract was void under **Section 20 of the Indian Contract Act** because both parties were under a "mistake of fact" regarding the Government's title to the Murli Hills, given the prior lease to the Kuchwar Co. ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65)).
*   **Defendants' Objection (Statutory Compliance):** Defendant No. 1 also argued the contract was void under **Section 30 of the Government of India Act, 1915**, due to non-compliance with statutory conditions ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65)).
*   **Procedural Objection (Draft vs. Final):** Defendant No. 1 contended that the terms were not final, specifically regarding royalty rates, which they claimed were subject to re-examination ([Sd8493cbbf7bf](Sd8493cbbf7bf)).
*   **Plaintiff's Reliance on Drafts:** The Lime Co. relied on draft leases sent to the Government ([S4538bb57e9b7](S4538bb57e9b7)). However, the High Court noted that the plaintiff failed to produce the executed leases when called upon during the trial ([S561ba2462cc0](S561ba2462cc0), [Sed979d2f7f61](Sed979d2f7f61)).
*   **High Court's View on Mistake:** The High Court accepted the argument that the contract was void due to mutual mistake of fact regarding title ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65)), citing **Section 20 of the Indian Contract Act** ([L88b9afef9f01](L88b9afef9f01)).

### 2. Prior Lease and Forfeiture
The validity of the new contract was inextricably linked to the status of the prior lease held by the Kuchwar Lime and Stone Co.

*   **Prior Lease Status:** The Kuchwar Co. held a 20-year lease granted in 1928 ([S830e8b76ae83](S830e8b76ae83)). This lease contained a prohibition against assignment without Government permission ([S861302b02ec6](S861302b02ec6)).
*   **Assignment and Forfeiture:** The Kuchwar Co. attempted to assign the lease to Subodh Gopal Bose in 1933. The Government deemed this a breach and forfeited the lease on March 27, 1934 ([Sa7d4a6c96918](Sa7d4a6c96918)).
*   **Legal Challenge:** The Kuchwar Co. sued for a declaration that the forfeiture was invalid. The High Court of Patna initially ruled in their favor, and the Privy Council affirmed this decision on November 19, 1937 ([Sc0a06713e0fd](Sc0a06713e0fd)). Consequently, the Government's assumption that it had the right to re-lease the property to the Lime Co. was legally flawed at the time of the contract formation.
*   **Impact on Specific Performance:** Because the Government lacked the legal title to grant the lease (due to the invalid forfeiture of the Kuchwar lease), the High Court held that the contract with the Lime Co. was void ab initio ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65)).

### 3. Term and Specific Performance
The Lime Co. sought specific performance of the contract for the period commencing April 1, 1948, until they obtained possession ([S97682527e61d](S97682527e61d)).

*   **Readiness and Willingness:** Under **Section 18(a) of the Specific Relief Act**, a plaintiff must be ready and willing to perform their part of the contract. The High Court held that the Lime Co. was not ready and willing because they were asking for more than entitled to under the contract by invoking Section 18(a) without relinquishing claims ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65), [S06087d490791](S06087d490791)).
*   **Relinquishment of Claims:** To claim specific performance under **Section 15 of the Specific Relief Act**, the plaintiff must relinquish all claims to further performance and compensation for the period prior to the decree. The High Court found that the Lime Co.'s application did not clearly show such a relinquishment ([S952f65c826eb](S952f65c826eb)). Although the plaintiff later argued they relinquished claims for the period prior to April 1, 1948 ([S7093796118f1](S7093796118f1), [Sed355cb02b6e](Sed355cb02b6e)), the High Court maintained that the fundamental defect (void contract) precluded relief.
*   **Reconstruction of Agreement:** The High Court refused specific performance under **Section 18(a)** because it would amount to a reconstruction of the agreement between the parties, which is not permitted ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65)).

### 4. Compensation
While the primary relief sought was specific performance, the Lime Co. also claimed compensation for the period after the expiry of the Kuchwar lease ([S106d60d4a58a](S106d60d4a58a)).

*   **Trial Court Decree:** The trial court decreed the suit for specific performance and compensation, determining the amount in subsequent proceedings ([S97682527e61d](S97682527e61d)).
*   **High Court Disposition:** The High Court dismissed the suit entirely. Since the contract was void, there was no breach of contract to which **Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act** (compensation for breach) could apply ([L09e01b11b030](L09e01b11b030)). The High Court held that no relief could be granted under the Specific Relief Act because the contract itself was invalid ([S4da221281e65](S4da221281e65)).
*   **Alternative Relief:** The plaintiff alternatively claimed relief under **Section 15** of the Specific Relief Act, but this was disallowed because the conditions (relinquishment of claims) were not met ([S952f65c826eb](S952f65c826eb)).

### 5. Procedural Objections and Other Defenses
*   **Limitation, Estoppel, and Waiver:** The trial court rejected the pleas of limitation, estoppel, waiver, and acquiescence raised by Defendant No. 1 ([Sa9049bcb69cf](Sa9049bcb69cf)).
*   **Locus Standi of Defendant No. 2:** Defendant No. 2 (Dalmia Jain & Co. Ltd.) was a subsequent lessee. The trial court found they had no locus standi to contest specific performance because they were aware of the Lime Co.'s prior contract ([Sa9049bcb69cf](Sa9049bcb69cf)).
*   **Legal Existence:** Defendant No. 2 denied the Lime Co.'s legal existence. The trial court rejected this, finding the Lime Co. had the right to sue ([Sa9049bcb69cf](Sa9049bcb69cf)).
*   **Res Judicata:** The High Court noted that the Kuchwar Co. had previously litigated the validity of the forfeiture. While the Lime Co. was not a party to that suit, the High Court's decision in the current appeal focused on the validity of the *new* contract rather than the res judicata effect of the prior suit on the Lime Co. specifically, though the outcome of the prior suit (affirming the Kuchwar lease) was the factual basis for the voidness of the Lime Co. contract.

### Conclusion
The appellate court (High Court) reversed the trial court's decree. The reasoning was that although a contract appeared to exist on its face, it was **void** due to a **mutual mistake of fact** regarding the Government's title to the property (due to the invalid forfeiture of the Kuchwar lease) and non-compliance with the **Government of India Act, 1915**. Consequently, **specific performance** was unavailable as it would require reconstructing a void agreement, and **compensation** was unavailable as there was no valid contract to breach. The procedural objections regarding the plaintiff's readiness and willingness to perform were secondary to the fundamental invalidity of the contract.
