### 1. Challenge to Arbitration Award: Separate Examination and Opportunity to Respond
The core challenge to the arbitration award rests on the allegation of **legal misconduct** by the arbitrator regarding the procedure of separate examinations.

*   **The Allegation:** The plaintiff alleged that the arbitrator, Sri Konakondla Rayalla Govindappa, committed legal misconduct by examining the parties separately. Specifically, the arbitrator examined the plaintiff on March 6, 1945, and Defendant 1 on March 10, 1945, in the absence of the other party [S5609e2c7f51d]. The plaintiff contended that the arbitrator perused a settlement dated May 1, 1927, and obtained a statement from Defendant 1 regarding it without giving the plaintiff an opportunity to be heard on the matter [S8255b747dac7].
*   **The Defense:** The defendants argued that the arbitrator's actions were merely to obtain a reiteration of the requests contained in the parties' written petitions for reference. They contended that no new facts were recorded from Defendant 1 that were not already present in his written statement (Exhibit No. 5), and therefore, no opportunity to respond was legally required [S2a96df6fbeff].
*   **Arbitrator's Stance:** The arbitrator stated that while the Court directed him to make an award based on the plaint and written statements, he proceeded to take statements from both parties as well as peruse the settlement document mentioned by Defendant 1 [Sa8295995d227].

### 2. Distinction of Maintenance Claim and Allegations
The underlying dispute involves a claim for maintenance and property rights arising from the death of P. Narayanappa in 1927.

She claimed she had to leave the family house due to disputes with the senior wife of Defendant 1 [Sc65014c8a714].
*   **Defendants' Allegations:** The defendants contested the claim on two main grounds:

    2.  **Denial of Stridhanam:** They denied her claim for jewels and clothes [S7e1cecb5a975].
*   **Arbitrator's Findings:** In his award, the arbitrator accepted the defendants' assertion regarding the 1927 settlement and awarded the plaintiff additional land (8 acres 17 cents) in addition to the 40 acres already given by the deceased [Sa8295995d227].
