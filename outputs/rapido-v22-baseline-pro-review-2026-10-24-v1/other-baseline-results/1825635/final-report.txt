Based on the supplied materials, here is the assessment of whether the later adoption supports the present property claim despite the compromise, payment, and asserted *res judicata* and estoppel, separated by findings, current scope, and open legal issues.

### 1. Separate Findings

**A. Validity of the Later Adoption (1943)**
*   **Finding:** The later adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai on December 12, 1943, was held proved and valid by both the Trial Judge and the High Court.
*   **Source:** [S72] confirms that the adoption was "held proved by, both the courts below and was held to be a valid adoption."
*   **Context:** This adoption occurred after the 1937 compromise decree ([S60]) and the Privy Council decision in *Anant v. Shankar* ([S62]), which had previously advised Gangabai that she could adopt again.

**B. Invalidity of the Defendant's Counter-Adoption**
*   **Finding:** The adoption of Defendant 2 (Jivaji) by Defendant 1 was held invalid because it was subsequent in date to the Plaintiff's adoption.
*   **Source:** [S69] states the Trial Judge concluded the adoption of Defendant 2 was "invalid by reason of the adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai which was anterior in date." [S72] reiterates that the High Court confirmed this finding.

**C. Effect of the 1937 Compromise Decree**
*   **Finding:** The 1937 decree explicitly declared the Plaintiff's adoption invalid and that he was not entitled to the family property ([S51]-[S53]). However, it included a compromise clause where Defendant 1 agreed to pay Rs. 8,000 to the Plaintiff to maintain peace ([S57]).
*   **Source:** [S51] notes the award declared the adoption "not valid." [S60] confirms the decree was made on August 6, 1937, after the Plaintiff admitted the terms and received the money.

**D. Estoppel and Res Judicata Arguments**
*   **Finding:** The Trial Judge held that while the Plaintiff was estopped from claiming possession *during* Defendant 1's lifetime, he was entitled to possession *after* her death. The Judge also found the suit was *not* barred by *res judicata*.
*   **Source:** [S69] states the Plaintiff was "estopped from claiming possession of the properties from the Defendant 1 and was only entitled to possession of the properties belonging to the family after her death." [S66] notes Defendant 1 argued *res judicata*, but the Trial Judge concluded the suit was "not barred."

### 2. Current Scope

The current scope of the legal analysis is limited to the **points of law** specifically canvassed before the Federal Court (the appellate body hearing the present appeal), as the facts were not contested.

*   **Primary Issue:** Whether the present suit is barred by *res judicata* due to the consent decree passed in Suit No. 291 of 1937 ([S75]).
*   **Secondary Issue:** Whether the Plaintiff's claim is barred by estoppel ([S75]).
*   **Factual Baseline:** The scope accepts as established facts that:
    1.  The 1943 adoption of the Plaintiff is valid ([S72]).
    2.  The 1943 adoption of Defendant 2 is invalid due to timing ([S72]).
    3.  The 1937 decree declared the adoption invalid but settled the financial aspect via a compromise payment ([S51]-[S60]).

### 3. Open Legal Issues

Despite the lower courts' findings, the following legal issues remain open for determination by the Federal Court based on the provided text:

**A. The Interaction of *Res Judicata* with Subsequent Events**
*   **The Issue:** The 1937 decree ([S60]) explicitly declared the adoption invalid ([S52]). The Plaintiff is now claiming rights based on a *new* adoption in 1943 ([S62], [S64]).
*   **The Question:** Does the principle of *res judicata* (Section 11 of the CPC, [L193e14a39240]) bar a fresh suit when the factual basis of the claim (the adoption) has materially changed after the first decree? The lower courts found the suit was not barred ([S66]), but this relies on the interpretation of whether the "matter directly and substantially in issue" in 1937 (validity of the *first* adoption) is the same as the issue in 1944 (validity of the *second* adoption).

**B. The Scope of Estoppel Regarding Property Rights**
*   **The Issue:** The Trial Judge applied estoppel to prevent the Plaintiff from claiming possession *during* Defendant 1's lifetime ([S69]), despite the 1937 decree having declared the adoption invalid.
*   **The Question:** Does the compromise payment of Rs. 8,000 ([S57]) and the subsequent maintenance payments ([S61]) create an estoppel that prevents the Plaintiff from asserting his title to the property *after* Defendant 1's death? The lower courts distinguished between possession during life and after death, but the legal basis for this distinction in light of the 1937 declaration remains a point of contention.

**C. Validity of the "Second Adoption" Under Hindu Law**
*   **The Issue:** While the lower courts accepted the 1943 adoption as valid, the 1937 arbitration award had declared the right of adoption "lost to Gangabai from the very beginning" ([S54]).
*   **The Question:** Does the Privy Council decision in *Anant v. Shankar* ([S62]), which allowed for a new adoption, override the specific terms of the 1937 compromise award regarding the "loss of right"? The materials suggest the Plaintiff acted on legal advice following the Privy Council decision, but the legal conflict between the specific compromise terms and the general law post-Privy Council is an open issue.

**D. Execution of the 1937 Decree vs. New Suit**
*   **The Issue:** Section 47 of the CPC ([Lf7825667c6c4]) states questions relating to the execution of a decree should be determined by the executing court, not a separate suit.
*   **The Question:** Since the 1937 decree included a compromise payment and a declaration of invalidity, does the Plaintiff's claim for possession constitute a question of "execution" of that decree (which might be barred) or a new cause of action based on a new legal fact (the 1943 adoption)? The lower courts treated it as a new suit, but the applicability of Section 47 is a potential legal hurdle.
