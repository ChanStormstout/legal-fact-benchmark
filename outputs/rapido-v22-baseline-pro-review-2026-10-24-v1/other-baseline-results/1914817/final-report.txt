### 1. Separation of Batches and Periods (Before and After Possession)

The litigation is divided into two distinct batches of suits, each with a specific timeline regarding possession and the claim for equitable set-off.

**Batch 1 (Appeals Nos. 68 to 74 of 1951):**
*   **Possession Date:** The plaintiff (Maharaja Bahadur Singh) obtained actual possession of the lands in **August 1913** [S830ce6ec7c1c].
*   **Initial Claim:** An application for ascertainment of mesne profits was made on **6th November 1918** [S830ce6ec7c1c].
*   **First Set of Objections:** The defendant resisted this, claiming the plaintiff was not entitled to interest and that deductions should be made for *munafa* and chowkidari dues [Sce40b4b270ac].
*   **Second Set of Objections (Equitable Set-off):** Five years later, on **24th June 1927**, the zamindar filed objections claiming a deduction by way of equitable set-off for payments made **subsequent to the date of delivery of possession** (post-1913), as well as for *munafa* payable after that date [S5646ec95254b].
*   **Trial Court Decision (1937):** The trial court decreed mesne profits but allowed deductions up to the date of assessment, **disallowing** the equitable set-off for the subsequent period [S5b45c374137a].
*   **District Judge Decision:** Reversed the trial court, allowing the equitable set-off for the subsequent period (defined as from the date of delivery of possession up to 1927) [S791d52f699e4], [Sd048fca3697e].
*   **High Court Decision:** Disallowed the claim for equitable set-off in its entirety for the subsequent period, restoring the trial court's decree [Sf6e09ab6eed2].

**Batch 2 (Appeals Nos. 75 to 92 of 1951):**
*   **Possession Date:** The plaintiffs obtained delivery of possession on **23rd November 1910** [Sa9b109d3e1de], [S136].
*   **Initial Claim:** Applications for ascertainment of mesne profits were filed on **8th December 1922** for the years 1906 to 1910 [Sa34cd2105e19].
*   **Objections:** On **28th May 1927**, the zamindar filed an application claiming deduction by way of equitable set-off for amounts due for rent from **1910 onwards** (post-possession) and for subsequent payments of revenue and cesses [Sa34cd2105e19].
*   **Trial Court Decision (1937):** Decreed mesne profits but allowed deductions only up to the date of delivery of possession, holding the appellant was **not entitled** to any equitable set-off for sums spent on revenue, cesses, or *munafa/profits* for the period **subsequent to the date of delivery of possession** [Sc4819a8e10e5].
*   **District Judge Decision:** Allowed the claim of equitable set-off for the period subsequent to delivery of possession, directing that defendant's dues be deducted from plaintiff's dues [S35c88a4edcdc], [Se89720073e4f].
*   **High Court Decision:** Restored the trial court's decision, disallowing the equitable set-off for the subsequent period [Sf6e09ab6eed2], [S144].

### 2. Competing Judgments and Reasoning

The core conflict lies between the Trial Courts (which largely disallowed the set-off for the post-possession period) and the District Judges (who allowed it), with the High Court siding with the Trial Courts.

*   **District Judge's Reasoning (Allowing Set-off):**
    *   The District Judge emphasized "substantial justice" given the "peculiar" and "tortuous" course of litigation spanning half a century [S114].
    *   He argued that it was not sufficient to say the plea was not raised initially because the litigation was extraordinary and unpredictable [S114].
    *   He held that no question of time-barred debts or unascertained sums arose in the context of equitable set-off [S115].
    *   He viewed the claim as a cross-demand arising from the same transaction, noting there was no substantial difference in the character of the parties during the entire period [S117].
    *   He allowed the set-off for the period from the date of delivery of possession up to 1927 [S118], [Sd048fca3697e].

*   **High Court's Reasoning (Disallowing Set-off):**
    *   The High Court modified the decrees of the District Judge and disallowed the equitable set-off for the subsequent period in both batches of appeals [Sf6e09ab6eed2], [S144].
    *   While the specific reasoning of the High Court in the excerpts is limited to the disposition, the Trial Courts' reasoning (which the High Court restored) was that the appellant was not entitled to set-off for sums spent on revenue, cesses, or profits for the period *after* possession was delivered [Sc4819a8e10e5].

### 3. New Appellate Grounds

During the pendency of the appeals in the Supreme Court (transferred from the Privy Council jurisdiction), the appellant applied to urge additional grounds not previously taken [S123]. These grounds specifically targeted the calculation of *munafa* (rent/profit):

1.  **Ground 1:** That the *munafa* should not be calculated on the basis of the principles laid down in *Radhacharan v. Maharaja Ranjit Singh* [S059cada2a54b].
2.  **Ground 2:** That the *munafa* should have been assessed on a "fair share of the profits from the land" rather than the established principles [S6c0a22a9bfbe].

These grounds relate to the quantum of the plaintiff's claim (mesne profits) rather than the defendant's right to set-off, but they are part of the reconstructed appellate record regarding the financial adjustments between the parties.

### Summary of Disposition
*   **Trial Courts:** Generally disallowed equitable set-off for the period *after* possession was delivered.
*   **District Judges:** Allowed equitable set-off for the period *after* possession was delivered, citing the need for substantial justice in long, complex litigation.
*   **High Court:** Disallowed the equitable set-off for the subsequent period in both batches, restoring the Trial Courts' decrees.
*   **Current Status:** The appeals are before the Supreme Court, where the appellant seeks to challenge the calculation of *munafa* on new grounds, while the core issue of the equitable set-off for the post-possession period remains the subject of the High Court's decision which the appellant is now appealing.
