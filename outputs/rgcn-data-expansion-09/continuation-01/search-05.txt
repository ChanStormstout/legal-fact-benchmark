Respondent no. 1 is his tenant under the petitioner in respect of one shop shown as red colour in the site plan on a monthly rent of Rs. 300/- excluding of other charges. Haridwar, Uttrakhand. Respondent no. 2 is a legal tenant and is in exclusive possession and in full control of the tenanted shop and he is running the business in the said shop himself exclusively. One notice dated 12.08.2008 was sent to the responsible no. 1. The tenancy of respondent no. 1 is expired on 30.11.2008 u/s. 106 of Transfer of Property Act. Thus, this petition.

Respondent, on the other hand, has stated to the contrary that respondent no. 2 is the son of respondent no. 1 and respondent no. 1 is still the proprietor of the said printing concerned. It is further stated by the respondent that nature of the tenancy has not been changed till today and since the inception of tenancy, the printing press is bing run till today under the control and supervision of respondent no. 1.

11. Thus, the limited point involved here in this petition filed u/s. 14 (1) (b) that whether the respondent no. 1 has subletted the premises to respondent no. 2 and whether the act alleged by the petitioner amounts to subletting or not. Section 14 (1) (b) DRC Act reads as under:-

> "That the tenant has, on or after the 9th day of June, 1952, sub-let assigned or other wise parted with the possession of the whole or any part of the premises without obtaining the consent in writing of the landlord"

12. Reliance can be relied upon following judgments for reference:-

> i.) "LIC v/s. Bharat Sales Ltd., 1995 (1) RCR (rent) 422". In this case, it is held by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi that direct evidence pertaining to the subletting is rarely available initially onus lies on landlord. Onus is discharged by the landlord. If, he brings material on record prima facily CASE No. RC - 01/08 6/10 evidence, leading to inference of subletting. Onus, then is on tenant to bring on record the facts within his exclusively knowledge and has to rebut the inference raised in favour of landlord.

CASE No. RC - 01/08 9/10

14. Similar is the situation present here in this case. In the present case, respondent no. 2 is admittedly the son of respondent no. 1 who is admittedly managing the affairs of the business run by the respondent no. 1 at the tenanted premises. It is however, stated by the respondent that respondent no. 1 is having the full control over the said tenanted shop for which the petitioner has not been able to show anything to the contrary except his oral evidence which has been countered by the respondents in their respective oral evidence. Respondents have further shown by way of some documents that the business in the premises is still in the name of S.R. Printers for which respondent no. 1 is proprietor. In these circumstances, therefore, I am of the considered opinion that in the light of the above mentioned reasonings and relying upon above mentioned judgment, the petitioner has not been able to show that respondent no. 1 has subletted the tenanted premises to respondent no. 2. Therefore, petitioner has been failed to show from the record that there is subletting in this case, therefore, he is not entitled to an order of eviction u/s. 14 (1) (b) of DRC Act. Thus, petition filed by the petitioner is dismissed without cost.

Rajdhani Law Reporters, where it is held that mere fact of permitting another person to use portion of premises without written consent of landlord is contrary to S. 14 (1) (b) and establishes sub­letting. If landlord shows possession of another then onus of proving capacity of possession is on the tenant who cannot camouflage sub­letting in the guise of license. Offering rent by such licensee to landlord affirms his sub­letting.

(ii) Duli Chand (D) By LRs Vs. Jagmender Dass, 1990 Rajdhani Law Reporters (SC) 13, where it is held that if a tenant of a shop permits his natural son given away in adoption to another to do his own proprietory 8 business in the shop, the tenant having no interest in the business and works elsewhere with no concurrent user of tenanted shop then it is parting with possession.
