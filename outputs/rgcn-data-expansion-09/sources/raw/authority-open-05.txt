L73:     cite12†Delhi Rent Control Act (59 of 1958) Ss. 14 and 39  (2)-Onus
L74:     of proving sub-letting--Mixed question of law and fact, what
L75:     is.

L79:     HEADNOTE:
L80:     The  appellant	took  a	 lease of  shop	 premises  from	 the
L81:     respondent.  From the time of letting, a chemist's  business
L82:     was carried on in the shop by S with the occasional help  of
L83:     the  appellant.	 S and the appellant were living as  husband
L84:     and wife to the knowledge of the respondent.  The respondent
L85:     applied under cite13†s. 14 of the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958, for
L86:     eviction of the appellant on the ground that she had  sublet
L87:     the premises toS. The Rent Controller and the  Tribunal
L88:     on  appeal  held  that	the appellant  and  S.	were  living
L89:     together  as  husband and wife, and   that  therefore  there
L90:     wasno  question	 of any subletting by  the  appellant.	In
L91:     second appeal,	holding that two substantial  questions	 of
L92:     law were involved namely, one relatingto the status of the
L93:     appellant  as  the wife of S, and the  other,  whether	sub-
L94:     lettingwas  established,  the High Court  concluded  that
L95:     there was subletting in favour oOf S.
L96:     Allowing the appeal to this Court,
L97:     HELD : (1) Under cite14†s. 39 (2) of the Act the High.-Court  could
L98:     interfere  in second appeal only if there was a	 substantial
L99:     question of law.  On the question whether the appellant	 was
L100:     legally	 married  no finding was necessary in  the  eviction
L101:     suit.	It was sufficient for the rent court to	 proceed  on
L102:     the finding that the appellant and S were living together as
L103:     husband and wife, whether they were legally married or	not.
L104:     [528C-D, E-F]
L105:     (2)The	question  whether there was subletting	is  not	 a
L106:     mixed question of law and fact.	 In a mixed question of	 law
L107:     and fact the ultimate conclusion has to be drawn by applying
L108:     principles   of	  law  to  basic  findings,   but   in	 the
L109:     determination  of a question of fact no application  of	 any
L110:     principle  of  law is required in finding either  the  basic
L111:     facts  or  in  arriving at  the	 ultimate  conclusion.	 The
L112:     question to be determined in the circumstances of this	case
L113:     was whether it was likely that the appellant had sublet	 the
L114:     premises  to S. The negative answer given by the rent  court
L115:     is  merely the factual common sense inference which did	 not
L116:     call  for the application of any principle of law.  [528F-G;
L117:     529A-B]
L118:     Meenakshi Mills, Madurai v. The Commissioner of	 Income-tax,
L119:     Madras, [1956] S.C.R. 691, followed.
L120:     (3)When eviction is sought on the ground of subletting the
L121:     onus  of  proving  subletting is on the	 landlord.   If	 the
L122:     landlord  prima-facie  shows that the occupant	was  in	 the
L123:     exclusive  possession of the premises let out  for  valuable
L124:     consideration, it would then be for the tenant to rebut	 the
L125:     evidence.   But in the present case the respondent  produced
L126:     no  evidence to show subletting in spite of the	 appellant's
L127:     denial in the written statement. [527C-D]
L128:     Associated  Hotels  of	India Ltd.  Delhi v.  S.  B.  Sardar
L129:     Ranjit Singh, [1968] 2 S.C.R. 548, followed.
L130:     (4)Under  s. 14 (4) premises could be deemed to have  been
L131:     sub-let by the tenant only when the Controller is  satisfied
L132:     that  some  person is let into possession  ostensibly  as  a
L133:     partner	  in  business	but  really  for  the  purposes	  of
L134:     subletting.  This provision has no application to the  facts
L135:     and circumstances of the present case. [526G-H]

L172: 
L173: > (ii)used the premises for a purpose other than that for which they were let; or
L174: > (k)that the tenant has, whether before or after the commencement of this Act, caused or permitted to be ,caused substantial damage to the premises, or notwithstanding previous notice has used or dealt 'with the premises in a manner contrary to any condition imposed on the landlord by the Government, or the Delhi Improvement Trust while giving him a lease of the land on which the premises are situated;"
L175: The respondent constructed the building known as the Hotel Imperial, New Delhi, on land leased to him by the Secretary of State for India in Council under a perpetual lease deed dated July 9, 1937. By a deed dated August 18, 1939, he leased to the appellant the hotel premises together with fittings and furniture for a term of 20 years commencing on September 15, 1939.
L176: On January 28, 1958, the respondent instituted the present suit alleging that in breach of the express conditions of the lease dated August .18, 1939, the appellant sub-let portions of the premises and made unauthorised additions and alterations in the premises, that on such breaches he was entitled to determine the lease and he did so, by notice in writing dated January 6, 1958. He claimed eviction of the appellant on the grounds mentioned in cls. (b), (c) and (k) of the proviso to cite12†s.
L177: 13(1) of the Act. The appellant filed its written statement on April 3, 1958 denying most of the material allegations in the plaint. The appellant also pleaded that the respondent had waived the breaches, if any, of the conditions of the lease by accepting rents with knowledge of such breaches and particularly by accepting rent on or about January 3, 1958. On April 24, 1958, Sri P. L. Vohra, counsel for the appellant, made the following statement before the trial Court :
L178: > "The plaintiff can seek ejectment of the defendant only under cite13†section 13 of Act 38 of 1952. In case the 551 plaintiff succeeds in establishing the liability of, the defendant for ejectment on any of the grounds given in .cite14†section 13 of the Rent Act, the defendant would not seek any protection under the terms of the lease deed dated 18th August, 1939 executed between the parties, as regards the period of lease fixed therein. . ."
L179: Having regard to the pleadings and statement of counsel, the Court settled the following issues on May 12, 1958 :

L182: 
L183: > 2. If so, was the same done with express or implied consent of the plaintiff ?
L184: > 3. Whether the defendant had sublet, assigned or otherwise parted with possession of any part of the suit premises after the commencement of Act 38 of 1952 ?
L185: 
L186: > 4.If so, was the same done with the prior consent in writing of the plaintiff ?
L187: 
L188: > 5.Whether the defendant has used the tenancy premises for a purpose other than that for which they were let ?

L192: 
L193: > 8. Is the defendant entitled to special cost ?
L194: 
L195: > 9. Whether the plaintiff is estopped or has waived his right to seek ejectment of the defendant on any of the grounds mentioned above ? If so, what and to what effect ?
L196: > 10.Whether the defendant is entitled to sublet any part of the hotel premises even when there was a clause to the contra in the lease dated the 18th August, 1939. and in face of statutory provisions under the cite15†Rent Control Act (for reasons given in para 16 of the amended written statement) ?"
L197: A tenant holding premises under a subsisting lease is pro- tected by the lease and needs no protection under the cite16†Rent Act . It was open to the appellant to contend that it was protected by 552 the terms of the lease dated August 18, 1939, that the breaches, if any, of the conditions of the lease had been waived by the respondent and that the lease had not determined. But the appellant deliberately elected to seek protection under s. 13 of the Act only.
L198: The appellant's counsel made a' formal statement in the trial Court that the appellant would not seek any protection under the terms of the lease deed as regards the period of the lease fixed therein. The Court accordingly settled the ten issues. Issue No. 8 was not pressed. All the other issues relate to thegrounds of eviction mentioned in cls. (b), (c) and
L199: (k) of theproviso to s. 13(1) of the Act. Issue No. 9 raises the question- of waiver of the respondent's right to seek ejectment on those grounds. Thus, the only questions in issue between the parties was whether the appellant was entitled to protection from eviction under cite13†s. 13 and whether any ground for eviction under the Act was made out. The case was tried and decided on this footing.
L200: We have come to this conclusion after a close examination of the 'pleadings, particulars, statement of counsel, issues and the judgment of the trial Court. No issue was raised on the question whether the breaches of the express conditions of the lease had been waived by the respondent, and whether the lease was still subsisting.
L201: The appellant sought to raise this plea in the High Court and also in this Court Having regard to the deliberate stand taken by the appellant in the trial Court, the appellant cannot be allowed to raise the plea at a later stage. The lease determined by efflux of time on September 15, 1959.
L202: Had the appellant taken the plea that the lease had not determined by forfeiture on the date of the institution of the suit, it is possible that the respondent might have filed another suit for ejectment of the appellant immediately after September 15, 1959. Because of the stand taken by the appellant, it was not necessary for the respondent to file another suit. This appeal must be decided on the footing that the lease had determined by forfeiture on the date of the institution of the present suit.

L303: KHALID, J. I have gone through the Judgment prepared by my learned brother. I agree with the conclusion that the appeal has to be allowed.
L304: We have before us two parties, both affluent. No tears need be shed either for the one or the other. The tenant before us, or to be precise the sub-tenant, is a firm which does not deserve any sympathy from us and that for an excellant reason. They had given an undertaking before this Court that they would withdraw the suit filed by them for fixation of fair rent.
L305: This undertaking they did not respect till now, obviously with the oblique motive of compelling the landlord to get the rent reduced and at the same time walk away with an order from this Court avoiding eviction. Left to myself, I would have declined relief to the appellants or at least directed them to pay a sum of Rs.5,000 every month as rent.
L306: However, in the peculiar facts and circumstances of this case, where the conduct of the landlord is anything but wholesome, I agree with my learned brother in the order passed by him allowing the appeal. But, I would like to make my position clear regarding the scope and purpose of cite14†section 17 and cite15†18 of the Act.
L307: The normal rule is that all rights created by a tenant disappear along with the disappearance of his tenancy unless there are special satisfactory safeguards for the sub- tenants. A sub-tenant has no independent existence de-hors the tenant who inducted him into possession. cite23†In the Act before us a subtenant is given a special right, not available to him under the general law, but that right is circumscribed by specific conditions laid down in section
L308: 17. We have chosen to rescue the appellants before us only because of the hide and seek 398 conduct displayed by the so-called tenant and the so-called land-lord in this case. The facts speak for themselves. Even a man who runs can see that the so-called tenant in this case is the alter ego of the so-called land-lord. There is a total identification between the two.
L309: It is their attempt to over reach the appellants by dubious methods that has, in fact, imperilled their case, and it is for this reason that the appellants get relief from us, even though strict adherence to the conditions imposed under cite14†section 17 is absent.
L310: In normal case a sub-tenant under the Act can get relief under the provision of the Act only if he satisfies the twin conditions laid down in cite14†section 17 , viz., that there must be the previous consent in writing by the land- lord, of the creation of the sub-tenancy and a notice in the prescribed manner by the sub-tenant of the creation of the sub-tenancy to the land-lord within one month of the date of such creation.
L311: It is only when these two conditions are satisfied that the consequences mentioned in cite25†section 18(1) will follow.
