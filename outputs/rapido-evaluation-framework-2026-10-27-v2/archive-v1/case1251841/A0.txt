### 3. Open Legal Questions and Statutory Analysis
The core legal question before the Court is whether a mortgagor in possession, prior to the enactment of **Section 65-A of the Transfer of Property Act, 1929**, had the power to grant a permanent lease that would bind the mortgagee.

*   **Statutory Context:**
    *   **Section 65-A (TPA):** Enacted in 1929, this section explicitly grants a mortgagor in possession the power to make leases binding on the mortgagee, subject to conditions (best rent, no premium, no renewal covenant, max 3 years for buildings, etc.). **[L502721c563fa | LAW:TPA65A]**
    *   **Section 66 (TPA):** Prior to Section 65-A, the governing provision was Section 66. It states a mortgagor is not liable for deterioration unless they commit an act "destructive or permanently injurious" if the security is insufficient or **will be rendered insufficient** by such act. **[L4cb87a2c0ccd | IKWEB-1251841:L124]**
    *   **Interpretation of "Permanent Lease":** A permanent lease is generally considered an act that renders the security insufficient because it transfers the beneficial interest indefinitely, unlike a lease for a term of years which might be seen as management. However, the High Court applied a specific test under Section 66: whether the lease rendered the security insufficient. **[Sd3c548138ec6 | IKWEB-1251841:L133]**

*   **The High Court's Reasoning (Reviewable):**
    *   The High Court determined that the crucial test under Section 66 was whether the lease rendered the mortgagee's security insufficient.
    *   Despite no specific allegation in the plaint regarding insufficiency, the High Court calculated figures and concluded that the lease of the disputed villages **did not** render the Bank's security insufficient.
    *   Consequently, the High Court held the lease valid under the law as it stood before 1929. **[S978119f4c532 | IKWEB-1251841:L134]**, **[S0c80c8bdc534 | IKWEB-1251841:L135]**

### 4. Assessment of Binding Effect
Based strictly on the provided text, the assessment of whether the lease binds the claimant mortgagee hinges on the application of **Section 66 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882** (as Section 65-A was not yet in force in 1925).

*   **The Test:** Under Section 66, a mortgagor cannot commit acts that render the security insufficient. The High Court interpreted this to mean that if the security remains sufficient despite the lease, the act is not "destructive or permanently injurious" in the legal sense required to invalidate the lease against the mortgagee.
*   **The Outcome in Lower Courts:** The High Court found the security was *not* rendered insufficient. Therefore, the lease was valid and **did not bind** the plaintiff (who stood in the shoes of the Bank). The plaintiff was entitled to possession because the lease was deemed valid but did not defeat the mortgagee's rights to recover possession once the mortgage was enforced and the property sold. **[S0c80c8bdc534 | IKWEB-1251841:L135]**
*   **The Supreme Court's Stance:** The Supreme Court limited its consideration to the specific question of the mortgagor's power to grant the lease under the pre-1929 law. It did not explicitly overturn the High Court's finding on the sufficiency of security in the provided excerpts, but it framed the issue as one of statutory interpretation regarding the power to lease prior to 1929. **[S34eef73a1804 | IKWEB-1251841:L121]**

**Conclusion:**
Under the law applicable at the time of the lease (pre-1929 Transfer of Property Act), the permanent lease granted by Wazir Narain Singh **did not bind the claimant mortgagee** according to the High Court's reasoning. The lease was valid because it did not render the mortgage security insufficient under Section 66. Consequently, the plaintiff, as the auction purchaser subrogated to the Bank's position, was entitled to recover possession from the lessees (Hiraman Ram and Chohan Ram). The primary open legal question resolved (at the High Court level and accepted as the surviving issue) was the interpretation of the mortgagor's power to lease under Section 66 before the codification of such power in Section 65-A.
