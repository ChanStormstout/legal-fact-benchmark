[IK-1841885:L101] In cite17†Saleem Bhai and Ors. v. [IK-1841885:L104] The trial Court must remember that if on a meaningful and not formal reading of the plaint it is manifestly vexatious and meritless in the sense of not disclosing a clear right to sue, it should exercise the power under Order VII Rule 11 of the Code taking care to see that the ground mentioned therein is fulfilled. [IK-1841885:L105] Arivandandam v. T.V. Satyapal and Anr. (1977 (4) SCC 467) It is trite law that not any particular plea has to be considered, and the whole plaint has to be read. [IK-1841885:L106] In Raptakos Brett & Co.Ltd. v. Ganesh Property (1998 (7) SCC 184) it was observed that the averments in the plaint as a whole have to be seen to find out whether clause
[IK-1841885:L107] 
[IK-1841885:L108] (d) of Rule 11 of Order VII was applicable.
[IK-1841885:L109] There cannot be any compartmentalization, dissection, segregation and inversions of the language of various paragraphs in the plaint. If such a course is adopted it would run counter to the cardinal canon of interpretation according to which a pleading has to be read as a whole to ascertain its true import. It is not permissible to cull out a sentence or a passage and to read it out of the context in isolation.
[IK-1841885:L110] Although it is the substance and not merely the form that has to be looked into, the pleading has to be construed as it stands without addition or subtraction or words or change of its apparent grammatical sense. [IK-1841885:L120] There is distinction between 'material facts' and 'particulars'. The words 'material facts' show that the facts necessary to formulate a complete cause of action must be stated. Order XXV, Rule 4 (see Philipps v. Philipps ((1878) 4 QBD
[IK-1841885:L122] > 127)); or "a further and better statement of claim" may be ordered under Rule 7.

The use of particulars is intended to meet a further and quite separate requirement of pleading, imposed in fairness and justice to the defendant.
[IK-1841885:L124] Their function is to fill in the picture of the plaintiff's cause of action with information sufficiently detailed to put the defendant on his guard as to the case he had to meet and to enable him to prepare for trial.

[IK-1841885:L126] > The word 'material' shows that the facts necessary to formulate a complete cause of action must be stated.
