# Legal AI: Research Design from Fact Canonicalization to Patterns and Legal Arguments

Research date: 2026-09-29. Status: design only; algorithms, legal-expert annotation and project experiments had not been completed. All expected benefits are hypotheses. This English rendering preserves the dated proposal. Its suggested enhanced comparators do not authorize changes to the current single-call LLM baseline, and proposed expert resources are not confirmed available resources.

## 1. Problem and research focus

Define the project as **building traceable fact representations from judgments, discovering fact combinations that recur across cases, and checking how those combinations connect to legal conditions and judicial arguments**.

The original PDF already provides an implementable canonicalization sequence: atomic extraction, evidence-backed role replacement, predicates/arguments, candidate retrieval, LLM relation judgments, conservative merging and provenance. It names FP-Growth, gSpan and ANCO-HITS. The task is to make these steps testable and comparable, not invent every component.

The initial application is cross-case comparison and knowledge discovery from judgment records, with limited issue-level argument support. Reasoning reconstruction is a separate optional task, with a different reference standard from legal defensibility. Judgment facts may already be selected or found by a court. They are not raw predecision evidence; deleting conclusion does not remove hindsight. Prospective claims need separately available predecision material and experiments.

A/B/C are modules requiring clear designs, not three mandatory novel algorithms. The main question is: **how can cases share a representation without losing legally material distinctions, and does that representation improve queries/comparisons on new cases?** Fact abstraction and reuse in A are primary; B patterns and C limited argumentation test utility. Controlled evidence determines whether complexity is worthwhile.

The candidate main contribution is a source-grounded abstraction/reuse task, benchmark and error analysis, informed by independent expert judgments. Secondary goals concern compact patterns and explicit premises for bounded rule application. These are research choices, not established publishable contributions. Section 10 retains robust-selection experiments without presuming novelty.

The closest literature already covers definition revision, query-guided review, uncertain pattern mining and robust set optimization. There is no project evidence yet that shared ambiguity is frequent or harmful enough to justify all these mechanisms. Establish representation value and failure mechanisms first.

| Module | Question | Inputs | Outputs | Primary error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: representation/canonicalization | Which vocabulary can cases share while preserving material differences? | Sourced assertions, case-local participants, events, time and status | Reusable predicates, mappings, arguments and qualifiers | False equivalence, or no reuse because everything stays separate |
| B: relational patterns | Which combinations recur in the same relationships and merit retention? | A plus case-local relations; training outcomes only for declared supervised tasks | Short bound patterns, witnesses, support and validation | Combining facts from different contracts/parties into a nonexistent situation |
| C: rules/arguments | Which legal conditions hold, with which exceptions/opposition? | Facts, candidate patterns, authorities and rule definitions | Sourced applications, support/attack chains and gaps | Association treated as law; correct formal inference from incorrect interpretation |

Authorities can directly constrain A's important distinctions and C's conditions. A necessary legal condition need not be frequent in B. Patterns suggest structures and empirical findings, not legal force.

## 2. Running example

This is a fictional contract example, not an actual jurisdiction's rule. A buyer paid under K1; a seller has not delivered under K2. Case-level flags payment=1 and nondelivery=1 would falsely match payment without delivery under the same contract. Store:

```text
PAYMENT_MADE(buyer_1, seller_1, contract_K1, payment_event_1)
DELIVERY_NOT_COMPLETED(seller_1, buyer_1, contract_K2, delivery_event_2)
```

The pattern is:

```text
PAYMENT_MADE(buyer, seller, contract, payment_event)
AND DELIVERY_NOT_COMPLETED(seller, buyer, contract, delivery_event)
```

Both contract variables must bind to one contract, so the recorded facts do not supply this witness. Applying a legal condition would additionally require the obligation, due date, exemptions and other sourced requirements, not just two facts.

“No match” means no complete same-contract witness in the records. In an open world it does not prove that no such facts exist. A stabilizes representation, B checks shared structure, and C evaluates legal significance using authorities.

## 3. Instances, predicates, clusters, families and patterns

A fact instance is a particular sourced assertion. Payments by different parties in different cases remain separate records/events. A standard predicate such as `PAYMENT_MADE(payer, payee, contract, event)` can be shared while arguments and provenance differ.

Define the clustering object. Prefer equivalent expressions/predicate definitions within a declared scope, with instances mapped to them. Resolving repeated mentions of one event within a case is a separate task. Cross-case type identity is not real-event identity.

A family is a broader concept or classification dimension: performance can include payment and delivery without making them equivalent. Families need not form a single-parent tree, and automatic induction of a large ontology is not a first-version requirement.

Semantic taxonomies also differ from legal-factor support hierarchies. The latter express support/weakening and strength, not simply subtype membership. [Bench-Capon](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10506-024-09405-x) and [Prakken and van Woerkom](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3233/FAIA251608) discuss intermediate factors and strength dimensions; collapsing them can alter precedent comparison. Do not call both hierarchies family without definitions.

A pattern combines predicates with shared participants, objects and ordering. A family organizes concepts; a pattern relates facts within cases.

A court finding and a party allegation of payment may share content predicate PAYMENT_MADE, but their complete assertions retain court_found/party_alleged. Status must enter matching and rule computation, not merely be stored. Allegation, dispute and finding should be source/time-aware dimensions: a proposition can be alleged, contested, then found.

## 4. Architecture choices

| Route | Benefit | Cost/risk | Proposed role in this dated design |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expert fact/factor schema, model extraction | Clear definitions, easier annotation/debugging | Expert effort; missed new concepts | Necessary comparator, suitable for a narrow pilot |
| Open extraction, embedding clustering, LLM merging, mining | Long-tail expression coverage | Drift, inconsistent granularity, relation loss and propagated errors | Comparator, not presumed default |
| Expert critical distinctions, model candidates, inspectable vocabulary mappings | Extensible, traceable and able to reject unreliable mappings | Requires policies, versions and review interfaces | Provisional recommendation |
| Query-local retrieval and factor/argument analysis without global canonicalization | Lower initial cost | Repeated analyses may disagree; weak corpus-wide statistics | Alternative testing whether global normalization is necessary |

If expert schema or direct retrieval achieves the same effect, additional modules need justification.

## 5. A: Representation and canonicalization

### 5.1 Define permissible abstraction

For one legal issue, experts write a short policy defining type equivalence, required qualifiers and abstention conditions. Annotation and algorithms use the same policy. Names can usually become case-local variables, but company cannot become controller, shareholder cannot become minority shareholder, and nonprovision cannot become refusal without evidence. Whether time, amount or intent matters depends on the task/legal condition.

This defines an explicit projection: dimensions that may be ignored when sharing expressions. Original information remains stored. The LLM cannot choose a different equivalence standard for every pair.

### 5.2 Interfaces

```text
SourceAssertion:
  assertion_id, case_id, claim_id_if_known, source_span
  predicate_candidate, argument_bindings, event_id_if_known
  polarity_and_quantifier_scope, speaker
  allegation_records, dispute_records, finding_records
  event_time, assertion_time, jurisdiction
  unknown_fields, extraction_version

PredicateDefinition:
  predicate_id, definition, argument_types
  required_facets, inclusion_examples, boundary_examples, exclusions
  parent_or_related_links, version

MappingDecision:
  assertion_id, predicate_id_or_unresolved
  projection_scope, preserved_facets, source_checks
  decision_label, model_or_reviewer, version

RelationDecision:
  expression_pair, legal_context
  EQUIVALENT | PARENT_CHILD | RELATED_SIBLINGS |
  CONTRADICTORY | UNRELATED | UNCERTAIN
```

Model confidence may be stored, but uncalibrated scores are not probabilities or sufficient merge evidence. Entity/contract/event IDs are case-local; cross-case type comparison uses argument structure rather than identical instance IDs.

Independently define competency questions: who did what to whom, which object, what order, who alleged it and whether the court found it. Evaluate structured-only answers separately from source-rereading answers, charging rereading cost. Otherwise a link-only system could masquerade as a complete representation.

### 5.3 First-version procedure

1. Extract atomic assertions from permitted sections and check every argument against sources. Unsupported roles/statuses remain unknown.
2. Generate candidate definitions and scopes by issue/types. Unknown is not known incompatibility; measure blocking misses.
3. Retrieve a few embedding candidates from definitions/expressions. Embeddings retrieve, not adjudicate equivalence.
4. Judge the six relations with structured context, material differences and source evidence. Parent/child, contradiction and unknown do not merge.
5. Require definition fit and member consistency. Compare all-member versus representative/boundary checks without assuming the latter has strict guarantees.
6. Add a mapping or retain a new type/unresolved item. Never delete instances or rewrite them as one synthetic fact.
7. Freeze vocabulary before validation/test/B. New types remain unknown/candidates until a new version; no test-driven changes to an evaluated version.

```text
fit_canonicalizer(training_assertions, expert_policy):
    definitions = seed_definitions(expert_policy)
    for expression in stable_order(training_assertions):
        candidates = retrieve_compatible_definitions(expression, definitions)
        decisions = judge_relations(expression, candidates, expert_policy)
        eligible = equivalent_with_no_material_conflict(decisions)
        if exactly_one_consistent_cluster(eligible, member_checks):
            record_mapping(expression, eligible.cluster)
        else:
            retain_unresolved_or_create_reviewed_definition(expression)
    audit_order_sensitivity_and_cluster_boundaries()
    return freeze(definitions, mappings, decision_log)
```

This is workflow pseudocode, not validated code. Member checks, ordering and definition creation are explicit design variables, not hidden inside “ask the LLM.”

### 5.4 Hypothesis and evaluation

Arguments/qualifiers expose distinctions that vectors can compress away. Definitions/member checks can reduce A≈B≈C chaining. Stricter checks can also reduce recall, increase review or encounter inconsistent model judgments.

Historical comparators include the full PDF process, expert schema, embedding/agglomerative clustering, structured-input direct LLM, [EDC](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.548/) and adapted [LLM-CER](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02509v1). Disclose adaptations: entity identity differs from legal fact-type identity.

Measure false merges, equivalence recall, category quality and error versus automatic coverage, with compression, expert time and retrieval recall. All-singletons/all-abstention cannot win. Match coverage, model and cost. Ablate status, arguments, time, member checks and rejection separately. Report natural samples separately from constructed hard pairs.

Separate T1 expression→predicate mapping, T2 case-local proposition/event coreference and T3 fact→legal-factor projection. Different contract IDs are a T2 distinction, not automatically a T1 negative. T3 needs its own factor definitions/applicability labels, not T1 scores.

## 6. B: Relational pattern discovery

### 6.1 Starting methods

| Method | Question | Limitation | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closed frequent itemsets / FP-Growth | Which types co-occur? | No default variable binding; frequent need not be useful | First reproducible comparator |
| Contrast/subgroup discovery, SSD++ | Which concise combinations distinguish groups? | Feature dependence and target leakage | Compare existing MDL redundancy control |
| Typed conjunctions / constrained ILP | Which variable relations/conditions hold? | Search size, bad arguments, sparsity | Relational starting point; existing methods decide whether a new searcher is needed |
| gSpan | Which local graphs recur? | Many patterns may lack legal/predictive relevance | Add when simple queries are insufficient |
| ANCO-HITS-like bipartite methods | How should two node types be grouped/ranked through signed edges? | Requires meaningful signs; not frequent-pattern mining | Not a mandatory backbone |

[Partisan Scale](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2012/proceedings/companion/p349.pdf) uses modified ANCO-HITS. The PDF's name alone does not define a legal graph/objective. Missing facts cannot automatically become negative edges.

### 6.2 Inputs and outputs

A pattern is a short typed conjunction. Shared variables mean the same participant, contract or event. Temporal predicates identify the two compared events. Store body, allowed statuses, matching cases/claims, fact witnesses, support, unknown/conflict counts, statistical/predictive measures and discovery/selection versions.

Prefer case–claim/issue units. If claim segmentation is unreliable, use a declared case-level task; do not invent a single WIN from partial relief.

### 6.3 Procedure

```text
discover_patterns(training_graphs, optional_training_labels):
    candidates = enumerate_short_typed_conjunctions(frozen_vocabulary)
    enforce_connected_variables_and_valid_argument_types(candidates)
    for pattern in candidates:
        matches = match_with_shared_bindings(training_graphs, pattern)
        count_once_per_target_unit(matches)
        retain_unknown_and_conflicting_observations()
        score_support_coverage_and_optional_contrast(pattern)
    compress_or_select_nonredundant_candidates(training_only)
    return candidates_with_witnesses

select_and_test(candidates, validation, locked_test):
    choose_complexity_and_selection_rule_on_validation()
    freeze_patterns_and_all_thresholds()
    evaluate_once_on_locked_test()
```

Begin with short, connected, typed combinations. Select length/support limits on training/validation and report cost curves instead of asserting an unmeasured optimum. Outcome labels allow incremental prediction comparisons against single facts, still only association. Without labels evaluate compression, coverage, stability and semantic correctness, not prediction.

### 6.4 Missing, negative and conflicting evidence

Distinguish positive support, explicit opposition, both and neither. Missing extracted payment is not nonpayment. Party denial and court finding cannot be pooled indiscriminately.

For existential patterns, failure to find a full witness is unknown. Refutation requires a reliably enumerated object domain and explicit counterevidence for every possible binding. An open domain cannot be closed by local counterexamples.

Match in named views, such as court findings or one party's allegations. Keep cross-view conflicts for C. Negative conditions need explicit negative evidence. Report definite matches, definite nonmatches, unknowns and conflicts, with their proportions of all units. Determinate-only denominators can bias results; add conservative counting/sensitivity analyses rather than treating all unknowns as negatives.

### 6.5 Utility tests

On identical canonical facts compare binary co-occurrence, simple same-contract/event join features and full relational patterns. Equal performance of the latter two means complex mining adds no value. Give existing transactional/subgroup methods the same relational-query features; this separates restored information from new search, as the first Pro review emphasized.

Measure binding errors, redundancy, length/coverage, resampling stability and locked-test incremental prediction where labels permit. Separate multi-contract/event cases from simple cases. Candidate multiplicity creates chance association: discovery, selection and independent confirmation must be distinct. Prespecify test families/corrections and account for case/family dependence. MDL, stability and significance are different evidence.

Fix the eligible-unit denominator. Pattern-specific determinate denominators can break decreasing-support assumptions and invalidate pruning. Initially use fixed-denominator recorded support and separately report unknowns. Keep statistical findings and rare legal exceptions in separate lists: recurrence needs confirmation; exceptions need sources/expert reasons. Low frequency does not imply low legal importance.

## 7. C: Rules, exceptions and arguments

### 7.1 Two meanings of rule

An empirical rule associates a combination with an outcome in samples. A normative rule assigns consequences under a jurisdiction/time, potentially subject to exceptions, priorities and burdens. B can suggest empirical candidates and conditions to inspect: pattern→case difference→candidate condition/exception→independent authority review. Even universal observed association does not establish legal necessity. Preserve rare exceptions.

### 7.2 Comparable routes

1. Authority RAG plus sourced structured reasons: lower construction cost, but correct citation can accompany incorrect application.
2. Factor-based analogy/argumentation: shared/distinguishing factors and opposing precedents; requires polarity, priorities and comparability definitions.
3. Rule extraction plus restricted execution: model drafts, experts confirm critical rules, then logic checks bindings/exceptions/derivations; formalization can still be wrong.

Compare the first two with a limited third route before promising complete automated legal formalization. Datalog, nonmonotonic arguments and SMT serve relational queries, defeasible rules and arithmetic respectively; include only what the task needs.

### 7.3 Rule interface and flow

```text
AuthorityRule:
  rule_id, source_document, exact_span
  jurisdiction, authority_level, effective_time, available_time, version
  antecedents, conclusion, exceptions
  priority_relations_if_supported, burden_if_explicit
  interpretation_notes, expert_review_status

ApplicationTrace:
  claim_or_issue_id, rule_id, matched_assertion_ids
  premise_statuses, exception_statuses
  supporting_arguments, opposing_arguments
  result = supported | defeated | undetermined
```

```text
apply_rules(target_case, authority_index, reviewed_rule_set):
    eligible_sources = filter_by_jurisdiction_and_time(authority_index)
    relevant_rules = retrieve_by_issue_and_fact_structure(eligible_sources)
    instantiate_rules_with_bound_case_arguments(relevant_rules)
    evaluate_premises_in_explicit_evidence_views()
    build_support_and_attack_relations_where_justified()
    apply_declared_semantics_and_priorities()
    return conclusions_with_source_to_premise_to_rule_trace()
```

Initially use a small reviewed rule set to compare natural-language application and explicit execution with identical knowledge. Do not invent burdens, defaults or priorities. Add open retrieval after the correct-authority experiment to separate retrieval failures from pattern utility. Unreviewed interpretations and review conditions remain explicitly separate from accepted-rule inference.

### 7.4 Formal guarantees

Formal tools establish properties relative to supplied facts/rules, not evidence truth, extraction fidelity or legal correctness. Compatibility is not entailment: under consistent classical logic, test F∧¬P unsatisfiable to establish F entails P; F∧P satisfiable is insufficient. Conflicting inputs need an explicit view/nonmonotonic semantics, not classical explosion. Unknown exceptions are governed by sourced rule/burden policies, never a universal unknown=false convention.

### 7.5 Closest work and evaluation

[Legal Rule Induction](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14104v1) already studies iterative induction/validation; [fact-to-law KG research](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17340v1) models reasoning paths; [Proof-Carrying Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-2289/10/9/295) combines LLMs and checkable nonmonotonic derivations. These combinations are not themselves new.

Evaluate authority retrieval, extraction, scope, premise matching, exceptions and support/rebuttal separately. Equal final accuracy can conceal different reasoning errors. [LEGIT](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.150/) separates issue coverage/correctness; specify version/inputs when adapting it.

Ablate patterns with identical authorities, independent authority verification with identical candidates, reviewed/automatic facts, and natural-language/symbolic execution with identical knowledge. Keep obsolete-authority, rare-exception and temporal challenges separate from natural tests. Correctly formalized reviewed rules/facts provide a diagnostic upper bound, not method scores.

## 8. Data and protocol

Read-only inspection of `land 1.jsonl` found 6,954 valid JSON records/unique doc_id; 6,418 ok, 497 empty, 39 error; 1,530 empty facts. URLs point to Indian Kanoon. Neither filename nor fields establish domain or section accuracy.

### 8.1 Audit

Stratify a small sample by court, year, dispute, completeness, paragraph provenance, procedural history, duplicates, multiple claims and outcomes. Then choose one issue with sufficient sources and feasible expert annotation. Obtain cited authorities and versions; determine whether a citation was asserted, accepted, distinguished or rejected. prec_* fields are leads, not gold.

### 8.2 Split before learning

Group related judgments by dispute before train/validation/test, with temporal extrapolation if appropriate. Keep appeals/lower decisions together. Authority availability follows the task; prospective analysis permits only sources available at the target time.

| Object | Training | Validation | Locked test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary, definitions, invented predicates, prompt examples | Learn/adjust | Select design | Frozen mappings; count unknowns |
| Merge thresholds, abstention, complexity | Fit | Tune | No changes |
| Patterns/outcome associations | Discover | Select | Evaluate only |
| Target reasoning, conclusion, actual citations | Task-defined | Same input policy | Forbidden in prediction; role declared in reconstruction |
| Outcomes | Only declared supervised modules | Evaluate/select | Final evaluation |

The PDF's canonicalization is outcome-blind. Outcome-driven vocabulary changes would define another supervised method trained only on training data. Citation edges do not imply same-dispute membership: unrelated cases sharing a precedent need not be grouped together. For continual learning, score before feedback; a revised version cannot rewrite earlier scores.

### 8.3 Annotation proposal

Start with two-person small-sample annotation/disagreement discussion before scaling. Planning figures are 30–50 cases for audit, about 300 expression pairs, and a small set of pattern witnesses/rule chains; these are budget starting points, not power guarantees or completed annotations.

Mix natural retrieved pairs and constructed distinctions in roles, negation, status, time, hierarchy and relations. Different case IDs alone do not make types inequivalent; event identity is separate. The original proposal calls for some legal-domain review with expertise, blinding, adjudication and time recorded. LLM self-evaluation can screen but is not independent human legal gold. Availability of such experts must be verified before depending on this extension.

### 8.4 Cost and fairness

Match models, sources, authorities, corpora, splits and expert budgets. Do not give the method full reasoning while a comparator sees only facts. Record input/output tokens, calls, actual cost, latency, candidate size, human minutes and caching. Estimate scaling from a measured small batch, not an invented total price. Pairwise comparisons grow quadratically; report retrieval savings and missed candidates separately.

## 9. Falsifiable hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Required evidence | Rejection/simplification |
|---|---|---|
| H1: material qualifiers improve canonicalization | Fewer false merges at equal coverage/budget without eliminating equivalence recall | Gains only from abstention/tokens, or structured direct LLM matches them |
| H2: binding improves pattern reliability/replication | Fewer false multi-contract/event matches and independent gains beyond simple joins | Relational ambiguity is rare or complex mining matches simple features |
| H3: A gains reach B/C | Fixed downstream modules improve witness/premise matching reproducibly after changing A | Better clustering without downstream gain, or extraction/legal interpretation dominates |

[Which Changes Matter / LexGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26530v1) already studies sensitivity to material changes and invariance to irrelevant changes. The potential question is more specific: which normalization errors corrupt which patterns/applications, and can fixed-budget repair prevent propagation? Binding, provenance and uncertainty are established ideas. Revise the paper claim if existing work already solves the task; do not manufacture novelty.

## 10. Three testable extensions

Pro branches differ. This proposal prioritizes independent evidence that A preserves distinctions. B set decisions remain worth comparing, with ordinary bounds and established redundancy control as defaults and joint robust optimization as a variant. C is a bounded application test. These designs are not three presumed innovations.

### A: Counterexample-driven definition revision

Maintain positive examples, boundaries and confirmed counterexamples for each definition. From training candidates select embedding-similar but semantically conflicting pairs. Ask experts/calibrated validators for the minimal difference, add source-supported qualifiers/parameters and recheck affected mappings, preserving versions.

Compare against random and low-confidence review at identical human budgets. If richer definitions explain gains, give comparators those definitions. [ClassMATe](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.55/) already requests uncertain-instance labels and revises definitions in a fixed-label task. Novelty requires a specific error model, selection criterion and legal-data evidence beyond difficult examples plus revision.

### B: Support bounds under joint interpretations

Retain several unexcluded mappings and constraints instead of treating the highest-scored mapping as true. Compute conservative/possible recorded support. Per-unit all/any interpretation bounds may be loose when units share a semantic decision; globally consistent worlds can tighten total support.

Bounds apply only to fixed extracted material and enumerated interpretations, not unseen real-world facts or legal-outcome probabilities. If the correct interpretation is missing, the interval may miss truth; measure candidate recall separately. Shared identity/type decisions must remain consistent rather than multiplying arbitrary LLM confidences. Start with small ambiguity sets and exact enumeration/constraint solving.

The hypothesis is that a few bad mappings can create spurious strong patterns; the risk is intervals so wide that all useful patterns disappear. Compare top mapping, threshold abstention, random review and reviewed-input upper bounds, with existing query-guided review using the same selector as the serious novelty comparison. [Mining Uncertain Data with Probabilistic Guarantees](https://cs.hku.hk/~ckcheng/papers/kdd10-mining.pdf) already studies possible-world patterns. Any increment must concern dependent semantic mappings, untrusted probabilities and budgeted review, not uncertainty propagation itself.

Let Z be finite interpretations satisfying confirmed constraints, and y(u,P,z)=1 when unit u has a complete admissible recorded witness for P in z. Zero means no contribution to recorded support, not worldly falsity.

```text
L(P) = min over z in Z of sum_u y(u,P,z)
U(P) = max over z in Z of sum_u y(u,P,z)

L(P) >= tau          : recorded support crosses the threshold in every interpretation
L(P) < tau <= U(P)   : crossing depends on unresolved interpretations
U(P) < tau          : current material and candidates cannot reach this support count
Z is empty          : constraints or candidates are invalid; return INVALID
```

Tau is a prespecified recorded-support threshold, not a legal threshold. Use SAT/CSP/MILP or enumeration. A timeout permits only proven outer bounds and an incomplete status, not exact extrema inferred from incumbents.

Example: one shared binary interpretation makes P hold in unit1 or unit2, exclusively. Total support is always one: exact [1,1] versus independent per-unit [0,2]. If decisions are actually independent, decompose rather than invent shared variables. Compare top mapping, local bounds and constrained global bounds with fixed short patterns. Measure candidate recall, constraint accuracy, solver cost, semantic errors and held-out recurrence; tighter is not automatically more correct.

[CPClean](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.05117), [Query-Guided Resolution](https://u.cs.biu.ac.il/~amstery/files/drien2023query.pdf) and [MESReduce](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08612v2) already address downstream uncertainty/review. Query-Guided Resolution permits unknown/correlated probabilities, so independence is not a valid invented contrast. The possible increment is whether real legal mapping errors and complementary pattern sets require another budget strategy.

Ranking ambiguities by affected threshold-near patterns divided by cost is only a heuristic. Review may remain unresolved or find all candidates wrong. Record OUT_OF_SET, withdraw affected guarantees and count failures in fixed-candidate experiments; do not silently expand candidates or drop cases. Candidate expansion creates a new version. Including unanswerable outcomes in minimax can tie all actions; no guaranteed gain follows.

#### Joint selection of complementary patterns

The actual decision is which k patterns help users compare cases. An ambiguity can change many individual scores without changing the best displayed set, or hide the only pattern covering an important distinction.

Freeze candidates P, worlds Z, reading cap k and task. Units are `(case_id, claim_id, procedural_stage)`; related-dispute groups control splits/dependence, not merged judgment units. Use declared case units where claims cannot be separated.

Define development comparison tasks identifying two cases, an issue and an evidence-backed important difference. Explicit opposite responses can form a contrast; refusal versus silence cannot. Bind objects explicitly; no-witness open-domain existentials are not refuted.

Let d(t,p,z)=1 when p answers task t through source witnesses and explicit difference evidence under z and the frozen relevance policy. Unknown/conflict/missingness do not score. All methods share that policy and prespecified task weights, initially equal.

```text
F(S,z) = sum_t w(t) * I[at least one p in S satisfies d(t,p,z)=1]
         - lambda * sum_(p in S) complexity(p)

S* = argmax over S subset of P, |S| <= k of min_(z in Z) F(S,z)
```

Complexity is declared length/reading burden. A task counts once regardless of redundant patterns, rewarding complementarity. Broad patterns that cover many cases but answer no task gain nothing in the first term. This is a task-utility proxy, not legal correctness; independent task quality/time remain necessary. Compression/coverage objectives define separate descriptive experiments.

[Robust Submodular Observation Selection](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v9/krause08b.html) already optimizes worst-case set utility under budget. Max–min structure/complementarity are not new. Its guarantees have function/budget conditions and do not automatically survive penalties/changed constraints.

Use tiny exhaustive references first, then MILP or scenario generation: optimize on some worlds, find the chosen set's worst admissible world, add it and repeat. The subset-world master optimum is an upper bound; a complete worst-case evaluation of the current feasible set is a lower bound. Claim convergence only with the required bound gap and solver evidence. Greedy/beam approaches have no automatic approximation guarantee here.

For illustration, p2/p3 swap which of two tasks they answer across two worlds. Individually unstable, together they always cover both. Resolving each output could waste review. This computational example does not establish real-data superiority over query-guided methods.

#### Review ambiguities that can change the selection

Initially experts answer local questions over existing candidates while extractor, candidate generator and definitions are frozen for attribution. Log definition changes separately from instance corrections. Propagate only genuinely shared decisions, not one case label across similar sentences.

For current S, simulate answer v to ambiguity d, retain Z(d=v), reoptimize, and measure continuing with S versus selecting again:

```text
regret(d,v) = max_T min_(z in Z(d=v)) F(T,z)
              - min_(z in Z(d=v)) F(S,z)

score(d) = answerability(type(d))
           * max_(valid candidate answers v) regret(d,v)
           / estimated_expert_minutes(d)
```

Answerability is an independently estimated proportion of questions experts can resolve, not LLM confidence. The score favors a consequential possible branch; it is neither expected value of information nor a worst-case guarantee. Without a reliable estimate, use 1 initially and separately report unresolved answers and actual time.

```text
Freeze P, initial Z, task specification and total budget
while budget remains:
    if Z is empty: record INVALID and stop affected computation
    compute the current pattern set S and solver bounds
    estimate branch effects and actual review costs for each ambiguity
    select a question under the current policy; show sources and allowed answers
    if an expert confirms a candidate:
        update only evidence-supported variables and dependencies
    elif the expert cannot decide:
        retain the interpretation set; record time and unresolved status
    elif the expert rejects every candidate:
        record OUT_OF_SET, revoke affected guarantees, retain the failure
    recompute with the shared set selector
Return patterns, witnesses, unresolved items, candidate failures and all costs
```

Unaffected work may continue after OUT_OF_SET, but affected failures enter coverage/error totals. A secondary candidate-expansion experiment must give every comparator equal correction opportunities/cost.

Compare random, least-confidence, query-output impact, explicit Query-Guided Resolution/MESReduce adaptations and set-decision impact. Query-output impact concerns individual matching/support uncertainty; using selected-set changes would already make it decision-aware. Share candidates, questions, answers, information and final selector. Report results by both question count and actual expert minutes.

Independently audit out-of-set truth, wrong constraints and extraction errors. Exact finite-world solving does not remove them. Source audits, candidate coverage, independent confirmation and terminal tasks complement conditional proofs.

### C: Allocate verification to legal-premise gaps

Expose evidence, missing premises and conflicts for every condition. Spend limited human/model review on unsupported conditions likely to change application status. A controlling exception may matter more than an auxiliary premise, but prioritization cannot fabricate its truth; unavailable evidence leaves the conclusion unresolved.

Compare fixed-order review, uniform repeated reflection and direct RAG at equal verification budgets: do complete correct application chains increase? More human work or avoiding hard cases is not an algorithmic gain. Verification/repair, proof obligations and active acquisition already exist; any contribution needs a specific selection objective and experiment.

Study these extensions separately. First test A, then ordinary versus robust B with matched information/budget. C budget allocation depends on reliable rules and expert evaluation and is not the first primary innovation.

## 11. Small feasibility experiment

Measure annotation feasibility/time under Section8 before scaling. Proposed start: about 90 dispute groups, split 30 development/30 discovery/30 confirmation, with temporal care. Initially one issue-limited document per group; count extra documents/cost separately. Freeze A after development and patterns/selection after discovery. Confirmation only evaluates; independent query references include unseen combinations. These are unsampled feasibility figures, not power guarantees. A separate B proposal suggested 200 units as a later extension, not an additive current commitment.

Choose one issue and at most about 40 short candidate patterns; freeze sources, vocabulary and interpretations. The terminal task compares important commonalities, differences and counterexamples without uncleaned WIN/LOSE. Refind witnesses on confirmation material without retuning.

Run stages rather than every Cartesian combination. Isolate B using reviewed representations, compare automatic A with one fixed B, then connect C if B is useful.

Primary measurements on unseen cases/query combinations are important-distinction recall, erroneous assertions, provenance correctness, coverage and expert time, versus source/local methods. B extensions measure independently confirmed nonredundant useful patterns per fixed expert time under error/coverage constraints. Also report valid-pattern recall, wrongful exclusions, unresolved/conflict, OUT_OF_SET, solver failures and all costs. The reference set is relative to frozen candidates/review policy, not all true patterns in the world.

Blind terminal evaluation measures important differences, omissions, citations and completion time. Control UI/input and counterbalance order; avoid having one reviewer repeat the same pair across conditions and gain memory advantages. Audit automated/human scoring differences. Development F is not terminal utility evidence.

Separate real shared ambiguity, independent local ambiguity, deleted correct candidates, wrong constraints, unanswerable questions, simple cases and multi-event cases. Synthetic perturbations diagnose mechanics, not natural-error effectiveness. Restrict claims if benefits exist only on engineered shared errors.

The final Pro planning example totals roughly 78 expert hours: 90 documents×2 people×12 minutes=36h; calibration/adjudication15h; 12 comparisons×4 outputs×2 people×12 minutes≈19h; live interaction8h. Twelve minutes per document is unmeasured; long/multiple documents and complex authorities may cost much more. This is neither an expert commitment nor a quote.

Compute cost as `sample count × independent reviewers × measured mean minutes / 60`, plus adjudication, guidelines, candidate/rule review and terminal evaluation. A simulated oracle must disclose the cost of building its answer bank; a small live interaction should validate difficulty/time. Question counts alone omit substantial work.

Simplify or change claims if candidate/constraint failures exclude truth; existing query-guided review with the same selector matches performance; robust methods only abstain more or improve internal F; existing miners on identical relational features suffice; or patterns do not improve terminal comparison. In the latter case retain auditable representation/error-propagation findings and reduce B/C application claims.

## 12. Implementation and stopping order

1. Audit domain, input time, unit, outcomes and expert policy; deliver frozen split/audit. Unsupported prospective claims become retrospective discovery/reconstruction.
2. Reproduce PDF/EDC and expert schema on a small sample, measuring merges/recall/cost. Resolve annotation-policy disagreements first.
3. Compare itemsets→simple binding→bounded patterns, initially with established compact selection. Expand robust selection/review only if natural ambiguity changes important outputs.
4. Test a few formalizable conditions/exceptions in C across retrieval, analogy and executable rules, separating fact/rule/inference errors.
5. Freeze and test H1–H3 before expanding toward the suggested 1,000 cases. Scale does not replace reference quality/task definition.

A first paper need not claim novelty in eight stages. Require a clear recurring error, an unmet need beyond established comparators, measurable gains and replication in actual legal material.

## 13. Proposal argument

Study cross-case fact abstraction for a bounded legal issue: which content can share canonical types, and which bindings, objects, times and statuses must remain to support reuse? Build a task from actual judgment sources and independent judgments; compare source retrieval, local comparison, expert schema, existing normalization and qualifier-preserving hybrid representations at equal information/model/budget. Locate extraction, abstraction and pattern errors and test their effect on unseen-case relational queries/comparisons. Compact patterns and bounded rule application are ablatable use cases; robust selection under shared ambiguity is a contingent extension, not presumed novel or necessary.

The evidence should identify distinctions that cannot be compressed, errors that manufacture downstream findings, and repairs that still help at matched coverage/cost. If existing or source-direct methods suffice, state that global normalization is unnecessary in that scope. Only a stable natural failure mechanism justifies a targeted new algorithm.
