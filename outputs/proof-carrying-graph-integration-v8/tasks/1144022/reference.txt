[IK-1144022:L237] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] In cite40†Krishna Ram Mahate (dead) by L.Rs. v, Mrs. Shobha Venkat Rao JT 1989 (3) SC 489 : (1989)4 SCC 131, the facts were that the respondent was inducted into' possession under an agreement to conduct business in a restaurant. On an attempted issue of notice of terminating the agreement, the respondent was dispossessed surreptitiously and a suit was laid by her for an order of injunction and for restoration of possession under cite11†Section 6 of the Act which was granted.
[IK-1144022:L238] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] The appeal was filed to this Court by the defendant/appellant. While deprecating the conduct of the owner, following the ratio in Lallu Yeshwant Singh and Ram Ratan's cases this court held that the occupant in possession cannot be dispossessed without recourse to law.
[IK-1144022:L239] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] When attempted to argue that by the date of the hearing" of the appeal the period of licence had expired, this Court discountenanced the contention and directed a receiver to lake over possession and the respondent was directed to be given possession of the restaurant.
[IK-1144022:L240] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] 45. Law respects possession even if there is no title to support it. No-one is permitted to take law in one's own hands and to dispossess the person in actual settled possession without due course of law. No person can be allowed to become a judge in his own case. The object of cite11†Section 6 is to discourage people to act in self help, however, good their title may be.
[IK-1144022:L241] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] The licensee in possession for well over 15 years is in settled possession and is entitled to remain in possession and make use of the premises for the purpose for which it was demised until it is ejected in due course of law. The acquiescence of the landlord in this context would be to the initial unlawful entry into possession and continuation thereafter but not to the continuance in possession of the licencee after the expiry or termination of the licence.
[IK-1144022:L242] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] That was what this court appears to have meant in the previous decisions. Take for instance that when a licence was granted for a couple of years and after its expiry, by efflux of time, or on termination, if the possession of the licensee, though unlawful and unjust is not protected, the aggressor or mighty would trample upon the rights of the weak and meek and denial of relief under cite11†Section 6 would put a premium upon the aggression or treachery or tricks.
[IK-1144022:L243] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] No doubt long delay in disposal of cases due to docket explosion became a ruse to unscrupulous litigant to abuse the due course of law to protract litigation and remain in unjust or wrongful possession of the property. Landlord could be suitably compensated by award of damages. It cannot, by any stretch of imagination, be said that a person in settled possession, though unlawful, is not entitled to the protection under cite11†Section 6 of the Act.
[IK-1144022:L244] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] Maintenance of law and order, and enthusing confidence in the efficacy of rule of law are condition precedent for orderly society. Therefore, giving primacy, legitimacy or legality to the conduct or acts of the landlord to take possession of the property in derogation of the due course of law would be deleterious to rule of law and a pat on high-handedness or self-help.
[IK-1144022:L245] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] 46. In Amirudin's case the facts were that Amirudin, plaintiff filed the suit to recover a room from the defendant when he was dispossessed on June 3, 1890. The defendant pleaded that he had been in possession and that the plaintiff trespassed into it by excluding him from the room and put a padlock on the door. He had removed the padlock and resumed possession.
[IK-1144022:L246] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] The plea, therefore, was that the plaintiff was a rank trespasser and the defendant resumed possession in his assertion as an owner, and as such, the plaintiff was not entitled to relief under cite14†Section 9 of the old Act. The trial court accepted that plea and held that the plaintiff was a trespasser. Under those circumstances, the court held that he was not entitled to possession. That ratio has no application to the facts.
[IK-1144022:L247] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] In cite24†Chandu Lal 's, case no doubt the ratio therein is in favour of the appellant. But it is difficult to approve of it. The licensee in settled possession is entitled to the remedy under cite11†Section 6 . But each case has to be considered in the light of its own facts.
[IK-1144022:L248] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] 47. In cite21†Maganlal Radia 's case the Bombay High Court held that a licensee whose licence has been terminated, even though he may have actual possession of the properly, is not protected by law to avail himself of the remedy of cite11†Section 6 . In that case he was evicted by a summary procedure, i.e. in due course of law. So the remedy under cite11†Section 6 was not available.
[IK-1144022:L249] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] That was not a suit under cite11†Section 6 but a Writ Petition under cite32†Article 226. This ratio renders little assistance to the appellant. In cite25†Harshad kumar Sunderlal Dalai 's case the facts were that access to the room to the licensee was only through the court yard of the landlord. On termination of the licence the landlord closed the door from inside and did not allow the petitioner to get into possession.
[IK-1144022:L250] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] When a suit under cite14†Section 9 was laid for possession, it was held that a trespasser thrown out from possession cannot claim possession against the true owner under cite14†Section 9 of the old Act. For the aforesaid reasons, the ratio is not good law.
[IK-1144022:L251] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] 48. In cite22†Puran Singh Sahni 's case the question therein was the distinction between licence and lease vis-a-vis the power of the Tribunal under cite41†Section 91 of the Co-op. Societies Act. It is also of no avail to the appellant. Thus I hold that the respondent, after expiry of the licence or its termination, was in settled possession, and its dispossession was otherwise than in due course of law. Therefore, the decree for possession under cite11†Section 6 of the Act is not illegal.
[IK-1144022:L252] [TARGET / JUDGMENT_TEXT] Remedy of judicial review under cite17†Article 136 of the Constitution is plenary and is not hedged with the bar of appeal or review prescribed by cite11†Section 6 of the Act. The contention of the counsel for the respondent that this court would not review the decision of the High Court is ex facie desperate and is rejected.
