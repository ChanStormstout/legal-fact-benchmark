## 摘要

Supreme court recounted fact and procedural history (Overall tenancy, standard-rent and possession litigation): The principal dispute concerns O. P. Kathpalia's tenancy and continued possession of the cottage attached to the bungalow at No. 1, Hailey Road, New Delhi. E. B. Brook originally leased the bungalow and inducted Kathpalia as a subtenant of the cottage. After Brook surrendered his tenancy, the original landlord, Lakhmir Singh, accepted Kathpalia as his direct tenant effective May 1, 1955. Lakhmir Singh subsequently transferred the entire property to respondents Nos. 2 and 3, who became the real contesting respondents. The litigation concerned the timeliness and determination of standard rent, the landlord's claim for arrears and possession, the validity of an alleged future-rent deposit direction, and the tenant's right to defend the possession suit. Supreme court operative disposition with unresolved merits (Final appellate orders and remand scope): The Supreme Court allowed Civil Appeal No. 464 of 1971 and remitted the landlord's appeal against the trial court's standard-rent determination to the District Judge, Delhi, for consideration of the proper amount. Separately, it allowed Civil Appeals Nos. 465 and 466 of 1971 and remitted the proceedings concerning interim rent, rent recovery and possession to the trial court for further adjudication on their merits, with Kathpalia's defence restored. The two remands have different receiving courts and different issues. Neither remand constitutes a final determination of the standard-rent amount, an adjudication that all future rent was paid, or a final decision awarding permanent possession to either side.

## 依据

ADMITTED_POSITION_RECOGNIZED_BY_SUPREME_COURT; Tenancy formation and classification for Section 11(b) [S02]

Brook inducted Kathpalia as a subtenant of the cottage on September 1, 1954, after commencement of the Delhi and Ajmer Rent Control Act, 1952. Brook surrendered his head tenancy on April 1, 1955, but Kathpalia remained in possession. By an agreement reached in June 1955, Lakhmir Singh accepted Kathpalia as a direct tenant effective May 1, 1955. The Supreme Court treated this subsequent direct tenancy as the relevant letting for the limitation dispute.

SUPREME_COURT_ADOPTED_LEGAL_INTERPRETATION; Supreme Court review of Section 11(b) limitation [S03]

Under Section 11(b), as construed and adopted by the Supreme Court, the six-month period for a standard-rent application concerning premises let after commencement of the Act runs from the commencement of the relevant letting to the particular applicant tenant. It does not necessarily run from the first-ever letting of the premises or from an earlier subtenancy that preceded recognition of the applicant's direct tenancy. The Court reasoned that the contrary construction would prevent subsequent tenants from challenging unreasonable rent and frustrate the protective purpose of the legislation.

SUPREME_COURT_ADOPTED_INTERPRETIVE_REASONING_AND_EVIDENTIARY_ASSESSMENT; Classification of post-Act subtenancy and direct tenancy [S04]

The Supreme Court distinguished Kathpalia's earlier post-Act subtenancy from his subsequently recognized direct tenancy. It observed that the record contained no evidence establishing that Lakhmir Singh had given written consent to Brook's induction of Kathpalia as subtenant. In discussing Sections 2(j) and 13(1)(b), the Court explained the statutory significance of unauthorized post-Act subletting, but did not finally adjudicate a separate eviction claim based on unlawful subletting. The absence of evidence of written consent is an evidentiary assessment, not independent proof that consent could never have existed.

SUPREME_COURT_ADOPTED_LIMITATION_HOLDING_WITH_SOURCE_DATE_DISCREPANCY; Section 8 application and Supreme Court limitation review [S05]

Kathpalia filed his Section 8 application for determination of standard rent in October 1955. The judgment records October 10, 1955 in its factual chronology but also reproduces October 1, 1955 when describing the trial court's reasoning. Both stated dates fall within six calendar months of the direct tenancy's effective commencement on May 1, 1955. The Supreme Court expressly held that the application was within time. This statement reports the Court's adopted conclusion; the conflicting filing-date references are not resolved into a single verified date.

TRIAL_COURT_FINDING_AND_SUPREME_COURT_ADOPTED_REMAND_DISPOSITION; Standard-rent determination, Civil Appeal No. 464 of 1971 [S07]

On August 4, 1960, the trial court held Kathpalia's standard-rent application timely and fixed standard rent at Rs. 855 per annum. The District Judge allowed the landlord-side appeal on limitation grounds without deciding the proper amount, and the High Court upheld that limitation approach. Because the amount had not been examined by those appellate courts, the Supreme Court allowed Civil Appeal No. 464 of 1971, set aside the adverse limitation decisions, and remitted the landlord's appeal to the District Judge for determination of the proper standard rent. The Supreme Court did not finally fix or affirm Rs. 855 per annum as the operative standard rent.

SUPREME_COURT_ADOPTED_STATUTORY_INTERPRETATION_AND_PROCEDURAL_STANDARD; Supreme Court review of eviction protection and interim rent [S11]

The Supreme Court adopted the statutory reasoning that Section 13 protects a tenant against recovery of possession unless an applicable statutory enabling ground is established. Section 4 prevents recovery of rent exceeding standard rent or a lawful increase. Where a standard-rent petition remains pending during an eviction suit based on nonpayment, the trial court should determine standard rent as a preliminary issue or, if that is not practicable, determine interim rent under Section 9 and specify appropriate payment dates and future deposit requirements. An eviction consequence cannot properly rest merely on alleged noncompliance before the required valid determination and directions have been made.

Because the purported future-rent direction was a legal nullity, the Supreme Court held that failure to comply with it could not justify striking off Kathpalia's defence under Section 13(5). ## 相反观点与处理

REPORTED_LOWER_COURT_REASONING_EXPRESSLY_REJECTED_BY_SUPREME_COURT; District Judge appeal, High Court revision and Supreme Court limitation review [S06]

The District Judge rejected Kathpalia's standard-rent application as time-barred by treating his September 1, 1954 induction as Brook's subtenant, or the earlier letting of the premises, as controlling the limitation period despite his subsequent direct tenancy effective May 1, 1955. The Punjab High Court substantially adopted that interpretation. The Supreme Court expressly rejected their construction of Section 11(b), holding that limitation must be assessed by reference to the relevant letting to the applicant tenant. The lower-court construction is reported solely as a rejected contrary position and is not an operative legal premise.

## 未决与裁判范围

The judgment contains inconsistent statements about the monthly contractual rent. Its initial account reports Rs. 277.50 per month as stated in the plaint, while its later account of the direct tenancy reports Rs. 269.50 per month and a payment of Rs. 539 for May and June 1955. The earlier purported future-rent order is described using Rs. 277.50. These statements should not be silently reconciled into a single verified contractual amount. The Rs. 855 annual standard rent was fixed at trial level but remained subject to the remitted landlord appeal.

The judgment expressly identifies October 10, 1955 as the filing date of Kathpalia's standard-rent application in its factual chronology, but reproduces October 1, 1955 in passages describing the trial court's and District Judge's limitation reasoning. The source does not resolve this discrepancy. The Supreme Court's conclusion that the application was timely is a reported judicial holding; the statement pack does not replace the conflicting date references with an independently verified single filing date.

Lakhmir Singh died on April 9, 1978 after having transferred the property to respondents Nos. 2 and 3 in December 1959. In the substitution proceedings, Kathpalia asserted that he first learned of the death through a letter delivered to the Supreme Court Registry on March 2, 1984; the respondents contested that assertion. The judgment also gives inconsistent years for the substitution application, referring in one passage to March 21, 1983 and elsewhere to March 21, 1984. The Supreme Court found sufficient cumulative reasons to condone delay, set aside abatement and permit substitution, but it did not independently establish an undisputed first-knowledge date or reconcile the conflicting filing years.

The Supreme Court did not finally decide the correct standard-rent amount, the appropriate interim-rent amount, whether subsequently required deposits would be made, or the substantive entitlement to recover possession of the cottage. The historically established deposit of claimed arrears is separate from the future conditional payment duties stated in the remand directions and the analogous precedent. The source-supported judicial holdings and procedural outcomes do not convert unexecuted or UNKNOWN conditional-rule results into TRUE factual premises. Further adjudication remained necessary in the respective remand courts.
