Supreme court adopted evaluation (Supreme Court Majority Evaluation): The fact that a landlord had knowledge that a person other than a tenant was in possession did not by itself always lead to an inference that the landlord had knowledge that the person in possession was a sub-tenant. Agreed supreme court holding (Supreme Court Majority Holding): The Supreme Court majority agreed that the High Court was right in its view that there was no evidence in the case to support the finding of acquiescence. Supreme court adopted evaluation (Supreme Court Majority Evaluation): If a Court had arrived at a finding without any evidence to support it, it can be legitimately said that it had not decided the case 'according to law' within the meaning of section 35. Majority legal interpretation (Supreme Court Majority Interpretation): The dispute concerns tenant eviction from premises to which section 54 does not apply, bringing the first proviso's direction to have regard to the 1958 Act into the statutory analysis. Majority legal interpretation (Supreme Court Majority Interpretation): Replacing the saved section 13 subletting ground with the materially different 1958 provisions would create or apply different substantive rights and liabilities rather than merely clarify or slightly modify the previous provisions.
