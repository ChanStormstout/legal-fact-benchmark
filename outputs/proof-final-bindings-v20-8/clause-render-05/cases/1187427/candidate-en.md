Court recorded notice (substantive statutory eligibility): A general or special order of the Government requires the applicant to vacate the allotted accommodation. Court recorded fact (appellate statutory interpretation): The applicant retired from Government service before the Government notice was served and before the section 14A eviction petition was filed. Supreme court adopted legal reasoning (appellate statutory interpretation): As the Supreme Court construed it, section 14A's word 'person' is not confined to a serving Government servant, and retirement alone does not extinguish eligibility. Supreme court analysis of contrary material (appellate statutory interpretation): The Government circular and the individual notice provide a narrower Government-servant-focused policy account, which the Supreme Court considered but did not adopt as a statutory restriction. Supreme court treatment of reported precedent (appellate statutory interpretation): The reported Nihal Chand decision did not resolve the present question of notice served after retirement, because its relevant notice had preceded retirement. Court recorded fact (appellate interpretation of mixed-use tenancy): The tenancy concerning the identified premises permits or includes both residential and professional purposes. Supreme court adopted legal application (appellate interpretation of mixed-use tenancy): The identified premises are residential accommodation in their established intended character, notwithstanding permission for professional use. Supreme court adopted legal application (appellate interpretation of mixed-use tenancy): The Supreme Court's construction of section 14A does not require that the residential accommodation be let exclusively for residential purposes. Reported precedent (comparison of substantive eviction grounds): The judgment reports earlier decisions treating authorized substantial mixed residential and professional or commercial letting as outside the residential-only eviction ground considered in those authorities.
