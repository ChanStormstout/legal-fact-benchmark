    JUDGMENT:

­

1. The present petition is an eviction petition filed on the grounds u/s 14 (1) (b) and (f) of Delhi Rent Control Act against the respondent in respect of one godown on the ground floor of the suit property bearing no. 2378­82, Chasma Building, Ballimaran, Delhi ­ 6.

2. Brief facts, as stated in the petition, are that the petitioner is the owner and landlady of the suit property and same has been let out to M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. @ Rs.240/­ p.m. but M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. is later on claimed to have been merged and was taken over by the present respondent bank and with this merger/acquisition the existence of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. has been brought to an end which amounts to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises without the consent of the petitioner. It is stated that the suit property has been lying locked for the last about 14 years and due to its closure for a long time, substantial damage has been caused or permitted to have been caused to the suit property. It is further stated that the suit property has been rendered collapsible any moment and is an alive danger for the other occupants of this property as well passers by and therefore immediate repairs to the premises is required which is not possible unless vacant possession thereof is handed over to the petitioner. It has been prayed that an eviction order may be passed in favour of the petitioner and against the respondent in respect of suit premises.

12. Admittedly, M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. was the tenant of the petitioner and the same has been merged with the Canara Bank by virtue of a scheme prepared by the Reserve Bank of India for amalgamation of Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank in exercise of powers conferred under sub­ section (4) of Section 45 of Banking Regulation Act 1949 .

13. Now, it has to be seen whether the aforesaid amalgamation/merger of the M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank amounts to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises. The respondent has proved the notification issued by the Govt. of India, Ministry of Finance, Department of Economic Affairs (Banking Division), New Delhi, dated 23.08.1985 as Ex. RW­1/1 and as per notification, RBI in exercise of powers conferred under sub section (4) of Section 45 of Banking Regulation Act 1949 has prepared a scheme for amalgamation of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank and in exercise of of powers conferred under Section 45 (7) of the said Act, the Central Government has sanctioned the scheme of amalgamation as per terms and conditions mentioned in the notification Ex. Asha Rohtagi & Ors. Vs Erstwhile New Bank of India】 through General Manager PNB" . As discussed herein above, it was held by the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi that amalgamation is an act of legislation and same does not amount to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the demised premises. In the present case also, the tenancy rights have been conferred on the Canara Bank by statute namely clause 2 of the notification dated 23.08.1985 promulgated by Central Government under Sub Section (4) of Section 45 of the Banking Regulation Act 1949. A perusal of clause 2 of notification Ex. RW­1/1 shows that all rights, powers, claims, demands, interests, authorities, privileges, benefits, assets and properties of the transferor bank, movable and immovable, including premises subject to all incidents of tenure and to the rent and other sums of money and covenants reserved by or contained in the leases or agreements under which they are held stand transferred to the transferee bank. Thus the tenancy rights of the M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. have been conferred to Canara Bank by clause 2 of notification dated 23.08.1985 issued under Sub Section (4) of Section 45 of the Banking Regulation Act 1949 and it does not amount to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises.

17. In view of aforesaid discussions and categorical law laid down by the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi in "Mrs. Asha Rohtagi & Ors. Vs Erstwhile New Bank of India through General Manager PNB", in the present case also the acquisition/merger of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with Canara Bank does not ­ 12 ­ amount to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises as the merger/acquisition has been done under the legislative scheme. So far the contention of the Ld. Counsel for the petitioner that the management of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. and management of Canara Bank are different and therefore the law laid down by Hon'b le High Court of Delhi is not applicable in the present case, is without any merit. It makes hardly any difference that management of two banks is not the same as the scheme by virtue of which M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. has been merged into the Canara Bank is legislative in nature and therefore in view of law laid down by the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi it does not amount to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises.
