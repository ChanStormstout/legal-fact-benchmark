L76:     JUDGMENT:
L77: ­
L78: 
L79: 1. The present petition is an eviction petition filed on the grounds u/s 14 (1) (b) and (f) of cite8†Delhi Rent Control Act against the respondent in respect of one godown on the ground floor of the suit property bearing no. 2378­82, Chasma Building, Ballimaran, Delhi ­ 6.
L80: 2. Brief facts, as stated in the petition, are that the petitioner is the owner and landlady of the suit property and same has been let out to M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. @ Rs.240/­ p.m. but M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. is later on claimed to have been merged and was taken over by the present respondent bank and with this merger/acquisition the existence of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L81: has been brought to an end which amounts to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises without the consent of the petitioner. It is stated that the suit property has been lying locked for the last about 14 years and due to its closure for a long time, substantial damage has been caused or permitted to have been caused to the suit property.
L82: It is further stated that the suit property has been rendered collapsible any moment and is an alive danger for the other occupants of this property as well passers by and therefore immediate repairs to the premises is required which is not possible unless vacant possession thereof is handed over to the petitioner. It has been prayed that an eviction order may be passed in favour of the petitioner and against the respondent in respect of suit premises.
L83: 3. The respondent bank has contested the present petition ­ 3 ­ and filed the written statement in which it has been submitted that in exercise of powers conferred under sub section (4) of cite9†Section 45 of the Banking Regulation Act 1949, Reserve Bank of India prepared a scheme for amalgamation of the Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L84: with Canara Bank and the said scheme sanctioned by the Central Government under cite10†Section 45 (7) of the said Act and was notified in the gazette of India dated 23.08.1985 and the said amalgamation is not a transfer of tenancy and does not amount to an act of sub­letting, assignment or parting with the premises and therefore present petition is not maintainable on the ground of sub­letting cite11†u/s 14 (1) (b) of DRC Act and is liable to be dismissed.
L85: It has been contended that petitioner has thorough knowledge that the premises in suit has been continuously in occupation of the respondent since the amalgamation of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L86: with Canara Bank as the petitioner has been continuously residing in the same locality/area where the premises in suit is situated and the petitioner and her representative have been regularly visiting the premises in suit and have never raised any objection to the premises being occupied by the respondent as a tenant.
L87: It has been submitted that the respondent bank has been in exclusive possession of tenanted premises since last 22 years in its own capacity and therefore there has been a implied consent of the landlord/petitioner for creation of tenancy in favour of the respondent. It is further contended that the petitioner was maintaining a saving account along with Mst. Atiya Rehman in their joint names with the respondent bank at Chandni Chowk Branch and the rent @ Rs.
L88: 240/­ per month has been credited in to the said Saving Account of the petitioner and the said rent was withdrawn from time to time by the petitioner and as such by her ­ 4 ­ conduct , the petitioner had impliedly accepted the respondent as her tenant and it cannot be alleged at this stage after the gap of 22 years by the landlord/ petitioner that the respondent bank is a sub­tenant in the tenanted premises.
L89: It has been further contended that there is no cause of action on the ground u/s 14 (1) (f) of cite8†DRC Act and it has been submitted that the tenanted premises are being used as a godown by the respondent bank which is opened regularly by the bank officials and the same do not require any substantial repairs and are not in a dangerous condition and moreover no such document has been filed by the petitioner to show that she had applied to Local Authority for getting the Plans sanctioned or that the Local Authority had issued any notice to the petitioner to carry out repairs on account of unsafe condition of the premises or that she has necessary funds for reconstruction.
L90: It has been further contended that present eviction petition filed by the petitioner is time barred as the petitioner/landlord did not take any action for years against the respondent bank and therefore the tenancy rights accrued in favour of the respondent. It has been further contended that the petitioner only wants to evict the respondent as she wants to let out the tenanted premise at a higher price and the respondent being the old tenant is an hurdle for the same.
L91: The respondent has prayed for dismissal of the petition.
L92: 4. The petitioner has filed the replication in which the averments made in the petition are reiterated and the averments made in the written statement are stated to be wrong and controverted.
L93: 5. In order to prove her case the petitioner has examined ­ 5 ­ herself as PW­1 and evidence by way of affidavit has been filed as Ex. P­1 in which she has reiterated the averments made in the petition. She has placed on record the site plan as Ex. PW­1/1, the newspaper clipping alognwith its translation as Ex. PW­1/2 (Colly) and certified copy of permission from Competent Authority (Slum) as Ex. PW­1/3.
L94: 6. On the other hand, the respondent bank has examined Sh. R.K. Gupta, Sr. Manager, Canara Bank as RW­1 and evidence by way of affidavit has been filed as Ex.R1/A in which averments made in the written statement has been reiterated. He has placed on record the Notification of Government of India dated 23.08.1985 published in Gazette of India as Ex. RW­1/1, certified copy of the Statement of Account of saving account of the petitioner as Ex.
L95: RW­1/2 (Colly) and letter dated 13.05.04 by the petitioner to the respondent bank with regard to change of her address as Ex. RW­1/3 and Ex. RW­1/4.
L96: 7. I have heard the counsel for the parties and perused the record carefully.
L97: 
L98: 8. Ground U/s 14(1)(b) of cite8†DRC Act 14 (1) (b) of cite8†DRC Act reads as under:­ That the tenant has on or after the 9th day of 1952 sub let, assigned or otherwise parted with the possession of the whole or any part of the premises without obtaining the consent in writing of the landlord.
L99: 9. The Ld, Counsel for the petitioner has argued that the petitioner had let out the suit premises to M/s Lakshmi ­ 6 ­ Commercial Bank Ltd. and M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. is claimed to have been merged and taken over by the respondent and as such M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. ceased to exist and this merger/acquisition has been done without the consent of the petitioner and therefore it amounts to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises. The Ld.
L100: Counsel for the petitioner has relied upon in support of his contentions the authorities titled as Parasam H. Rao Vs. Shantiprassad N. Kumar 1980 cite12†RLR SC 747, Cox & Kings Ltd. Vs. Chander Malhotra 1997 RLR 116 (SC), South Asia Industries Pvt. Ltd. Vs. S. Anup Singh and cite13†Singer India Ltd., Appellant Vs. Chander Mohan Chadha & Ors , Respondents AIR 2004 SC 4368.
L101: 10. On the other hand the Ld. Counsel for the respondent has vehemently argued that under the scheme of amalgamation u/s (4) of cite9†Section 45 of Banking Regulation Act 1949, RBI prepared a scheme for amalgamation of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank which was sanctioned by the Central Government and the same was notified in the Gazette of India dated 23.08.1985 and as such M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L102: merged with the Canara Bank under the legislative scheme and therefore it does not amount to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises. Ld. Counsel for the respondent has vehemently contended that the respondent is not sub tenant under the petitioner as the M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L103: was merged with the Canara Bank in the year 1985 and the present petition has been filed after a gap of 22 years and moreover, the respondent bank has been paying the rent all along to the petitioner and the petitioner was also maintaining a saving ­ 7 ­ account with the Canara Bank since 1988. She has further argued that the petitioner knows that the respondent bank has taken over M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L104: and the respondent bank is also remitting the rentals in the account of the petitioner and petitioner has not raised any objections for the last 22 years and therefore by virtue of acquiescence, the respondent has become the tenant as the petitioner has by her conduct accepted the respondent as a tenant. The Ld.
L105: Counsel for the respondent has also argued that by virtue of aforesaid merger, all the assets and liabilities including the lease agreement, movable and immovable properties were taken over by the Canara Bank . In support of her contention, the Ld. Counsel for the respondent has relied upon in an authority titled as "cite14†Mrs. Asha Rohtagi & Ors. Vs Erstwhile New Bank of India through General Manager PNB". On the other hand, Ld.
L106: Counsel for the petitioner has argued that the authority relied upon by the Ld. Counsel for the respondent is not applicable in the facts and circumstances of the present case as the management of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. which was the tenant of the petitioner and the management of the Canara Bank are entirely different and in the authority relied upon by Ld. Counsel for the respondent, both the banks were Nationalised bank.
L107: 11. Facts in the present case are within the narrow compass. The case of the petitioner is that M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. was the tenant and same has been merged with the Canara Bank without the consent of the petitioner and it amounts to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises. On the other hand, the stand of the respondent bank is ­ 8 ­ that M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L108: has been merged with the Canara Bank under the legislative scheme by virtue of notification sanctioned by the Central Government cite15†u/s 45 (7) of Banking Regulation Act 1949 on 23.08.1985 and amalgamation under the legislative scheme does not amount to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises.
L109: 12. Admittedly, M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. was the tenant of the petitioner and the same has been merged with the Canara Bank by virtue of a scheme prepared by the Reserve Bank of India for amalgamation of Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank in exercise of powers conferred under sub­ section (4) of cite9†Section 45 of Banking Regulation Act 1949 .
L110: 13. Now, it has to be seen whether the aforesaid amalgamation/merger of the M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank amounts to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises. The respondent has proved the notification issued by the Govt. of India, Ministry of Finance, Department of Economic Affairs (Banking Division), New Delhi, dated 23.08.1985 as Ex.
L111: RW­1/1 and as per notification, RBI in exercise of powers conferred under sub section (4) of cite9†Section 45 of Banking Regulation Act 1949 has prepared a scheme for amalgamation of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank and in exercise of of powers conferred under cite10†Section 45 (7) of the said Act, the Central Government has sanctioned the scheme of amalgamation as per terms and conditions mentioned in the notification Ex. RW­1/1. The relevant part i.e. clause (2) of notification Ex.
L112: RW­1/1 reads as under:
L113: > "As from the date which the Central Government may specify for ­ 9 ­ this purpose under sub­section (7) of cite10†Section 45 of the said Act (hereinafter referred to as the prescribed date) all rights, powers, claims, demands, interests, authorities, privileges, benefits, assets and properties of the transferor bank, movable and immovable, including premises subject to all incidents of tenure and to the rents and other sums of money and covenants reserved by or contained in the leases or agreements under which they are held, all office, furniture, loose equipment, plant, apparatus and appliances, books, papers, stocks of stationery, other stocks and stores, all investments in stocks, shares and securities, all bills receivable in hand and in transit, all cash in hand and on current or deposit account (including money at call or short notice) with banks, bullion, all book debts, mortgage debts and other debts with the benefit of securities, or any guarantee therefor, all other, if any, property rights and assets benefit of all guarantees in connection with the business of the transferor bank shall, subject to the other provisions of this scheme, stand transferred to, and become the properties and assets of, the transferee bank; and as from the prescribed date all the liabilities, duties and obligations of the transferor bank shall be and shall become the liabilities, duties and obligations of the transferee bank to the extent and in the manner provided hereinafter."
L114: 14. I have gone through the authorities relied upon by both the Ld. counsels for the parties.
L115: 15. In the authorities relied upon by Ld. Counsel for the petitioner, the tenant somehow was found to be instrumental in bringing about the amalgamation and transfer of tenancy rights. However, in the present case, the amalgamation of M/s Lakshmi ­ 10 ­ Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank is an act of legislation. The facts in the authority "cite14†Mrs. Asha Rohtagi & Ors. Vs Erstwhile New Bank of India through General Manager PNB"
L116: > relied upon by the Ld. Counsel for the respondent are similar to the facts in the present case.
L117: In the aforesaid authority, the New Bank of India was merged with the Punjab National Bank vide notification dated 04.09.1993 issued by Government of India cite16†u/s 9 of the Banking Companies Act 1980 and the question arose whether the aforesaid amalgamation/merger amounts to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the demised premise and it was held in the aforesaid authority that this scheme was legislative in character and it does not amount to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the demised premises.
L118: In the aforesaid authority, the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi also referred and discussed all the authorities relied upon by the Ld. Counsel for the petitioner and it was held that the in all those cases, the tenant some how was found to be instrumental in bringing about the amalgamation and transfer of tenancy rights.
L119: It was also held that the tenancy rights have been conferred on the Punjab National Bank by statute namely clause 4(2) of the notification dated 04.09.1993 promulgated by Central Government under cite16†Section 9 of the Banking Companies Act. 1980 which scheme is legislative in character. It does not amount to merger of two companies under the provisions of cite17†Companies Act . Therefore the ratio of M/s Parasram Harnand Rao, M/s cite12†General Radio and Appliances Co. Ltd and others and Cox & Kings Ltd.
L120: and another Vs. Chandler Malhotra will not apply to the facts of the present case.
L121: > ­ 11 ­
L122: 16. Similarly the facts of the present case are squarely covered with the facts in the authority "cite14†Mrs. Asha Rohtagi & Ors. Vs Erstwhile New Bank of India through General Manager PNB" . As discussed herein above, it was held by the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi that amalgamation is an act of legislation and same does not amount to subletting, assigning or parting with the possession of the demised premises.
L123: In the present case also, the tenancy rights have been conferred on the Canara Bank by statute namely clause 2 of the notification dated 23.08.1985 promulgated by Central Government under Sub Section (4) of cite9†Section 45 of the Banking Regulation Act 1949. A perusal of clause 2 of notification Ex.
L124: RW­1/1 shows that all rights, powers, claims, demands, interests, authorities, privileges, benefits, assets and properties of the transferor bank, movable and immovable, including premises subject to all incidents of tenure and to the rent and other sums of money and covenants reserved by or contained in the leases or agreements under which they are held stand transferred to the transferee bank. Thus the tenancy rights of the M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L125: have been conferred to Canara Bank by clause 2 of notification dated 23.08.1985 issued under Sub Section (4) of cite9†Section 45 of the Banking Regulation Act 1949 and it does not amount to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises.
L126: 17. In view of aforesaid discussions and categorical law cite14†laid down by the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi in "cite14†Mrs. Asha Rohtagi & Ors. Vs Erstwhile New Bank of India through General Manager PNB", in the present case also the acquisition/merger of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with Canara Bank does not ­ 12 ­ amount to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises as the merger/acquisition has been done under the legislative scheme. So far the contention of the Ld.
L127: Counsel for the petitioner that the management of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. and management of Canara Bank are different and therefore the law cite14†laid down by Hon'b le High Court of Delhi is not applicable in the present case, is without any merit. It makes hardly any difference that management of two banks is not the same as the scheme by virtue of which M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd.
L128: has been merged into the Canara Bank is legislative in nature and therefore in view of law cite14†laid down by the Hon'b le High Court of Delhi it does not amount to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises.
L129: 18. I also find considerable force in the contention of the Ld. Counsel for the respondent that by way of her conduct the petitioner has impliedly accepted the respondent as a tenant. In the present case, admittedly the rentals of the tenanted premises is paid by the respondent bank to the petitioner. Admittedly, the petitioner has opened a saving account with the respondent bank in the year 1988 and the account opening form dated 04.07.08 is Ex. PW­1/R­1.
L130: Admittedly, the suit property is situated within the premises where the petitioner is residing. PW­1 has also admitted in her cross­examination that she has known about the merger of Laxmi Commercial Bank Ltd. for the last 15­16 years. The aforesaid statement of PW­1 shows that the petitioner is in the knowledge of existence of Canara Bank for the last 15­16 years.
L131: She is also maintaining a saving account with the respondent bank and she is also receiving rent from the respondent bank and she has never raised any objections to the occupation of the ­ 13 ­ respondent bank in the suit premises. This shows that the petitioner has acquiesced the respondent as a tenant.
L132: 19. In view of aforesaid discussion, in my considered opinion, the petitioner has failed to prove that the merger/acquisition of M/s Lakshmi Commercial Bank Ltd. with the Canara Bank amounts to sub­letting, assigning or parting with the possession of the suit premises. Therefore, no ground is made out cite11†u/s 14 (1) (b) of DRC Act and present petition is dismissed under this ground.
L133: 20. Ground U/s 14(1)(f) of cite8†DRC Act That the premises have become unsafe or unfit for human habitation and are required bonafide by the landlord for carrying out repairs which cannot be carried out out without the premises being vacated.
L134: 21. The case of the petitioner is that the suit premises has been lying locked for the last about 14 years and due to its closure for a long time, substantial damage has been caused to the suit property and the suit property has been rendered collapsible any moment and same requires immediate repairs which is not possible unless the vacant possession is handed over to the petitioner.
L135: On the other hand, the respondent has denied that the suit premises remains lying locked and it has been contended that the the suit premises are being used as a godown by the respondent bank which is opened regularly by the bank official and same is not in a dangerous condition and does not require any repairs.
L136: ­ 14 ­
L137: 22. The onus was upon the petitioner to lead cogent evidence to prove that the suit premises have become unfit and unsafe for dwelling and it has become dangerous and collapsible at any moment and same requires immediate repairs, but the petitioner has failed to lead any cogent evidence to prove the aforesaid ground. The petitioner has not examined any expert witness or architect to prove that what damage has been caused to the suit property and to prove that suit premises requires immediate repairs.
L138: Even the petitioner has failed to place on record the photographs showing extent of damage and condition of building. Although, the petitioner has placed on record the newspaper clipping Ex. PW­1/2 (Colly) to prove that the suit property which is also known as Chasma Building has become dangerous but merely by filing the newspaper cutting, the grounds u/s 14 (1) (f) of cite8†DRC Act is not proved. Ld.
L139: Counsel for the petitioner has argued that premises in question was built in 1950' s and it has outlived its age as the premises are 60 years old and are unfit and unsafe for human habitation. In support of his contention he has relied upon in "cite18†Jagdish & Another Vs. Sanjay Kumar & Ors.," Punjab & Haryana High Court. I do not find any merit in this contention of the Ld.
L140: Counsel for the petitioner for the want of insufficient evidence to prove that suit premises have become unfit and unsafe for human habitation. The facts in the authority relied upon by the Ld.
L141: Counsel for the petitioner is not applicable in the present case as in the authority there was ample evidence that the suit shop has become unfit and unsafe for human habitation and it has developed cracks and same has become dangerous but in the present case there is no evidence regarding the condition of the suit property as discussed herein ­ 15 ­ above. The petitioner has not led any cogent and clinching evidence regarding the existing condition of the suit property.
L142: Therefore, the petitioner has failed to prove the grounds cite19†u/s 14 (1) L143: (f) of cite8†DRC Act and thus the petition is dismissed under this ground also.
L144: 
L145: 23. In view of aforesaid discussions, the present petition cite11†u/s 14 (1) (b) and cite20†u/s 14 (1) (f) of Delhi Rent Control Act is dismissed. No order as to costs.
