L158: 9. I have heard the arguments as well as perused the record. My issue-wise findings are given below:-
L159: 10.Issue No. 1. Whether the plaintiff is entitled for recovery of possession of the suit property?OPP:- The onus to prove this issue was on the plaintiff. The case of the plaintiff is that they have purchased the property in the auction conducted by Hon'ble High Court of Delhi and on 22.08.72, the sale certificate Ex. PW-1/9 was issued in favour of plaintiff. The defendants are claiming themselves to be the tenant on the basis of tenancies created by mortgagees and seeking protection of cite12†DRC Act .
L160: It is observed that full bench of Delhi High Court vide judgment dated 11.09.87 Ex. PW-1/20 has already examined the question "Whether a tenant inducted by a mortgagee in possession can claim the benefit of the protection afforded by the Rent Control Legislation after the redemption of the mortgage?".
L161: The Hon'ble Full Bench has held that tenant inducted by mortgagee in possession cannot claim the benefit of the protection afforded by Rent Control Legislation after redemption of mortgage unless the mortgagor had permitted the mortgagee to induct a tenant even beyond the term of mortgage or has concurred to the creation of lease or has adopted it. The relevant paras of the judgment of the Hon'ble Full Bench are as under:
L162: > "4. One of the instances where a partial transfer of ownership rights come into existence is where the owner creates a mortgage. A mortgage is the transfer of an interest in specific CS-279/2010 Page:-13/39 immovable property for the purpose of securing the payment of money advanced or to be advanced by way of loan, an existing or future debt, or the performance of an engagement which may give rise to a pecuniary liability.
L163: Where the mortgagor delivers possession or expressly or by implication binds himself to deliver possession of the mortgaged property to the mortgagee and authorize him to retain such possession until payment of the mortgage-money and to receive the rents and profits accruing from the property or any part of such rents and profits and to appropriate the same in lieu of interest or in payment of the mortgage-money, or partly in lieu of interest, or in payment of the mortgage-money, the transaction is called an usufructuary mortgage and the mortgagee an usufructuary mortgagee.
L164: (See cite16†S. 58 of the Transfer of Property Act. The usufructuary mortgage is, thus a transfer of some interests in the specific immoveable property. Distinguished from a sale, where all the rights of ownership which the transferor has passed to the transferee, in an usufrutuary mortgage only some of the ownership rights are transferred in an usufrutuary mortgage only some of the ownership rights are transferred and the rest remain with the mortgagor.
L165: What is transferred is the right to get the mortgage property sold to realize mortgage money and the right of possession and enjoyment of usufruct. The other rights remain with the owner / mortgagor. He is the owner of the equity of redemption. As provided in cite17†S. 60 of the Transfer of Property Act, on payment of the mortgaged money after it has become due, the mortgagor has the right to recover the possession of the mortgaged property.
L166: All the rights of the CS-279/2010 Page:-14/39 usufructuary mortgagee are wiped out on redemption".
L167: 5. Clauses (a) and (e) and last para of cite18†S.76 of the Transfer of Property Act read as under :
L168: 
L169: > 76. Liabilities of mortgagee in possession. - When, during the continuance of the mortgage, the mortgagee takes possession of the mortgaged property :-
L170: 
L171: > (a) he must manage the property as a person of ordinary prudence would manage it if it were his own; .................
L172: 
L173: > (e) he must not commit any act which is destructive or permanently injurious to the property :
L174: 
L175: > ................
L176: > If the mortgagee fails to perform any of the duties imposed upon him by this Section, he may, when accounts are taken in pursuance of a decree made under this chapter, be debited with the loss, if any, occasioned by such failure."
L177: 6. Clause (a) imposes an obligation on the mortgagee in possession to act as a prudent owner in the management of the mortgaged property. Under the last para of S.76 he is liable to be debited with the loss if he fails to perform this duty. By reason of these statutory provisions the mortgagee in possession, in our opinion, is competent to grant a lease by way of an act of prudent management unless, of course, there is a prohibition to do so under the mortgage contract.
L178: 7. Though the mortgagee in possession, in discharge of the statutory obligations to manage the property as a prudent owner, can create a lease, such a lease, however, cannot be CS-279/2010 Page:-15/39 created for a period exceeding beyond the period of mortgage unless the mortgage contract authorities the mortgagee to create a lease which may ensure after redemption, the principle being that a person cannot transfer a larger estate than he himself has.
L179: Every subordinate interest must perish with the superior interest on which it is dependent. This principle has statutory recognition in cite19†S.111(c) of the Transfer of Property Act which provides that "a lease of immoveable property determines where the interest of the lessor in the property terminates on, or his power to dispose of extends only to, the happening of any event - by the happening of such event."
L180: 8. As seen above, the interests of a mortgagee in possession the lessor terminates on redemption. It follows that the lease of the immoveable property created by him will also determine on redemption. It has so held by the Supreme Court in various decisions.
L181: 
L182: 9. The first decision on this point is cite20†Mahabir Gope v. Harbans Narain Singh , AIR 1952 SC 205. It was held :
L183: > "The general rule is that a person cannot by transfer or otherwise confer a better title on another than he himself has. A mortgagee cannot, therefore, create an interest in the mortgaged property which will ensure beyond the termination of his interest as mortgagee.
L184: Further, the mortgagee, who takes possession of the mortgaged property, must manage it as a person of ordinary prudence would manage it if it were his own; and he must not commit any act which is destructive or permanently injurious to the property; See S.76 sub-clauses (a) CS-279/2010 Page:-16/39 and (e) of the cite21†Transfer of Property Act . It follows that he may grant leases not extending beyond the period of the mortgage; any leases granted by him must come to an end at redemption.
L185: A mortgagee cannot during the subsistence of the mortgage act in a manner detrimental to the mortgagors interests such as by giving a lease which may enable the tenant to acquire permanent or occupancy rights in the land thereby defeating the mortgagors right to khas possession; it would be an act which would fall within the provisions of cite18†S.76 , sub-clause (e) of the cite21†Transfer of Property Act ."
L186: 11. Similarly, in cite22†All India Film Corporation Ltd. v. Raja Gyan Nath , 1969 (3) SCC 79 : (AIR 1969 NSC 185) it was held :
L187: > "The first question to consider is this : Did the tenancy created by the mortgagee in possession survive the termination of the mortgagee interest so as to be binding on the purchaser ? A general proposition of law is that no person can confer on another a better title than he himself has. A mortgage is a transfer of an interest in specific immovable property for the purpose of securing repayment of a loan. A mortgagees interest lasts only as long as the mortgage has not been paid off.
L188: Therefore, on redemption of the mortgage the title of the mortgagee comes to an end. A derivative title from him must ordinarily come to an end with the termination of the mortgagees title. The mortgagee by creating a tenancy becomes the lessor of the property, but his interest as lessor is conterminous with his mortgagee interest.
L189: cite19†S.111(c) of the Transfer of Property Act provides that a lease of immovable CS-279/2010 Page:-17/39 property determines where the interest of the lessor in the property terminates on, or his power to dispose of the same, extends only to the happening of any event - by the happening of such event. The duration of the mortgagees interest determines his position as the lessor.
L190: The relationship of lessor and lessee cannot subsist beyond the mortgagees interest unless the relationship is agreed to by the mortgagor or a fresh relationship is recreated. This the mortgagor or the person succeeding to do the mortgagors interest may elect to do. But if he does not, the lessee cannot claim any rights beyond the term of his original lessors interest. L192: 12. Following these decisions we hold that the general rule is that every subordinate interest must perish with the superior interest on which it is dependent. A mortgagee in possession may grant a lease but he cannot create a lease of the mortgaged property which may ensure beyond the termination of his own interest as a mortgagee unless he has been empowered to do so under the mortgagee contract.
L193: The relationship of landlord and tenant between the mortgagee in possession and his tenant, comes to an end on redemption unless the relationship is agreed by the mortgagor or a fresh relationship is recreated.
