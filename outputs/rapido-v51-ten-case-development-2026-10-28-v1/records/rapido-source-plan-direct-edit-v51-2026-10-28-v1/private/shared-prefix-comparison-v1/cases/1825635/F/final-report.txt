The present property claim rests on a retrospective reconstruction of the appellate and procedural record concerning the family of Devarao Bapuji Deshpande. The decisive events begin with the death of Devarao in 1904, leaving him surviving his widow Sunderabai (Defendant 1) and Gangabai, the widow of his pre-deceased son Shankar. Devarao was the sole surviving coparcener, and after his death, Defendant 1 and Gangabai were the only two members of the family surviving [S20246ada4849]. In February 1934, Gangabai adopted Devaji (the Plaintiff) as a son to her deceased husband Shankar. This adoption was disputed by Defendant 1 regarding its validity [Sf7dcb524f39e].

To resolve this dispute, the parties referred the matter to the sole arbitration of V. D. Belvi. The arbitrator communicated an opinion suggesting an amicable settlement, resulting in an award made in terms of a compromise [S9dd7a3b732a8]. The award explicitly declared that the Plaintiff's adoption was not valid [S9f17902f1d48], that Gangabai's right of adoption was lost from the beginning [S3989c965b576], and that the Plaintiff could never become entitled to the family property [Sf6f730f0b668]. However, for the purpose of maintaining peace and preventing waste of family property, Defendant 1 agreed to pay the Plaintiff Rs. 8,000 as consideration. If unpaid, the Plaintiff was authorized to recover this sum from the family property in Defendant 1's possession [S8a33124e1ea0][Sfd0ce58a6982]. Additionally, Gangabai's existing decree for maintenance against Defendant 1 was to continue permanently [S46ac0b7a4796].

This award was converted into a court decree in Suit No. 291 of 1937 before the First Class Subordinate Judge at Belgaum. Crucially, the Plaintiff admitted the terms of the compromise, the award, and the receipt of the Rs. 8,000, leading the Court to pass a consent decree accordingly on August 6, 1937 [Sdd3b6960e932]. Defendant 1 continued paying maintenance to Gangabai until December 1943 [S65068c4339b6].

The legal landscape shifted following the Privy Council decision in *Anant v. Shankar* (July 26, 1943). Some lawyer seems to have advised Gangabai that she could adopt the plaintiff again; following this advice, Gangabai adopted the Plaintiff as a son to her deceased husband Shankar again on the 12th December, 1943 [S03b0cc76dee1]. Upon learning of this, Defendant 1 adopted her daughter's son, Jivaji (Defendant 2), as a son to her deceased husband Devarao. It is alleged that she ante-dated the deed of adoption executed by her in his favour to the 28th November, 1943 [S36367218d137]. The Plaintiff filed Regular Original Suit No. 330 of 1944 claiming possession of family properties based on the December 12, 1943 adoption [Sf9938846f754]. Defendants 1 and 2 raised defenses of res judicata based on the 1937 decree and estoppel [S38e4fbc658ac][S1fe7eb26b370].

The learned Trial Judge came to the conclusion that the suit was not barred by 'res judicata', that the adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai on 12th December, 1943 was proved and that the same was valid, and that the adoption of the Defendant 2 by the Defendant 1 was also proved though he did not record a finding on the issue as to what was the date of that adoption [S91731545b1c1]. He came to the conclusion that the adoption of the Defendant 2 by the Defendant 1 was invalid by reason of the adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai which was anterior in date [S3a43f85b5230]. However, the Trial Court held the Plaintiff was estopped from claiming immediate possession from Defendant 1, granting him only the right to possession after Defendant 1's death [S3a43f85b5230][S2433e45f0607]. The High Court confirmed this decree [S8f3fb996ddf5]. The findings of facts reached by both the courts below were not contested before us. The adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai on the 12th December, 1943 was held proved by both the courts below and was held to be a valid adoption. The adoption of the Defendant 2 by the Defendant 1 was equally held by both the courts as subsequent in date to the adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai and therefore invalid [Sd07f365b8d4e]. The remaining points of law were whether the 1937 consent decree barred the current suit via res judicata or estoppel [Scb38f598e6f7].

Applying Section 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure, res judicata bars suits where the matter directly and substantially in issue has been finally decided between the same parties litigating under the same title [L193e14a39240]. The 1937 decree addressed the validity of the first adoption (1934). The current suit relies on the second adoption in 1943. Under Section 11 CPC, res judicata applies to matters directly and substantially in issue in a former suit [L193e14a39240]. The award declared the Plaintiff "not and can never become entitled" to the property [Sf6f730f0b668]. Whether this declaration binds future causes of action arising from a new adoption, or if the new adoption constitutes a distinct matter not previously adjudicated, remains an open legal issue requiring interpretation of the consent decree's scope.

Section 115 of the Evidence Act defines estoppel as preventing a person from denying the truth of a thing they intentionally caused another to believe and act upon [L65ec615070d2]. The Plaintiff admitted the terms of the 1937 award, including the invalidity of the first adoption, and received Rs. 8,000 [Sdd3b6960e932]. The issue is whether this admission estops him from claiming rights based on the 1943 adoption. Since the 1943 adoption occurred after the 1937 decree, the application of estoppel depends on whether the earlier admission covered future acts or merely settled past disputes. The record confirms the admission but leaves the legal effect of that admission on the new cause of action unresolved.
