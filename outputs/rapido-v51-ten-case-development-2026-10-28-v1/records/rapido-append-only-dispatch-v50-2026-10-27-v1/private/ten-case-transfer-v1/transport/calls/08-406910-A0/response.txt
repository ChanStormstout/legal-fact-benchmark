Based on the provided materials, here is an assessment of the competing positions regarding debt relief for the 1931 mortgage and the objection concerning the reopening of accounts from 1922.

### 1. Competing Positions on Debt Relief (Agriculturist Status and "Loan" Definition)

**The Judgment-Debtors’ Position:**
*   **Claim:** The petitioners (five out of ten judgment-debtors) argued they were agriculturists within the meaning of the U.P. Debt Redemption Act (No. XIII of 1940). Consequently, they sought to have the decree amended by reducing interest rates to Rs. 4-8-0 per annum from 1922 and adjusting accounts to show nothing was outstanding [S95aee3f2145e].
*   **Evidence:** They relied on testimony that Sita Ram’s income tax liability did not exceed the cess payable on his land, thereby keeping him within the definition of an agriculturist under the proviso to Section 2(2) of the earlier U.P. Agriculturists' Relief Act, a finding accepted by the trial court in 1939 [S2fbf3e5eb127]. In the 1942 application, witnesses confirmed Sita Ram’s salary was low and income tax deductions began only in February 1932, after the 1931 mortgage date [S0fd10e2fb41a].

**The Decree-Holders’ Position:**
*   **Objection:** The decree-holders contended that the petitioners were members of a joint Hindu family and not all were agriculturists. Specifically, they argued Sita Ram paid income tax at the time of the 1931 mortgage and the application, disqualifying the group [Saee84f81cd7f].
*   **"Loan" Argument:** They asserted that the debt advanced under the July 28, 1931 mortgage deed was not a "loan" as defined in the Act because it was used to pay off an earlier mortgage, implying it might fall outside the statutory protection or definition [Saee84f81cd7f]. Under Section 2(9) of the Act, a "loan" includes advances recoverable from an agriculturist, but excludes advances where liability has been transferred to another person [Se9482e7714f9].

**What the Record Establishes:**
*   **Trial Court Finding (1943):** The Additional Civil Judge found that Sita Ram was *not* assessed to income tax at the date of the 1931 mortgage or the 1942 application. Therefore, the applicants were agriculturists, and the case related to a "loan" as defined in the Act [S9adf66efbf95].
*   **High Court Finding (1948):** The High Court dismissed the appeal on this point. It held that the question of Sita Ram's status in 1931 was immaterial because it was not denied that all judgment-debtors were agriculturists at the date of the suit (1938). Furthermore, citing Section 21 of the Act, the Court noted that recovery was limited to mortgaged property, so the proviso regarding personal liability in the definition of "loan" did not apply. Thus, the Act covered the transaction [Sdb081d7e6939, S98c21090d1c5].

### 2. Competing Positions on Reopening Accounts from 1922

**The Judgment-Debtors’ Position:**
*   **Claim:** The petitioners specifically prayed for accounts to be taken from June 22, 1922, when the original loan of Rs. 8,000 was advanced. They argued the 1931 mortgage was merely a renewal/consolidation of the same debt, and thus the reduced interest rate should apply from the inception of the debt in 1922 [S95aee3f2145e].

**The Decree-Holders’ Position:**
*   **Procedural Stance:** In their initial petition of objection, the decree-holders did *not* contest the position that the debt originated in 1922 [Se54d7d19029a].
*   **Subsequent Argument:** At the hearing before the Additional Civil Judge, the decree-holders admitted the other debtors were agriculturists but maintained Sita Ram’s exclusion disqualified them. Regarding the accounts, they evidently relied on evidence from the 1938 suit rather than producing new books of account [S7ad75b5a8d65].

**What the Record Establishes:**
*   **Trial Court Finding (1943):** The Additional Civil Judge reopened the accounts from June 2, 1922. He concluded that the mortgagees in both the 1922 and 1931 deeds were effectively the same entity. After applying the Act’s reduced interest rates from 1922, he found the entire principal and interest had been discharged. He declared the decree fully satisfied [Sfa55b32acae2].
*   **High Court Finding (1948):** The High Court noted that the specific question of whether accounts should be reopened from 1922 or 1931 was *not raised* by the decree-holders in their appeal to the High Court [Sb6f4e643830c].

### 3. Legally or Procedurally Unresolved Issues

**Unresolved Legal Questions:**
1.  **Validity of Reopening Accounts from 1922:** While the Trial Court ordered accounts reopened from 1922, the High Court did not adjudicate this specific issue because it was not raised in the appeal. The Supreme Court appeal (the current record) arises from a certificate granted under Section 110 CPC. The core legal tension remains: Does the U.P. Debt Redemption Act allow the recalculation of interest from the *original* advance date (1922) if the operative decree is based on a later instrument (1931), particularly when the 1931 deed recites that the funds were used to pay off the 1922 debt?
2.  **Definition of "Loan" and Transfer of Liability:** The decree-holders initially argued the 1931 advance was not a "loan" because it repaid a prior debt. The High Court resolved this by focusing on the status of the borrowers at the time of the suit and the nature of the security (mortgage vs. personal liability). However, the precise interaction between Section 2(9)'s exclusion of advances where liability is "transferred to another person" and the consolidation of debts via a new mortgage deed remains a complex interpretive area not fully detailed in the excerpted findings beyond the High Court's reliance on Section 21.

**Procedural Issues:**
1.  **Waiver/Estoppel:** The decree-holders failed to contest the 1922 origin of the debt in their initial objection [Se54d7d19029a] and failed to raise the "reopening of accounts" issue in their High Court appeal [Sb6f4e643830c]. This raises questions about whether these points are now barred by procedural rules (such as Order XLI Rule 21 CPC or principles of estoppel under Section 115 of the Evidence Act [L65ec615070d2]), though the Supreme Court is hearing the matter on a certificate.
2.  **Non-Production of Books:** The Trial Court adversely commented on the decree-holders' non-production of books of account [S111ddbfcfbeb]. The legal consequence of this failure—specifically whether it shifts the burden of proof entirely to the decree-holder to prove the amount due from 1931 onwards—is a procedural fact established by the lower court but its appellate weight is part of the unresolved dispute.

**Conclusion:**
The available record establishes that the judgment-debtors qualify as agriculturists under the Act and that the transaction constitutes a "loan." The Trial Court determined the debt was fully discharged by calculating interest from 1922. The High Court affirmed the eligibility and applicability of the Act but left the specific accounting start date (1922 vs. 1931) unaddressed due to procedural omissions by the decree-holders. The Supreme Court must therefore resolve whether the lower courts correctly applied the Act's provisions to reopen accounts from the original 1922 advance despite the 1931 mortgage being the subject of the decree.
