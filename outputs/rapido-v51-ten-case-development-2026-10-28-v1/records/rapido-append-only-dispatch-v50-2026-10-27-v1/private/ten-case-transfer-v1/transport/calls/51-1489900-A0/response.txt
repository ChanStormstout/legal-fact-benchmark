Based on the provided materials, here is an assessment of the competing land ownership and possession claims in *Civil Appeal No. 61 of 1951* (decided April 21, 1954), involving the descendants of Dalmir, Dilmor, Chhinga, Amir Khan, and Sharif Khan regarding village Manota.

### 1. Competing Claims: Ownership and Possession

**The Plaintiffs’ Claim (Descendants of Dalmir):**
*   **Full Ownership:** The plaintiffs claimed to be full owners in possession of 819 Bighas 19 Biswas of land [Se312cbc09a9c | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L90].
*   **No Right to Partition for Defendants:** They alleged that defendants Nos. 1–16 (descendants of Dilmor, Chhinga, Alif Khan, and Sharif Khan) had no right to claim partition of this land [Se312cbc09a9c | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L90].
*   **Limited Rights of Defendants:** The plaintiffs asserted that the defendants were entitled only to the produce of 140 Bighas 19 Biswas possessed by them, without payment of land revenue [Se312cbc09a9c | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L90].
*   **Basis of Claim:** This position relied on an agreement dated September 11, 1861, embodied in the *Wajib-ul-arz* of that Settlement and repeated in subsequent settlements, which allegedly debarred the defendants from claiming partition [Sfce6b2ed4bf5 | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L92].

**The Defendants’ Claim (Contesting Defendants Nos. 1–16):**
*   **Proprietary Share:** The defendants pleaded that they were recorded in revenue papers as owners of a 4/5th share in the disputed land, while the plaintiffs held a 1/5th share [S03736d37f9fe | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L93].
*   **Right to Partition:** As proprietors, they contended they were inherently entitled to claim partition [S03736d37f9fe | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L93; S1e2ef59ee816 | IKWEB-1489900:U_RECORD_high_court:L96].
*   **Invalidity of Restriction:** They denied that the *Wajib-ul-arz* restrictions were binding after the expiry of the specific Settlement term, arguing such conditions could not permanently bar their statutory right to partition [S03736d37f9fe | IKWEB-1489900:U_INITIAL:L93].

### 2. Documentary Evidence and Settlement History

The title dispute spans four thirty-year Settlements: 1839–42, 1872–79, 1903–08, and 1938–39 [S25b51ad4a1f3 | IKWEB-1489900:U_V2_settlement_context:L99].

*   **Original Grant (1822):** The property was originally granted to Dalmir by Nawab Ahmad Bakhsh Khan Rais. There is no written record of this grant’s terms [S205e9a446ea9 | IKWEB-1489900:U_V2_settlement_context:L101].
*   **1861 Agreement / Wajib-ul-arz:**
    *   Paragraph 18 states the tenure is *zamindari*, with Dalmir liable for profit and loss of the entire village [S417aaf0285ac | IKWEB-1489900:U_V2_early_agreement:L102].
    *   Other *biswadars* (co-sharers) are described as owners of the produce of land they cultivate but pay no revenue [Sa3f85bc956f4 | IKWEB-1489900:U_A:L103].
    *   Paragraph 2 allows partition "of our accord" in accordance with shares shown in Khewat papers [S238d7f39e142 | IKWEB-1489900:U_A:L105].
    *   Paragraph 5 describes the village as *bilijmal* (joint), with sons of Dalmir paying revenue for all shares due to relationship [S75f388f02fc5 | IKWEB-1489900:U_B:L113].
*   **Revenue Records:**
    *   The Khewat and Khatauni prepared during the Settlement record all five branches as being in possession of a 1/5th share each [S9eb062f6a7de | IKWEB-1489900:U_B:L117].
    *   The Jamabandi of 1937–38 also shows all five branches in possession of equal shares [S47def472a3c1 | IKWEB-1489900:U_D:L120].
    *   Testimony from plaintiff Mehrab admitted that defendants were shown as proprietors in the Jamabandi and he never objected [S7c5282f5f79b | IKWEB-1489900:U_F:L121].

### 3. Earlier Litigation and Procedural History

**Prior Civil Litigation (1891–1893):**
*   Alif Khan filed a suit for a declaration of his 1/5th share after his application for partition was rejected [S39b2e2b00a49 | IKWEB-1489900:U_E:L122].
*   The Subordinate Judge decreed the claim, upheld by the Divisional Judge [Se8038316b346 | IKWEB-1489900:U_E:L123].
*   **Chief Court Judgment (June 15, 1893):** In second appeal, the Chief Court amended the decree. It declared the plaintiff entitled to a 1/5th share subject to qualifications in the *Khewat* and *Wajib-ul-arz*. Crucially, it interpreted the *Wajib-ul-arz* to prohibit general division among co-sharers *only while the Wajib-ul-arz was in force*. It held that the five sons of Dalmir could separate inter se, but other co-sharers could not [Sc2d688a625ac | IKWEB-1489900:U_E:L125].

**Current Suit Proceedings:**
*   **Trial Court (Assistant Collector/Senior Sub-Judge):** Decreed the plaintiffs' claim, holding defendants were entitled only to produce of 140 Bighas without interest in remaining land [S3dcb2f432439 | IKWEB-1489900:U_RECORD_trial:L94].
*   **High Court (Lahore):** Reversed the trial decree. It held that:
    1.  Defendants are entitled to a 4/5th share as proprietors.
    2.  The original agreement was binding only so long as the Settlements were in force.
    3.  The restriction ceased to have effect upon the expiry of the Settlements.
    4.  The renewal of terms in the 1938–39 Settlement was not binding because contesting defendants did not agree to them [S4b2b3a6e0136 | IKWEB-1489900:U_RECORD_high_court:L95].
    5.  Relying on the 1893 Chief Court judgment, the High Court held the prohibition of partition did not survive the expiry of the Settlement period [S1e2ef59ee816 | IKWEB-1489900:U_RECORD_high_court:L96].
    6.  Consequently, the suit was dismissed [S1e2ef59ee816 | IKWEB-1489900:U_RECORD_high_court:L96].
