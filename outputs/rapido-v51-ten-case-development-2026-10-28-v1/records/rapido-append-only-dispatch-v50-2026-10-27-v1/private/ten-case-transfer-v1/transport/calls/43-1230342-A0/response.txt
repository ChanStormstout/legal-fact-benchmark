Based on the supplied appellate and procedural records, the competing claims to the five devised properties after the death of Dhuj Singh are assessed as follows:

**1. The Competing Claims**
The dispute arose between the plaintiff (Bajrang Bahadur Singh, the elder brother) and the defendant (Bakhtraj Kuer, the widow of Dhuj Singh).
*   **The Elder Brother’s Claim:** The plaintiff argued that Dhuj Singh held only a life interest in the properties bequeathed by their father, Raja Bisheshwar Bux Singh. Upon Dhuj Singh's death, the plaintiff claimed the property vested in him as the heir of the late Raja. Alternatively, he argued that even if Dhuj Singh had an absolute interest, the plaintiff was entitled to succeed under Section 14(b) read with Section 22(5) of the Oudh Estates Act because the properties were taluqdari in character [S2c031f54c36e, S77cd77b33003, S3e6f868167e9].
*   **The Widow’s Claim:** The defendant resisted the claim, arguing that Bisheshwar, as full owner, was competent to dispose of the properties as he liked. She contended that the will created a life estate for Dhuj Singh followed by a devise in her favor as his personal heir, meaning the properties vested in her upon Dhuj Singh's death [Sea2ab0080131].

**2. Support from the Will and Procedural Record**
The record supports the widow’s position through the specific language of the will and the findings of the lower courts.

*   **Interpretation of the Will:** The trial court translated the will into paragraphs, which the Chief Court found useful. Key provisions include:
    *   Paragraph 3 states Dhuj Singh may remain in possession "as an absolute owner," though with no right of transfer [Sc05db5e25d16, S36554a9c0fa0].
    *   Paragraph 4 explicitly provides that if Dhuj Singh is not living at the time of the testator's death, "his son or whoever may be his male heir or **widow** may remain in possession... as an absolute owner" [S060f91fbf4ce].
    *   Paragraph 6 reinforces that the proprietor of the estate (the elder brother) or other heirs "will not eject Dhuj Singh or his heirs or successors in any way" [Sf45dbac59824].
    *   Paragraph 7 provides for reversion to the estate only if Dhuj Singh or his heirs become "ever heirless" [S5903ed9e2c13].

*   **Procedural Findings:**
    *   The Civil Judge concluded that while Dhuj Singh got a life interest, similar life estates were created in favor of his personal heirs in succession, with the ultimate remainder going to the holder of the estate only when the line of personal heirs became extinct. Consequently, the defendant (the widow) was held entitled to the properties so long as she was alive [Sba226bb61b31].
    *   The Chief Court of Avadh affirmed this decision, dismissing the plaintiff's appeal [S6fa8295e645b].
    *   Prior to the suit, the appellate revenue authority had already set aside an initial mutation order in favor of the plaintiff and directed mutation in the name of the defendant [S1b0d3314e6d9].

**3. Remaining Interpretive Limits**
Despite the support for the widow’s claim, certain interpretive limits and unresolved legal questions remain within the scope of the provided materials:

*   **Statutory Conflict:** The plaintiff relied on the Oudh Estates Act, specifically Sections 13-A, 14, and 22(5), arguing that statutory rules of succession for taluqdari properties should override the will’s provisions regarding the widow. The record notes this argument but does not provide the Supreme Court's final ruling on whether these statutory provisions successfully displaced the testamentary intent identified by the lower courts [S1229b046ee9f, S08139e99b23b].
*   **Nature of Interest:** There is tension in the will's language between granting "absolute ownership" (Paragraphs 3 and 4) and restricting transfer rights (Paragraph 6). The lower courts interpreted this as creating successive life interests rather than true absolute ownership, but the precise legal characterization of the widow's interest vis-à-vis the statutory definition of "taluqdari" succession remains a point of contention raised by the appellant [S77cd77b33003, Sba226bb61b31].
*   **Non-Taluqdari Property:** While four properties are undisputedly taluqdari, the fifth property (List B) is admittedly not taluqdari in character. The record does not specify if the interpretation of the will differs for this non-taluqdari asset compared to the others, leaving its disposition subject to general Hindu law principles or the specific terms of the will without the overlay of the Oudh Estates Act restrictions [S29fb4571d5c7].
