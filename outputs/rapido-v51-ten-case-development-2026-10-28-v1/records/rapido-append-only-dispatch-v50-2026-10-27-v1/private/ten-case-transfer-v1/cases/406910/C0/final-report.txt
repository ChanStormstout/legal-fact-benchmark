The available record establishes a procedural history concerning debt relief applications under the U.P. Debt Redemption Act, 1940, arising from mortgage decrees passed in 1939. The central dispute involves two distinct issues: (1) the eligibility of the judgment-debtors for debt relief based on their status as 'agriculturists' and the definition of 'loan,' and (2) the temporal scope of account reopening—specifically whether accounts should be calculated from the original 1922 mortgage or the subsequent 1931 mortgage.

**Decisive Events and Participants**
The litigation originated with a mortgage deed executed on June 22, 1922, by Madho Ram, Sita Ram, Jai Ram, and Lakshman (sons of Pandit Raja Ram Pant Sess) in favor of Damodarji for Rs. 8,000 [Sa0cc2677dff8, Sf6ab759d9134]. On July 28, 1931, these mortgagors and their sons executed a new mortgage over the same properties in favor of Kothi Kamta Nathji Vishwanathji for Rs. 3,000, reciting that this sum was used to pay off the earlier 1922 debt [S5c8b7a555503]. In May 1938, Girjadharji and Murlidharji (heirs/representatives of Damodarji) filed Suit No. 33 of 1938 against the mortgagors for recovery of principal and interest under the 1931 deed [S1f373a0149f9]. The trial court passed a preliminary decree on March 31, 1939, declaring Rs. 9,497-14-1 due after scaling down interest under the U.P. Agriculturists' Relief Act, 1934 [S15b6886f8dbb]. Following the enactment of the U.P. Debt Redemption Act, 1940, five of the ten judgment-debtors applied under Section 8 of the Act on April 11, 1942, seeking amendment of the 1939 decree [Sa0cc2677dff8, S15b6886f8dbb].

**Competing Positions on Debt Relief Eligibility**
The applicants argued they were agriculturists entitled to reduced interest rates from 1922, asserting that after adjustment, nothing remained outstanding [S95aee3f2145e]. The decree-holders objected, contending that the petitioners were members of a joint Hindu family and that Sita Ram’s payment of income-tax disqualified them from being 'agriculturists' under Section 2(2) of the relevant Acts [S3dcbf8433ad4, Saee84f81cd7f]. They further argued that the 1931 advance was not a 'loan' as defined in the Act because it did not meet the condition of being made to an agriculturist at the time of the advance [Saee84f81cd7f].

The Additional Civil Judge initially found that Sita Ram’s income-tax assessment for 1931-32 did not exceed the cess payable on his land, thus preserving his status as an agriculturist [S2fbf3e5eb127]. However, during the hearing of the 1942 application, the decree-holders’ pleader admitted that all judgment-debtors except Sita Ram were agriculturists but argued that Sita Ram’s inclusion in the mortgage barred the entire group from benefits [S78a3f73dc953]. Evidence showed Sita Ram was a teacher earning Rs. 90/month, with no income-tax deducted until February 1932 [S0352dfc9457d, S3c4a5a9113b1]. The Additional Civil Judge held that since Sita Ram was not assessed to income-tax at the date of the 1931 mortgage or the 1942 application, the applicants were agriculturists and the transaction qualified as a loan [S9adf66efbf95].

On appeal, the High Court Division Bench (April 16, 1948) resolved the eligibility issue differently. It held that the question of Sita Ram’s status on July 28, 1931, was immaterial because it was undisputed that all judgment-debtors were agriculturists on the date of the suit (1938) [Sdb081d7e6939]. Relying on Section 21 of the U.P. Debt Redemption Act, the High Court reasoned that since the mortgage amount could only be recovered from the property and not personally, the proviso to the definition of 'loan' (which requires the advance to be made to an agriculturist) had no application [S98c21090d1c5]. Consequently, the decree-holders’ objection regarding the 'loan' definition failed, and the appeal was dismissed on this point [S98c21090d1c5, Sb6f4e643830c].

**Objection Regarding Reopening Accounts (1922 vs. 1931)**
The second major dispute concerned the starting point for accounting. The applicants specifically prayed for accounts to be taken from 1922, arguing the debt was actually advanced then [S95aee3f2145e]. The decree-holders did not contest this specific prayer in their written objections [Se54d7d19029a]. The Additional Civil Judge reopened accounts from June 2, 1922, finding that the mortgagees in both deeds were effectively the same entity and noting the decree-holders’ failure to produce books of account [Sfa55b32acae2, S111ddbfcfbeb]. He concluded that the entire principal and interest had been discharged, declaring the decree fully satisfied [Sfa55b32acae2].

The decree-holders appealed to the High Court, but the record indicates they did not raise the question of whether accounts should be reopened from 1922 or 1931 before the High Court [Sb6f4e643830c]. The High Court judgment focused solely on the agriculturist/loan eligibility issue [Sdb081d7e6939]. The current appeal before the Supreme Court arises via a certificate granted under Section 110 of the Code of Civil Procedure [Sb6f4e643830c].

**Legal Conditions and Application**
Section 8 of the U.P. Debt Redemption Act allows an agriculturist liable under a decree to apply for amendment if the decree relates to a 'suit to which this Act applies' [Sa73583c33d3c, Sdf34c029df7b]. A 'suit to which this Act applies' is one relating to a 'loan' [Sa129e594786c]. 'Loan' is defined as an advance made before June 1, 1940, recoverable from an agriculturist, provided the advance was made to an agriculturist [Se9482e7714f9, Sff81fb3988bd]. The High Court’s interpretation relied on Section 21, which limits recovery to the mortgaged property, thereby negating the need to prove the borrower was an agriculturist *at the time of the advance* for the purpose of the proviso, as long as they were agriculturists at the time of the suit [S98c21090d1c5].

**Remaining Gaps and Unresolved Issues**
While the High Court settled the eligibility issue, the record leaves the temporal scope of the accounting unresolved in the context of the final appellate disposition. The Additional Civil Judge’s order discharging the decree based on 1922 accounts stands as a factual finding, but its legal validity depends on whether the Supreme Court accepts the premise that the 1931 mortgage merely consolidated the 1922 debt or constitutes a separate transaction subject to different calculation rules. The decree-holders’ failure to contest the 1922 start date in their initial objections [Se54d7d19029a] and their failure to raise the issue in the High Court appeal [Sb6f4e643830c] suggests a potential waiver or estoppel argument, though the Supreme Court’s review under Section 110 CPC may allow examination of substantial questions of law regarding the applicability of the Act to the 1931 instrument versus the 1922 origin. The precise legal effect of the 1931 recital that funds were used to pay the 1922 debt remains a critical factual determinant for the accounting period.
