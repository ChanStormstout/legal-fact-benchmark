The appellate record in Civil Appeal No. 158 of 1951 [S0641d613dbf3] concerns a dispute over the succession to the estate of Musammat Ram Piari, who died in April 1941 [S372c1e6f95b9]. The appellant (whose name is not explicitly stated in the provided excerpts, though Gurbachan Singh appears as counsel for the respondent) claimed to be the sole lawful heir and husband of Ram Piari, seeking possession of her movable and immovable properties against the respondents: Parvin Kumari (alleged daughter) and Shrimati Raj Kumari (defendant No. 2) [Sa7984f7b8dcf][Sac440ce83951]. The central legal issue is whether the appellant’s claim of spousal inheritance priority under custom supersedes the daughter’s rights, contingent upon proving a valid marriage and establishing the applicability of that custom to the specific property provenance.

**Competing Positions on Marriage and Property Provenance**
The appellant alleged he married Ram Piari, an employee of Raj Kumari, approximately 22 years prior to the suit, and that she lived with him at Hoshiarpur, giving birth to Parvin Kumari in 1929 [Sfc423fc780e5]. He asserted the properties were acquired by Ram Piari using his money [S372c1e6f95b9]. Conversely, the respondents denied the marriage, claiming Ram Piari had eloped with a motor driver in 1921 and returned to Raj Kumari’s estate in 1932 with Parvin Kumari [S8e8de02b9c7c]. They argued the properties were acquired by Raj Kumari with her own funds for Ram Piari’s benefit, and that two cars belonged solely to Raj Kumari, with Ram Piari acting only as a *benamidar* [Sadbb580bf3ae]. Raj Kumari further contended that Ram Piari had made a will bequeathing the assets to Parvin Kumari [S68be74e96323].

**Evidentiary Findings on Marriage**
The trial court decreed the suit in favor of the appellant regarding all properties except the two cars, holding that Ram Piari was legally married to the appellant [S2fd19e63ea03]. This finding relied on direct testimony from four witnesses (Babu Ram, Anant Ram, Babu, and Asa Ram) who claimed presence at the marriage ceremony [Sc736afd37517], and circumstantial evidence including cohabitation, social recognition as husband and wife, and correspondence where Raj Kumari addressed Ram Piari care of the plaintiff [Sd1bc00e3b08c][S868ae1b691ea]. However, the High Court reversed this finding, holding that while long cohabitation raised a presumption of marriage, it was completely rebutted [S10465291501e]. The High Court found the direct evidence unconvincing, noting the witnesses were interested or hired, and that the lack of broader community testimony was not excused by Raj Kumari’s influence [S1daedcf061d9][Sc439b9763805]. Crucially, the High Court relied on contradictory statements: Ram Piari’s mother testified no marriage occurred [S2d87090d5919], Ram Piari’s will stated the same [S2d87090d5919], and Parvin Kumari testified she never saw her father [Sab2054b5482f]. Additionally, several witnesses confirmed Ram Piari had eloped with a driver [Sb814f8668443]. The High Court also determined that certain relationship opinions did not satisfy Section 50 of the Indian Evidence Act because the witnesses lacked special means of knowledge [L6052a06ef1a9][Sc073ce64d379].

**Customary Law and Succession Priority**
Regarding succession, the trial court held the appellant was governed by customary law applicable to Rajputs of Hoshiarpur district, which granted him preferential heirship over the daughter, rendering Ram Piari’s will invalid due to lack of power to bequeath under custom [S2fd19e63ea03][S383c56531d7a]. The High Court, however, reached different conclusions on custom. It accepted that the appellant belonged to an agricultural tribe governed by local custom [Sfa4924e67740]. Yet, it found no local or general custom allowing a husband to succeed in preference to a daughter regarding property given to the deceased by a stranger (Raj Kumari) [Sfa4924e67740]. Consequently, the High Court applied Hindu law, under which Parvin Kumari, as the daughter, was entitled to succeed in preference to the plaintiff [Sfa4924e67740].

**Unresolved Legal Issues and Procedural Status**
The appeal before the Supreme Court challenges the High Court’s dismissal. The decisive conflict lies in the validity of the marriage and the scope of custom. If the marriage is proven, the question remains whether custom overrides statutory Hindu law for self-acquired or gifted property. The High Court’s rejection of the marriage was accompanied by an independent finding that no applicable custom granted the husband priority over the daughter for the specific properties in question, leading to the application of Hindu law succession favoring the daughter. The record indicates the appellant obtained a certificate under Sections 109 and 110 of the Code of Civil Procedure to appeal [S72d705116180]. The final disposition of this appeal is withheld from the provided excerpts, leaving the ultimate legal effect of the High Court’s findings on marriage rebuttal and custom limitations unresolved in the target judgment.
