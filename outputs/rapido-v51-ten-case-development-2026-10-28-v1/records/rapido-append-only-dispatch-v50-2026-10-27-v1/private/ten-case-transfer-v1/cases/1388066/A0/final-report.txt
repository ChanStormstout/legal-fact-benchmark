Based on the supplied appellate record and legal materials, here is an assessment of the claimed spousal inheritance priority against the daughter in *Gurbachan Singh v. Parvin Kumari* (Civil Appeal No. 158 of 1951).

### 1. Marriage Evidence
The central factual dispute was whether the appellant (plaintiff) was legally married to the deceased, Ram Piari. The evidence presented by both sides conflicted significantly:

*   **Plaintiff’s Case:** He alleged a marriage performed with "great pomp and show" about 22 years prior, resulting in cohabitation at Hoshiarpur and the birth of a daughter, Parvin Kumari [Sfc423fc780e5 | IKWEB-1388066:L188]. To prove this, he relied on:
    *   **Direct Witnesses:** Four witnesses (Babu Ram, Anant Ram, Babu, Asa Ram) claimed to have attended the marriage ceremony [Sc736afd37517 | IKWEB-1388066:L205].
    *   **Circumstantial/Conduct Evidence:** Witnesses testified that Ram Piari lived with the plaintiff as his wife, was treated as such by neighbors, and that defendant Raj Kumari addressed correspondence to her "care of plaintiff" [Sd1bc00e3b08c | IKWEB-1388066:L208]. Paras Ram (Ram Piari’s brother) allegedly addressed the plaintiff as *jija* (sister’s husband) [S36a6c315daba | IKWEB-1388066:L210].
*   **Defendants’ Case:** They denied the marriage, alleging Ram Piari had eloped with a motor driver in 1921 and returned to Raj Kumari’s estate after 11 years with her daughter [S8e8de02b9c7c | IKWEB-1388066:L203]. Ram Piari’s mother and several witnesses deposed that she was never married to the plaintiff [S2d87090d5919 | IKWEB-1388066:L225; Sb814f8668443 | IKWEB-1388066:L230].
*   **Judicial Findings on Evidence:**
    *   The Trial Court accepted the plaintiff’s version, finding him to be the legally wedded husband [S2fd19e63ea03 | IKWEB-1388066:L193].
    *   The High Court reversed this, holding that while long cohabitation raised a presumption of marriage, it was "completely rebutted." The High Court found the direct witnesses unconvincing (one hired, three interested) and noted that the circumstantial evidence did not strictly comply with **Section 50 of the Indian Evidence Act** because the witnesses lacked "special means of knowledge" regarding the relationship [S10465291501e | IKWEB-1388066:L196; Sc073ce64d379 | IKWEB-1388066:L218]. Section 50 requires opinion expressed by conduct from persons with special knowledge to prove relationship [L6052a06ef1a9 | LAW:EA50].

### 2. Property Provenance
The source of the disputed assets determined which law might apply (customary vs. statutory/Hindu Law).

*   **Plaintiff’s Claim:** Ram Piari acquired movable and immovable properties "in her own name with the aid of his [the plaintiff's] money" [S372c1e6f95b9 | IKWEB-1388066:L189]. He argued these were self-acquired properties subject to customary succession rules favoring the husband.
*   **Defendants’ Claim:** Raj Kumari asserted the properties were acquired by her with her own money for Ram Piari’s benefit, making Ram Piari a *benamidar* (nominal holder) or recipient of gifts [S68be74e96323 | IKWEB-1388066:L191; S6da2cd32c27b | IKWEB-1388066:L204]. Specifically, two cars were claimed to belong entirely to Raj Kumari [Sadbb580bf3ae | IKWEB-1388066:L192].
*   **Trial Court Finding:** The trial court held that the properties belonged to Ram Piari but excluded the two cars, which were found to belong to Raj Kumari [S2fd19e63ea03 | IKWEB-1388066:L193].

### 3. Custom and Succession Priority
The legal basis for the husband’s claim rested on customary law applicable to Rajputs in Hoshiarpur district.

*   **Appellant’s Argument:** As a Rajput governed by custom, the husband is entitled to succeed to the wife’s property to the exclusion of the daughter [Sb7fd20eb752b | IKWEB-1388066:L190].
*   **High Court’s Analysis of Custom:**
    1.  It acknowledged the appellant belonged to an agricultural tribe governed by Rajput customs [Sfa4924e67740 | IKWEB-1388066:L197].
    2.  However, it found **no local or general custom** allowing a husband to succeed in preference to a daughter regarding property given to the wife by a stranger (Raj Kumari) [Sfa4924e67740 | IKWEB-1388066:L197].
    3.  Consequently, the High Court applied Hindu Law, under which the daughter (Parvin Kumari) has priority over the husband in inheriting the mother’s separate property [Sfa4924e67740 | IKWEB-1388066:L197].
*   **Will Validity:** The defendants produced a will by Ram Piari bequeathing property to Parvin Kumari. The Trial Court invalidated this will, stating that under customary law, Ram Piari had no power to make a will [S383c56531d7a | IKWEB-1388066:L194]. This finding depended entirely on the validity of the marriage and the applicability of custom.

### 5. Unresolved Legal Issues
The Supreme Court appeal raises several critical issues that remain unresolved in the provided text (which ends before the final judgment):

1.  **Standard of Proof for Marriage:** Whether the High Court erred in rejecting the presumption of marriage arising from long cohabitation and social recognition, particularly when interpreting **Section 50 of the Evidence Act**. The plaintiff argues the circumstantial evidence (conduct of family members, official records) was sufficient [Sd1bc00e3b08c | IKWEB-1388066:L208].
2.  **Scope of Customary Law:** If the marriage is proven, does the specific custom of Hoshiarpur Rajputs indeed exclude daughters in favor of husbands for *self-acquired* property? The High Court distinguished between property acquired by the wife herself versus property gifted by a third party (Raj Kumari), suggesting custom might not apply to the latter [Sfa4924e67740 | IKWEB-1388066:L197].
3.  **Validity of the Will:** If customary law applies and restricts testamentary power (as found by the Trial Court), the will is invalid. If Hindu Law applies (as found by the High Court), the will’s validity depends on Ram Piari’s capacity under Hindu Law, which generally allows women to dispose of their separate property.
4.  **Benami Ownership:** The status of the property as belonging to Ram Piari versus being held *benami* for Raj Kumari remains contested, though the Trial Court largely accepted Ram Piari’s ownership except for the cars [S2fd19e63ea03 | IKWEB-1388066:L193].

**Conclusion:**
The claimed spousal inheritance priority hinges entirely on proving the marriage. The Trial Court granted priority based on established marriage and custom. The High Court denied priority by finding the marriage unproven and applying Hindu Law where the daughter takes precedence. The Supreme Court must determine if the High Court’s rejection of the marriage evidence was legally sound under the Evidence Act and if the distinction regarding the provenance of the property (gifted vs. self-acquired) correctly limits the application of customary succession rules.
