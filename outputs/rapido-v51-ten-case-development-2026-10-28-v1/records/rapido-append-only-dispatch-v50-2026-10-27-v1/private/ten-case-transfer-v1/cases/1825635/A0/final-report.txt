Based on the provided appellate and procedural records, here is an assessment of whether the later adoption (1943) supports the present property claim despite the earlier compromise, payment, and asserted defenses of *res judicata* and estoppel.

### 1. Separate Findings of Fact
The following findings were established by the Trial Court and confirmed by the High Court, and were **not contested** before the Federal Court [Scd42552232a7 | IKWEB-1825635:L48; Sd07f365b8d4e | IKWEB-1825635:L72]:

*   **Validity of Later Adoption:** The adoption of the Plaintiff by Gangabai on December 12, 1943, was proved and held to be a **valid adoption** [S91731545b1c1 | IKWEB-1825635:L67; Sd07f365b8d4e | IKWEB-1825635:L72].
*   **Invalidity of Competing Adoption:** The adoption of Defendant 2 (Jivaji) by Defendant 1 (Sunderabai) was held to be subsequent in date to the Plaintiff’s 1943 adoption and therefore **invalid** [S3a43f85b5230 | IKWEB-1825635:L68; Sd07f365b8d4e | IKWEB-1825635:L72].
*   **Status of Prior Award:** The prior arbitration award (1934/1937) declared the *first* adoption (1934) invalid and stated the Plaintiff "can never become entitled" to family property [S9f17902f1d48 | IKWEB-1825635:L52; Sf6f730f0b668 | IKWEB-1825635:L56]. However, this award was based on the specific facts existing at that time.
*   **Payment Consideration:** Defendant 1 paid Rs. 8,000 to the Plaintiff under the terms of the 1937 decree [Sdd3b6960e932 | IKWEB-1825635:L60].

### 2. Current Scope of Legal Issues
The scope of the appeal before the Federal Court was limited strictly to two points of law regarding the effect of the 1937 consent decree on the 1943 suit [S289df08b91b8 | IKWEB-1825635:L73; Scb38f598e6f7 | IKWEB-1825635:L75]:

1.  **Res Judicata:** Whether the present suit (filed Jan 1944) was barred by *res judicata* due to the consent decree passed in Suit No. 291 of 1937.
2.  **Estoppel:** Whether the Plaintiff’s claim was barred by estoppel due to his admission of the 1937 compromise and receipt of consideration.

*Note: The factual validity of the 1943 adoption itself was outside the scope of these legal bars because it occurred after the 1937 decree.*

### 3. Assessment of Defenses vs. Later Adoption

#### A. Res Judicata (CPC Section 11)
Under **LAW:CPC11**, *res judicata* applies only if the matter directly and substantially in issue has been heard and finally decided in a former suit between the same parties litigating under the same title.

*   **Analysis:** The 1937 decree adjudicated the validity of the **1934 adoption**. It did not adjudicate the validity of a **future** adoption made in 1943.
*   **Change in Circumstances:** The record notes that the Privy Council decision in *Anant v. Shankar* (July 26, 1943) prompted the second adoption [S03b0cc76dee1 | IKWEB-1825635:L62]. This represents a new cause of action arising from a new fact (the 1943 adoption) occurring after the finality of the 1937 decree.
*   **Conclusion:** The later adoption creates a distinct legal status not covered by the previous judgment. Therefore, the 1937 decree does not operate as *res judicata* against the claim based on the 1943 adoption. The Trial Judge correctly concluded the suit was **not barred** by *res judicata* [S91731545b1c1 | IKWEB-1825635:L67].

#### B. Estoppel (Evidence Act Section 115)
Under **LAW:EA115**, estoppel prevents a person from denying the truth of a thing they previously caused another to believe to be true.

*   **Analysis:** The Plaintiff admitted the 1934 adoption was invalid and accepted Rs. 8,000 as consideration for relinquishing claims related to *that* specific adoption [Sdd3b6960e932 | IKWEB-1825635:L60; S9f17902f1d48 | IKWEB-1825635:L52].
*   **Scope of Admission:** The admission was specific to the 1934 transaction. It does not logically extend to bar a claim arising from a completely separate act (the 1943 adoption) performed years later, especially when the legal landscape had changed (*Anant v. Shankar*).
*   **Trial Court Finding:** Despite the payment and prior admission, the Trial Judge held that while the Plaintiff was estopped from claiming possession *during* Defendant 1's lifetime, he was **not estopped** from establishing his status as the validly adopted son via the 1943 deed [S3a43f85b5230 | IKWEB-1825635:L68].
*   **Conclusion:** The later adoption supports the claim of *status* (being the son of Shankar). The estoppel defense failed to negate the validity of the new adoption but succeeded in delaying the *possession* remedy until after Defendant 1's death.

### 4. Open Legal Issues & Disposition Logic

While the target court's final reasoning is withheld, the record highlights the following critical distinction in the disposition:

1.  **Status vs. Possession:** The courts distinguished between the right to be recognized as an heir (Status) and the right to immediate control of assets (Possession).
    *   The 1943 adoption was valid, granting the Plaintiff the status of Shankar's son [S91731545b1c1 | IKWEB-1825635:L67].
    *   However, the Trial Judge granted declarations of status but **dismissed the suit for immediate possession**, holding the Plaintiff was entitled to possession only **after the death of Defendant 1** [S3a43f85b5230 | IKWEB-1825635:L68; S2433e45f0607 | IKWEB-1825635:L69].
2.  **Effect of Compromise on Future Rights:** The open question is whether the 1937 clause stating the Plaintiff "can never become entitled" [Sf6f730f0b668 | IKWEB-1825635:L56] acts as a perpetual bar. The lower courts effectively ruled it did not, as the 1943 adoption was a new event.
3.  **Priority of Adoptions:** Since Defendant 2's adoption was found to be subsequent to the Plaintiff's 1943 adoption, Defendant 2's claim was invalid [S3a43f85b5230 | IKWEB-1825635:L68].

### Final Assessment
**Yes, the later adoption supports the present property claim regarding *title/status*, despite the compromise and payment.**

*   **Res Judicata** does not apply because the 1943 adoption is a new cause of action arising after the 1937 decree.
*   **Estoppel** does not bar the declaration of valid adoption because the prior admission concerned only the 1934 adoption.
*   **Limitation:** The support is qualified. The Plaintiff obtains a declaration of valid adoption and future entitlement, but **immediate possession** is denied until the death of the current holder (Defendant 1), likely due to the residual effect of the maintenance arrangement and the specific terms of the prior compromise which settled the immediate dispute [S46ac0b7a4796 | IKWEB-1825635:L59; S3a43f85b5230 | IKWEB-1825635:L68].
