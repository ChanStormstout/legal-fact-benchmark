L71:     tenant.

L75:     HEADNOTE:
L76:         The premises in question had been let out in April, 1968
L77:     to the appellant at a monthly rent of Rs.340 by the  husband
L78:     of respondent No. 1 and the father of respondents Nos. 2  to
L79:     8.  In	July, 1977 the landlord filed an  eviction  petition
L80:     against	 the appellant on the ground of bona  fide  require-
L81:     ment.  In  September, 1978 appellant's wife was	 allotted  a
L82:     Government quarter due to her employment as a teacher In the
L83:     Government school. In March, 1986 the first respondent filed
L84:     a  petition before the Rent Controller that the wife of	 the
L85:     appellant  having been allotted a residential  quarter	came
L86:     within	the mischief of clause (h) of cite13†Section 14(1) of	 the
L87:     Act and was, therefore, liable for eviction.
L88:         The	 appellant  contended that he had not  acquired	 any
L89:     house  and that the quarter in question was allotted to	 his
L90:     wife  on joint allotment basis on compassionate grounds	 and
L91:     that  the  same	 had been surrendered  and,  therefore,	 the
L92:     appellant  was	not  liable to be evicted.  It	was  further
L93:     contended  that	 the  allotted accommodation  could  not  be
L94:     treated	 as alternative accommodation for the appellant	 and
L95:     his family.
L96:         The	 Additional Rent Controller held that having  regard
L97:     to the provisions of law, as the tenant had acquired  vacant
L98:     possession  for residence, he became disentitled  to  retain
L99:     the premises in question and, therefore, passed an order  of
L100:     eviction.
L101:         The	 Rent Control Tribunal dismissed the appeal  of	 the
L102:     appellant. The High Court rejected the second appeal.
L103:         In the appeal to this Court, the question for considera-
L104:     tion  was: whether under clause (h) of cite13†Section 14(1) of	 the
L105:     Act  allotment of a house to the wife, who was a  Government
L106:     employee,  in  all circumstances disentitled the  tenant  to
L107:     retain the tenanted premises.
L108:     	   1185
L109:     Allowing the appeal, this Court,
L110:         HELD:  1. The premises in question which the wife  occu-
L111:     pied was indisputably not the matrimonial home. The  husband
L112:     would  not,  therefore, have any statutory  or	legal  right
L113:     against	 the Government to use and enjoy the allotted  prem-
L114:     ises  to  the  wife of the tenant because of  her  job.	 The
L115:     tenant	cannot	be made to lose his tenancy because  of	 the
L116:     wife  acquiring possession of a flat or allotment of a	flat
L117:     because of her official duties over which the husband has no
L118:     right or domain or occupation. [1194C-D]
L119:         2.1	 The  purpose of the cite14†Delhi Rent Control Act is  to
L120:     control	 rents	and evictions; in other	 words,	 to  control
L121:     unreasonable  evictions and to ensure that in an  atmosphere
L122:     of  acute shortage of accommodation, there is proper  enjoy-
L123:     ment  of  available spaces by those who	 want  and  deserve.
L124:     [1189G-H]
L125:         2.2	 Unless acquisition of a premises or allotment of  a
L126:     premises  or a part of a premises by the tenant in which  he
L127:     has domain which he can reasonably and alternatively use  as
L128:     substitute  for	 the place he is using in  the	tenancy,  it
L129:     cannot lead to forfeiture of his right to occupy his tenant-
L130:     ed  premises.  The case would be otherwise,  however,  if  a
L131:     tenant comes into possession of a premises or is allotted  a
L132:     piece  of  residence or acquires vacant	 possession  of	 the
L133:     premises then such a tenant cannot prevent, if other  condi-
L134:     tions are fulfilled under cite12†Section 14(1)(h) of the Act  being
L135:     liable to forfeiture of his tenancy. [1190B-C]
L136:         2.3	 Tenancy is a right vested in the tenant.  The	main
L137:     purpose	 of the Act is the protection of tenants from  evic-
L138:     tion. The various provisos to sub-section (1) of cite15†section 14 L139:     laid  down the exception to this rule. The intention of	 the
L140:     Legislature  in divesting the tenant of his right was  based
L141:     upon  the fact that the tenant had legally acquired  another
L142:     residence  as of right. There is no law according  to  which
L143:     husband and wife could be deemed to be one person. [1191E-F]
L144:         2.4	 The acquisition of other residence must be  by	 the
L145:     tenant	himself before proviso (h) of sub-s. (1) of  cite15†section 14 of the Act would apply. [1191D]
L146:         2.5	 If  a wife or husband acquires a property  and	 the
L147:     other  spouse if he/she is the tenant, has a legal right  by
L148:     virtue	of  such acquisition and stay there, then  only	 can
L149:     such  acquisition or allotment of premises would  disentitle
L150:     or attract the provisions of cl. (h) of cite13†section 14(1) of the
L151:     Act, otherwise the whole purpose would be defeated. That  is
L152:     the rationale behind the scheme. [1191F-G]
L153:     1186
L154:         2.6 From the fact that the wife of the tenant was allot-
L155:     ted a temporary Government accommodation, it cannot be	said
L156:     that there was admission by virtue of which the tenant could
L157:     lose his tenancy that the wife has acquired a house which is
L158:     available to the husband over which the husband has a domain
L159:     which  could be a substitute to the tenanted premises.	This
L160:     fact of acquisition or allotment of fiat in the name of wife
L161:     (which	incidentally she has lost having given up  the	job)
L162:     can be in certain circumstances a factor in judging the bona
L163:     fide needs of the landlord; but the same indisputably cannot
L164:     be any ground to evict the tenant on the ground that he	 has
L165:     acquired vacant possession or allotted residence in terms of
L166:     cl. (h) of cite13†section 14(1) . [1192E-F, 1193A-B]
L167:         3.	The rights, if any, of the parties in  the  eviction
L168:     petition  on  the ground of bona fide need of  the  landlord
L169:     will not in any manner be prejudicially affected. [1194F]
L170:         [In England the rights of the spouses to the matrimonial
L171:     home  are now governed by the provisions of the	 Matrimonial
L172:     Homes  Act, 1967. Where one spouse is entitled to  occupy  a
L173:     dwelling  house by virtue of any estate or interest or	con-
L174:     tract  or by virtue of any enactment giving him or  her	 the
L175:     right  to remain in occupation, and the other spouse is	 not
L176:     so entitled, then the spouse not so entitled has the certain
L177:     rights (known as "rights of occupation"), that is to say, if
L178:     in  occupation, a right not to be evicted or  excluded	from
L179:     the  dwelling  house or any part of it by the  other  spouse
L180:     except with the leave of the court given by an order; if not
L181:     in occupation, a right with the leave of the court so  given
L182:     to enter into and occupy the dwelling house. [1193D-G]
L183:         Such  rights  are  not granted in India.  But  with	 the
L184:     change of situation and complex problems arising, it is high
L185:     time to give the wife or the spouse a right of occupation in
L186:     a truly matrimonial home, in case of marriage breaking up or
L187:     in case of strained relationship between the husband and the
L188:     wife.] [1193G]
L189:         Prem Chand and another v. Sher Singh, [1981] Delhi	Rent
L190:     Judgment  287;	Smt. Revti Devi v. Kishan  Lal,	 [1970]	 All
L191:     India  Rent  Control Journal 418; Phiroze Bamanji  Desai  v.
L192:     Chandrakant  M. Patel and others, [1974] 3 SCR 267;  Galanan

L206: Mr. Soli J. Sorabjee, Dr. Roxna Swamy and Bharat Sangal for the Appellant.
L207: A.B. Rohtagi and Miss Bina Gupta for the Respondents. The Judgment of the Court was delivered by SABYASACHI MUKHARJI, J. What is the true meaning of the expression 'tenant has before or after the commencement of the Act, built, acquired vacant possession of, or been allotted, a residence' in terms of clause (h) of cite13†Section 14(1) of the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 (hereinafter called 'the Act') is the question raised in this appeal in the backdrop of interesting set of facts.
L208: This is an appeal by the tenant against the judgment and order dated 6th April, 1987 of the Delhi High Court. To the facts first, however, we must go to appreciate the point. The appellant was at all material times since 1968 a tenant of the ground floor of premises No. 2/14, Kalkaji Extention, New Delhi. The premises had been let out in April, 1968 to the appel- lant at a monthly rent of Rs.340 per month by one Shri R.N. Kurra, deceased husband of respondent No. 1 and father of respondents Nos. 2 to 8.
L209: The premises consist of two bed rooms, one drawing room, one dining room, one kitchen, two bath rooms and court yard at the back and porch in the front and one store and also one verandah. It is the case of the appellant that originally the appellant had occupied these alongwith his wife, his aged mother, his son, daughter, brother and sister-in-law. However, the brother and sister- in-law have since moved out and since 1979 the appellant's wife Smt.
L210: Santosh Raj was only staying with the family off and on for short periods usually when the appellant was on tour. In the meantime on or about 20th July, 1977 the land- lord filed eviction petition against the appellant on the ground of bona fide requirement. On or about 25th September, 1978 the appellant's wife Smt. Santosh Raj was allotted Flat No. 93, Sadiq Nagar, i.e., a government quarter was given to her due to her employment as a teacher in the Government Girls Higher Secondary School No.
L211: III, Kalkaji, New Delhi. She goes to live there leaving the rest of her family in the premises in dispute. It is the case Of the appellant aS made out in appeal before us that one Shri P.R. Arya and his family were asked to share with the appellant's wife on a joint-allotment basis because it was not safe for a lady to live alone. The case of the appellant was that the relation- ship between the appellant and his wife Smt. 1188 Santosh Raj was not very good. There were differences of opinion.
