L76:          There was	'much ado  about nothing'  about these	Writ
L77:     Petitions  under   cite14†Article 32 of  the  Constitution.	 The
L78:     petitions  sought   to	challenge   the	 Vires	of  cite11†sections 14(1)(b) , cite12†16(2) and, incidentally, cite13†sec. 30(ii) of the Tamil
L79:     Nadu Buildings	(Lease and  Rent Control)  Act, 1960  on the
L80:     ground of  being arbitrary, discriminatory and unreasonable.
L81:     The different  petitions had  different facts,	and  it	 was
L82:     considered appropriate	to deal	 with the  facts of the writ
L83:     petition filed by Prabhakaran Nair (Writ Petition No. 506 of
L84:     1986) as a typical case to appreciate the points in issue.
L85:          In	 that	case,	the   respondents-landlords,   after
L86:     purchasing the premises in dispute from the erstwhile owner,
L87:     filed an application for the eviction of the petitioner from
L88:     the said  premises on  the grounds  of non-payment  of	rent
L89:     under cite15†section 10(2)(1) , unlawful  sub-letting under cite16†section 10(2)(ii)(a) , causing  damages to the premises under cite17†section 10(2)(iii) and demolition and reconstruction of the premises
L90:     under section 14(1)(b) of the Tamil Nadu Rent Act. The Trial
L91:     Court ordered  eviction only  under cite11†section 14(1)(b) of the
L92:     Act for	 demolition and	 reconstruction, rejecting the other
L93:     grounds. The  appellate court  dismissed the  appeal of	 the
L94:     petitioner. The High Court also dismissed the civil revision
L95:     petition of  the petitioner.  The petitioner  then  filed  a
L96:     petition  for  Special	Leave  in  this	 Court	against	 the
L97:     judgment and  order of the High Court. In the meanwhile, the
L98:     City Civil  Court, on  January	29,  1983,  granted  interim
L99:     injunction,  restraining   the	respondents-landlords	from
L100:     demolishing the building till the disposal of an application
L101:     filed by  the petitioner  in the suit, against the erstwhile
L102:     owner and  the present landlords for specific performance of
L103:     an agreement  to sell  the premises  to the  petitioner. The
L104:     injunction was	stated to  have been confirmed and was still
L105:     continuing as  the said application for specific performance
L106:     was still pending in the City Civil Court.
L107:     2
L108:          This Court	 dismissed the	petition for  special leave,
L109:     observing that	the petitioner	would be at liberty to file,
L110:     if so  advised, a  writ petition  under cite14†Article 32 of	 the
L111:     Constitution, challenging  the validity	 of cite11†section 14(1)(b) L112:     of  the	 Act.  The  petitioner	filed  this  writ  petition,
L113:     challenging the	 validity of  sections 14(1)(b) and 16(2) of
L114:     the Tamil  Nadu Rent Act as being arbitrary, discriminatory,
L115:     unreasonable   and    unconstitutional,	   and	  contending
L116:     consequently that  the eviction	 order	passed	against	 him
L117:     under cite11†section 14(1)(b) was  illegal. Several  of the  other
L118:     writ petitions were on this issue.
L119:          Dismissing the Writ Petitions, the Court,
L120:     ^
L121:          HELD: In  this case,  the Court  was not concerned with
L122:     clause (ii) of section 30 of the Tamil Nadu Act, a challenge
L123:     to the	validity of  which had been accepted by the Court in
L124:     Rattan Arya  and others	 v. State of Tamil Nadu and another,
L125:     [1986] 3  S.C.C. 385  and the cite13†section 30(ii) had been struck
L126:     down as violative of cite18†Article 14 of the Constitution. [10E]
L127:          Under cite11†section 14(1)(b) of	 the Act,  a landlord  could
L128:     make an application to the Rent Controller for possession of
L129:     a building,  and the  Rent Controller, if satisfied that the
L130:     building was  bona fide	 required by  the landlord  for	 the
L131:     immediate purpose  of demolition and such demolition was for
L132:     the purpose  of erecting  a new	 building on the site of the
L133:     building sought	 to be	demolished,  might  pass  an  order,
L134:     directing the  tenant to  deliver possession of the building
L135:     to  the	  landlord  before   a	specified  date.  Under	 the
L136:     provisions of the Act, the landlord has to commence the work
L137:     of demolition  not later  than	one  month  and	 the  entire
L138:     demolition work	 shall be  completed before  the  expiry  of
L139:     three months  from the	date he	 recovers possession  of the
L140:     entire building,  and in  the  case  of	 massive  buildings,
L141:     demolition can	take six  months or  even a  year, in  which
L142:     case, for  reasons to be recorded in writing, the controller
L143:     may allow  further period.  During that	 period a tenant was
L144:     bound  to   have  found	  some	other  suitable	 alternative
L145:     accommodation. In the case of a building vacated for repairs
L146:     under cite19†section 14(1)(a) of the Act, a tenant may arrange for
L147:     a temporary  accommodation for	a few months and then return
L148:     to the	building.  It  was  not	 practicable  and  would  be
L149:     anomalous to  expect a	landlord to take back a tenant for a
L150:     re-constructed building	 after a  long lapse  of time during
L151:     which the  tenant must	necessarily have  found	 some  other
L152:     suitable accommodation.	 This was  the true  purpose  behind
L153:     cite11†section 14(1)(b) read with cite20†section 14(2)(b) . In that view of
L154:     the matter,  the Court	was unable  to accept the submission
L155:     that in	 providing for the re-induction of the tenant in the
L156:     case of repairs and not in the
L157:     3
L158:     case of	 re-construction, there	 was  any  unreasonable	 and
L159:     irrational classification  without any basis. The absence of
L160:     the provision  for reinduction	does not ipso facto make the
L161:     provisions of the Act unfair or make the Act self-defeating.
L162:     [11G, 12A-C,D-G, 18E]
L163:          As regards	 the submission	 that in  most of  the	Rent
L164:     Acts, there  was a  provision for re-induction of the tenant
L165:     after re-construction,	but in	the case  of the  Tamil Nadu
L166:     Act, there  was no  such provision and this was violative of
L167:     cite18†Article 14 of  the	Constitution,  cite18†Article 14 of	 the
L168:     Constitution does  not authorise  the striking down of a law
L169:     of one	State on  the ground  that in contrast with a law of
L170:     another State  on the  same  subjects,	its  provisions	 are
L171:     discriminatory, and  nor does  it contemplate  a law  of the
L172:     centre or  of the  State dealing with similar subjects being
L173:     held to	 be unconstitutional  by a  process  of	 comparative
L174:     study of  the provisions  of two  enactments; the  source of
L175:     authority for  the two	statutes being different, cite18†Article 14 L176:     could have  no application,  as observed  by a	Constitution
L177:     Bench of  this Court  in the State of Madhya Pradesh v. G.C.
L178:     Mandawar, [1955] 1 S.C.R. 599. [12G, 13A-C]
L179:          cite10†The Act sought to	 restore the  balance in  the  scale
L180:     which is  otherwise weighted in favour of the stronger party
L181:     which had  larger bargaining  power. cite10†The Act balances	 the
L182:     scales and  regulates the  rights of  the parties fairly and
L183:     cannot be  construed only  in favour of the tenant. The main
L184:     provision of  cite11†section 14(1)(b) enables a landlord to make an
L185:     application to	the rent  controller for  possession of	 the
L186:     building  for	demolition  for	 re-construction  of  a	 new
L187:     building in  its place.	 If the Rent Controller is satisfied
L188:     with the  bona fide  need of  the landlord,  he may  pass an
L189:     order, directing  the tenant  to deliver  possession of	 the
L190:     building to the landlord before a specified date. There must
L191:     be a  bona fide	 need of  the landlord. It could not be said
L192:     that cite11†section 14(1)(b) was  arbitrary	and  that  excessive
L193:     powers had been given to the landlords. [16G-H, 17D-E]
L194:          The provisions  of the  Act imposed restrictions on the
L195:     landlord's right  under the  common law	 or the	 cite21†Transfer of Property Act to evict	the tenant  after the termination of
L196:     his tenancy.  The nature,  the form  and the  extent of	 the
L197:     restrictions to	 be imposed  on	 the  landlord's  right	 and
L198:     consequent extent  of the  protection to  be  given  to	 the
L199:     tenants is  a matter  of legislative policy and judgment. It
L200:     is inevitably  bound to	 vary  from  one  State	 to  another
L201:     according to  the local,  peculiar conditions  prevailing in
L202:     each State.  When the Courts are confronted with the problem
L203:     of a  legislation being	 violative of cite18†Article 14 , the Courts
L204:     are not	 concerned with the unwisdom of the legislation. "In
L205:     short, unconstitutionality and not unwisdom of a legislation
L206:     is the narrow area of judicial review"
L207:     4
L208:     observations of	 Krishna Iyer,	J. in  Murthy  Match  Works,
L209:     etc., v.  Asstt. Collector  of Central Excise, etc., [1974]3
L210:     S.C.R. 121, may be seen in this connection. [18F-H,19G]
L211:          The  purpose  underlying  cite11†section 14(1)(b) read	with
L212:     cite12†section 16(2) of the  Act is  to  remove  or  mitigate	 the
L213:     disinclination on the part of the landlords to expend moneys
L214:     for demolition	of the dilapidated buildings and reconstruct
L215:     new buildings  in their	 places. It  is a  matter  of  which
L216:     judicial notice	 can be	 taken that  the return from the old
L217:     and dilapidated	 buildings is  very meagre,  and in  several
L218:     cases, such  buildings prove  uneconomic for  the landlords,
L219:     resulting in  the deterioration	 of  the  condition  of	 the
L220:     buildings, and	there are  even collapses of such buildings.
L221:     It is for this purpose that the landlord is given by cite11†section 14(1)(b) , read	with cite22†section 16 , an incentive in the form of
L222:     exemption from	the provisions	of the Act for five years in
L223:     respect	 of   the  reconstructed   building.  The  principle
